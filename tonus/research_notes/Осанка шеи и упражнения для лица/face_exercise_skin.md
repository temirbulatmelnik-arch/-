# Facial exercise, tongue posture ("mewing") and other non-invasive methods for the lower face / jawline — evidence notes

> Method note for the report writer: in this session the page-fetch tool could not reach any domain (DNS failure for PubMed/PMC, JAMA, SciELO, Wikipedia, Europe PMC), so every finding below comes from search-engine summaries of abstracts, registry records, publisher pages and press coverage. That is not full-text reading. Numbers are given as reported. Where a figure could only be found in secondary or vendor sources, it is flagged. The headline numbers (Alam 2018, Kavanagh 2012, Hughes 2013, Myung 2025, Jung 2024, Kilmer 2016, REFINE) should be checked against the full texts before they appear in the app.
>
> Population caveat that applies throughout: almost all facial-exercise, device and massage trials enrolled **women**, mostly aged 30–65. No trial of facial exercise in young athletic men was found. A 2025 systematic review lists the "lack of male participants" as a key limitation ([Aesthet Surg J Open Forum 2025, PMC12658320](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12658320/)).

## 1. RCTs and systematic reviews of facial exercise, face yoga and device-based facial "training" (EMS/NMES, resistance mouthpieces, massage tools)

### Takeaway
The evidence is low-quality and almost entirely in women. The best-known study (Alam 2018, JAMA Dermatology) was an uncontrolled pilot: 27 women enrolled, 16 completed. After 20 weeks, blinded raters scored the cheeks as fuller and judged the women about 2.7 years younger. Every systematic review (2014, 2021, 2024, 2025) concludes that the evidence cannot establish efficacy. Device studies (NMES, the Pao mouthpiece) show increases in muscle thickness on ultrasound, but they are single, often industry-linked trials, and none studied men. No randomized trial of voluntary facial exercise with a no-treatment control and published results was found up to 2026.

### Cited Findings
**Landmark pilot: Alam et al. 2018, JAMA Dermatology**
- Design: single-group, uncontrolled pre/post pilot in women aged 40–65. Participants had two 90-minute in-person training sessions with a facial-exercise instructor, then did 30 min of exercise daily for weeks 1–8 and 30 min every other day for weeks 9–20 — [Northwestern Now press release](https://news.northwestern.edu/stories/2018/january/facial-exercises-help-middle-aged-women-appear-more-youthful); [MDedge summary](https://mdedge.com/content/facial-exercises-improved-appearance-small-study-middle-aged-women)
- 27 recruited, 16 completed the full 20 weeks. The program had 32 exercises, each held about 1 minute, developed by co-author Gary Sikorski (Happy Face Yoga). Exercises were not tested individually, so "it's not clear which exercises were the most beneficial" (Alam) — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2018/01/180103160142.htm); [Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/317443)
- Results (blinded dermatologist ratings of standardized photos, validated scale): upper-cheek fullness improved from 1.8 to 1.1 and lower-cheek fullness from 1.6 to 0.9 (both P = .003). Estimated age fell from about 51 to 49 years (P = .002), or precisely 50.8 at baseline, 49.6 at week 8 and 48.1 at week 20 — [Practical Dermatology](https://practicaldermatology.com/news/daily-facial-exercises-help-women-to-look-three-years-younger-in-20-weeks/2457938); [MDedge](https://mdedge.com/content/facial-exercises-improved-appearance-small-study-middle-aged-women); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2018/01/180103160142.htm)
- Authors' proposed mechanism: "exercise-actuated hypertrophy of cheek and other muscles" (a hypothesis; muscle was not measured). Stated limitations: small sample, women only, many dropouts, no control group, self-selected motivated participants. Their conclusion was that the regimen "may modestly improve" appearance in "selected middle-aged women" — [MDedge](https://mdedge.com/content/facial-exercises-improved-appearance-small-study-middle-aged-women)
- Citation: Alam M, Walter AJ, Geisler A, et al. Association of facial exercise with the appearance of aging. JAMA Dermatol. 2018;154(3):365–367. DOI 10.1001/jamadermatol.2017.5142 — [JAMA Dermatology (proxy link from search)](https://jamanetwork-com.libproxy.ajou.ac.kr/journals/jamadermatology/fullarticle/2666801)

**Systematic reviews**
- The review the brief calls "De Vos 2014, Clin Exp Dermatol" is actually **Van Borsel J, De Vos M-C, Bastiaansen K, Welvaert J, Lambert J. The effectiveness of facial exercises for facial rejuvenation: a systematic review. Aesthet Surg J. 2014;34(1):22–27** (e-published 2013). It found 9 reports. All 9 reported positive outcomes, but none used a control group or randomization. Designs were single-case reports, small case series or single-group pre/post. Conclusion: "evidence to date is insufficient to determine whether facial exercises are effective" — [search summary + Virginia library record](https://sources.mandala.library.virginia.edu/node/30601); [ResearchGate PDF](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259271554_The_Effectiveness_of_Facial_Exercises_for_Facial_Rejuvenation_A_Systematic_Review); [MedEsthetics news: "Benefits… Inconclusive"](https://www.medestheticsmag.com/news/article/21145684/benefits-of-facial-exercises-for-aesthetic-rejuvenation-inconclusive). A news summary says all 9 studies were done in South America — [MedEsthetics](https://www.medestheticsmag.com/news/article/21145684/benefits-of-facial-exercises-for-aesthetic-rejuvenation-inconclusive)
- 2025 PROSPERO-registered PRISMA review: **Efficacy of Conservative Techniques for Mechanical Facial Rejuvenation: A Systematic Review**, Aesthet Surg J Open Forum 2025, DOI 10.1093/asjof/ojaf144. 12 studies, 321 women aged 30–70: facial exercises (n=6), myofunctional therapy (n=4), manual massage (n=2). Improvements were "confined to localized regions, such as the cheeks, jawline, and periorbital areas." No adverse events were recorded. Limitations: heterogeneity, small samples, no men. Conclusion: "current evidence remains insufficient to establish the efficacy… with confidence" — [PMC12658320](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12658320/); [OUP](https://academic.oup.com/asjopenforum/advance-article/8313738)
- **Facial exercises for rejuvenation: A systematic review**, Revista CEFAC (Brazil). 608 records screened, 7 included, all case series or case-control; no RCTs. All reported improvement in wrinkles and expression lines. Risk of bias was low in the case series and moderate in the 2 case-control studies. The authors call for RCTs — [SciELO](https://www.scielo.br/j/rcefac/a/QJYZ8mZXHD7krKHxGzhsJGz/?lang=en); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/386069895_Facial_exercises_for_rejuvenation_A_systematic_review). *Date conflict*: one search summary said November 2024, another said 2021. The ResearchGate ID suggests late 2024.
- **Myofunctional Speech Therapy for Facial Rejuvenation and Orofacial Function Improvement: A Systematic Review** (search Jan–Mar 2023): 21 studies. In most, participants learned exercises in weekly sessions and practised daily at home — [PMC11204933](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11204933/)
- 2021 Korean review (Physical Therapy Korea): 11 studies, of which 4 were facial exercise for rejuvenation and 7 were facial-muscle strengthening — [KoreaScience](https://www.koreascience.or.kr/article/JAKO202107547753341.page)
- Another review (seven studies) reports that facial acupuncture and facial exercises "have the potential to improve skin laxity, wrinkle length, muscle thickness, and pigmentary changes"; details not verified — [Walden ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.waldenu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1744&context=facpubs)

**Trials 2012–2026 (devices, face yoga, massage)**
- **Kavanagh et al. 2012, J Cosmet Dermatol: NMES facial device RCT** ("Slendertone Face"; partially blinded, no sham). 108 healthy women, mean age 43.7, using the device 20 min/day, 5 days/wk for 12 weeks, vs a no-intervention control. On assessor-blinded ultrasound, zygomaticus major thickness rose **18.6% vs baseline** in the NMES group with no change in controls. Between-group difference P = 0.05 at 6 weeks and P = 0.0001 at 12 weeks. Industry-linked — [Wiley, DOI 10.1111/jocd.12007](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jocd.12007); [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT01030380](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01030380); [University of Galway record](https://research.universityofgalway.ie/en/publications/use-of-a-neuromuscular-electrical-stimulation-device-for-facial-m/)
- **Hwang UJ, Kwon OY, Jung SH, Ahn SH, Gwak GT. Effect of a Facial Muscle Exercise Device on Facial Rejuvenation. Aesthet Surg J. 2018;38(5):463–476.** 50 women used the "Pao" resistance mouthpiece for 30 seconds twice a day for 8 weeks. On ultrasound, cross-sectional area of **zygomaticus major and digastric** increased. On laser scanning, midface and **jawline surface distances decreased** and lower-face surface area and volume decreased significantly. A commentary warns the results cannot be extrapolated to other commercial exercisers. The abstract does not mention a control group — [PMC5888959](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5888959); [Explore Plastic Surgery commentary](https://www.exploreplasticsurgery.com/?p=26723)
- **Ahn et al. 2025, J Cosmet Dermatol 24(6):e70236. Facial roller vs gua sha RCT.** 34 women aged 20–50, randomized 17/17, 10 min, 5×/week for 8 weeks. Outcomes included jawline surface distance, muscle tone (MyotonPRO) and skin elasticity. Gua sha mainly reduced muscle-tone parameters, the roller mainly improved skin elasticity, and both changed contour. It compared two active arms with no sham and no untreated control. Secondary sources put contour changes at about 2–3 mm (figures not verified) — [PMC12121324](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12121324/); [Wiley DOI 10.1111/jocd.70236](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jocd.70236); [Dermatology Times](https://www.dermatologytimes.com/at-home-facial-massage-methods-clinically-proven-to-improve-skin-elasticity-tone-and-contour); [open-access PDF, PolyU](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/handle/10397/113936)
- **Intensive face yoga, Medicina 2025.** Pre-experimental single-group trial in middle-aged women. Tonus and stiffness of frontalis, corrugator supercilii and orbicularis oculi changed significantly (upper face only). The authors call for RCTs — [PMC12112979](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12112979/); [Myoton](https://www.myoton.com/publication/effect-of-intensive-face-yoga-on-facial-muscles-tonus-stiffness-and-elasticity-in-middle-aged-women-a-pre-experimental-clinical-trial/)
- Ongoing: NCT07285577 compares an 8-week face-yoga/facial-muscle-exercise program with fractional microneedling RF in 40 women aged ≥30. Primary outcome: ≥25% improvement on a global aesthetic scale and satisfaction at day 90. No results yet — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07285577)
- 2025 Japanese EMS study (14 women, 10 min/day, 12 weeks, CT measurement) reported muscle-thickness increases at 4 and 12 weeks. Small, uncontrolled. Exact record not verified (the search returned [PubMed 39749931](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39749931/) and [J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/koshohin/49/2/49_490201/_article/-char/ja)).
- **Jaw exerciser case report (Cureus 2024):** 2 cases, about 3 months. No measurable change in double chin, jawline or neck tightness. The authors write that "the mastication muscles targeted by these exercises do not directly affect submental fat or skin elasticity" — [PMC11681191](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11681191/)

### Inferences
- Alam's effect is real but modest and was rated subjectively: about −2.7 years of perceived age and roughly a 0.7-point gain in cheek fullness on a 0–3 scale. It comes from 16 self-selected completers with no control group. Regression to the mean, photo-condition drift and weight change cannot be excluded.
- Alam's significant changes were in **cheek fullness** (midface volume), not jawline, jowls or the submental area. For a lean young man whose goal is a *tighter* lower face, "fuller cheeks" is not the target outcome. The cheek result says nothing directly about a sharper mandibular border.
- The only data pointing to a "tighter jawline" come from a device study (Hwang 2018: reduced jawline surface distance, digastric CSA up) and a massage study (Ahn 2025: contour changes of about 2–3 mm). Both are small, in women, uncontrolled or without a sham. Short-term contour changes after massage may reflect fluid shifts or tone rather than structure. This is speculation; the trials did not measure fluid.
- Dosage benchmarks from the literature for a progressive app: Alam used 30 min/day for 8 weeks, then 30 min on alternate days to week 20. Kavanagh's NMES was 20 min/day, 5 days/wk, 12 weeks. Hwang's device was 30 s twice a day for 8 weeks. Ahn's massage was 10 min, 5×/wk, 8 weeks. Measurable changes appeared at 6–12 weeks in these studies.
- Honest framing for the app: "low-certainty evidence, mostly from women over 40; expect subtle changes at best."

### Gaps
- No full texts could be fetched (tool failure), so exact SDs, effect sizes, adherence rates and lists of individual exercises were not extracted (for example, the full list of Alam's 32 exercises).
- No RCT of voluntary facial exercise with an untreated control and published results was found up to 2026. No trial in men, and none in young lean adults.
- The study the brief calls "Hwang 2022" could not be identified. The closest match is Hwang et al. 2018 (ASJ, Pao device).
- No study measured submental fat, the cervicomental angle or the mandibular border as primary outcomes of facial exercise.
- A December 2024 RCT comparing topical bakuchiol with facial exercises for facial aging was mentioned in a search summary, but its record and results could not be located, so it is not cited.
- A 2024 non-randomized study of high-intensity facial electrical stimulation plus RF (37 participants, 24 weeks; larger muscle changes reported, authors call for RCTs) was mentioned in a search summary without an identifiable source record, so it is not cited.

## 2. Can facial muscles hypertrophy, and does extra muscle volume "lift" the lower face? Mechanisms of lower-face aging

### Takeaway
Facial and masticatory muscles can thicken when loaded. This has been shown for zygomaticus major with NMES and the Pao device, for digastric with the Pao device, and for masseter with isometric or chewing training in older adults. Muscle thickness was not linked to a validated jawline outcome, and 6 months of hard gum chewing in healthy adults did *not* change masseter thickness or mandibular shape. The lower-face "oval" depends mostly on bone, fat compartments, retaining ligaments, skin and body fat. Mimetic muscle is a thin layer that plays a smaller part.

### Cited Findings
- Zygomaticus major thickness +18.6% after 12 weeks of NMES (women, mean age 43.7) — [Kavanagh 2012, J Cosmet Dermatol](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jocd.12007)
- Zygomaticus major and digastric cross-sectional area increased after 8 weeks of resistance-mouthpiece exercise — [Hwang 2018, ASJ, PMC5888959](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5888959)
- Older adults with missing teeth, 4-week isometric clenching RCT: significant improvements in maximum occlusal force and in masseter thickness both contracted and at rest — [PMC8012634](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8012634/)
- Community-dwelling older Koreans (n=40, 6 weeks): chewing exercise alone and chewing plus NMES both increased bite force and masseter thickness, with the combination better — [Park 2020, IJERPH, PMC7369815](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7369815/); [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/17/13/4902)
- A double-blind placebo-controlled RCT of NMES on masseter thickness in people ≥65 exists; only the title was seen — [PMC7312302](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7312302/)
- **Healthy adults, gum-chewing RCT (Jung et al. 2024, J Oral Rehabil 51(12):2529–2536, DOI 10.1111/joor.13830).** n=58, gum chewed 3×/day for 6 months vs no training. Maximum occlusal force rose by 3 months (p<.001 vs control) and occlusal contact area increased (p=.020). **Masseter thickness and mandibular shape did not change significantly** — [CiteDrive record](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/effects-of-gum-chewing-training-on-occlusal-force-masseter-muscle-thickness-and-mandibular-shape-a-randomised-controlled-clinical-trial/); [Yonsei repository](https://ir.ymlib.yonsei.ac.kr/handle/22282913/200840)
- MRI shows zygomaticus major gets shorter and *wider/thicker* during contraction. Muscle thickness readings therefore depend on facial expression, which matters for how studies measure it — [Aesthetic Plast Surg 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00266-024-03876-8)
- **Lower-face aging mechanisms:**
  - Bone changes are millimetres in size but visible. Resorption of the inferior mandible, especially with tooth loss, can increase the mandibular angle and reduce chin height — ["The Facial Aging Process From the 'Inside Out'", Aesthet Surg J, sjaa339](https://www.ovid.com/journals/aesj/pdf/10.1093/asj/sjaa339~the-facial-aging-process-from-the-inside-out)
  - Mandibular contour mechanisms: atrophy of the superior and inferior mandibular fat compartments, dehiscence of the mandibular septum so that these fat compartments slide down into the neck, bone resorption and skin laxity. The inferior mandibular border is the platform where lower-face muscles, ligaments and the mandibular septum insert — [Thieme (Facial Plast Surg)](https://thieme-connect.de/products/ejournals/html/10.1055/s-0039-1683857)
  - A systematic review of 93 articles concluded that aging probably *begins* with loss of maxillary and mandibular bone volume, followed by a cascade through interconnected tissue layers — [Univ. Salamanca record](https://produccioncientifica.usal.es/documentos/615bc1acd9bf6a26275cdd85?lang=eu)
  - Jowls can form from distension of the lower masseteric ligaments, enlargement of the lower premasseteric space and buccal fat prolapse (educational, not peer-reviewed) — [3D4Medical](https://3d4medical.com/?p=4679)
  - A 2025 hypothesis paper (Yi & Wan, "The Aging Process of Facial Muscles") argues that progressive muscle *contracture* drives remodelling and invokes Wolff's law: weak muscles exert less tension on bony attachments, which favours osteoclast activity, with resorption of the mandibular angle, anterior maxilla and orbital rims. It also notes the orbicularis retaining ligament weakens with age. This is a mechanistic hypothesis and is not established — [PMC12715075](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12715075/)
- The hyoid descends with age in both sexes. Higher BMI is associated with a more anterior hyoid, about 2 mm per 5 BMI points (CT, n=282) — [Plast Reconstr Surg Glob Open 2024, DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/2ff23666d17146a3a0f629ac336eafd3)

### Inferences
- "Muscle hypertrophy lifts skin" is plausible for the *midface*, where zygomaticus and levator muscles lie under the malar fat. It is less plausible for the *jawline*. Jowls and the submental bulge come from fat compartments, ligament laxity and skin rather than weak mimetic muscle. Bigger masseters widen the lower face (bigonial width) rather than sharpening the mandibular border, and in healthy young adults even 6 months of chewing did not thicken them (Jung 2024).
- The digastric finding (Hwang 2018) is the only exercise-related evidence touching a submental muscle. It suggests suprahyoid loading can increase muscle size. Whether that improves submental contour in a lean young man is unknown.
- For a 190 cm, 90 kg athletic man with an intact skeleton and dentition, the age-related mechanisms above (bone resorption, septal dehiscence, fat descent) are largely not yet active. Gains from "anti-aging" facial exercise would therefore be expected to be smaller than in the 40–65-year-old women studied. This is an extrapolation.

### Gaps
- No longitudinal data on age-related change in mimetic-muscle thickness or fibre type were found.
- No study links measured facial-muscle hypertrophy to an objective jawline, jowl or cervicomental outcome.

## 3. Platysma exercises, neck bands and jawline definition

### Takeaway
There is no controlled evidence that platysma exercise improves the jawline or neck. The evidence points the other way. Vertical platysmal bands form when the muscle contracts, and *relaxing* the platysma with botulinum toxin is FDA-approved (October 2024) and improves both neck bands and perceived jawline definition. Deliberate, repeated maximal platysma contraction ("neck curl"/grimace drills) is therefore unlikely to help and could plausibly accentuate bands in susceptible people. That risk is inferred, not proven.

### Cited Findings
- Ultrasound in 24 adults plus dissection of 10 cadavers (Yonsei): platysma thickness is uniform at rest, but its medial edges thicken and bulge on contraction. This supports muscle activity as the driver of bands — [Yonsei repository](https://ir.ymlib.yonsei.ac.kr/handle/22282913/197744)
- **FDA approval, October 2024:** Botox Cosmetic (onabotulinumtoxinA) for temporary improvement of moderate-to-severe vertical platysma bands "connecting the jaw and neck" in adults. It is the first neurotoxin with this indication. Doses are 26, 31 or 36 U by severity, injected along the jawline and bands. In phase 3, 65% and 62% of treated patients were "Very Satisfied/Satisfied" with neck and jawline definition at day 14, vs 12% on placebo — [Practical Dermatology](https://practicaldermatology.com/news/botox-approved-platysma-bands/2468436/); [Aesthetics Journal](https://aestheticsjournal.com/news/fda-approves-botox-for-vertical-platysma-bands/); [American Spa](https://www.americanspa.com/medical-spa/fda-approves-botox-cosmetic-moderate-severe-neck-bands); [Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/allergans-botox-cosmetic-gets-fda-approval-jaw-neck-bands)
- Second phase 3 trial: 426 adults, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, single treatment. Primary endpoint met (p<0.0001) on investigator and subject assessments. No new safety signals (manufacturer press release) — [AbbVie, 18 Sep 2023](https://news.abbvie.com/2023-09-18-Allergan-Aesthetics-Announces-Positive-Topline-Results-from-Second-Phase-3-Study-of-OnabotulinumtoxinA-BOTOX-R-Cosmetic-for-the-Treatment-of-Platysma-Prominence)
- Coverage of the label states that a contracting platysma "can create neck bands and can also create a less defined jawline" (industry-sourced) — [Practical Dermatology](https://practicaldermatology.com/news/botox-approved-platysma-bands/2468436/)
- Botulinum toxin for the aging neck was described in 1999 (Plast Reconstr Surg), with a 4-grade band classification — [Healthline summary](https://healthline.com/health/platysmal-bands); [QxMD record](https://www.qxmd.com/r/9950556)
- Aesthetic-clinic sources (not studies) advise people with visible bands against heavy neck work and grimacing because it can make bands more prominent — [Artemis clinic blog](https://artemis.co/blog/platysmal-bands)
- In the EMG reference study of 12 healthy adults, platysma activity was recorded during "wrinkling the neck skin". This confirms that the grimace/neck-tensing movement in many "jawline" routines is a platysma contraction — [PMC7647650](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7647650)

### Inferences
- Exercises that maximally depress the lip corners and tense the neck skin (exaggerated "O-E" vowels with grimace, "neck curl with chin tuck and teeth bared", "jaw-drop grimace") load the platysma and depressor anguli oris. The best-supported medical treatment for platysmal bands works by weakening that muscle, so these drills should not be marketed as "jawline definers". If included at all, they belong at low volume. Neck-band appearance could be added to the app's "stop" criteria.
- The suprahyoid group, not the platysma, is the more rational target for "under-chin" training (CTAR/chin-tuck, tongue-to-palate; see Section 4). There is EMG evidence that these exercises activate it, though no evidence yet that they change contour.

### Gaps
- No trial of platysma *strengthening* for neck or jawline appearance was found, in either direction. The "may worsen bands" concern comes from mechanism and clinician opinion only.
- The 10-year facial-palsy observation (76% bands on the non-paralysed side) appeared only in a Healthline secondary source and could not be verified.

## 4. Tongue posture ("mewing"/orthotropics), suprahyoid tone, head posture and the cervicomental angle

### Takeaway
No controlled study shows that mewing changes facial bone in adults. The American Association of Orthodontists stated in January 2024 that there is "no scientific evidence" for jawline reshaping and that risks outweigh benefits. What is supported is narrower: pressing the tongue to the palate measurably activates suprahyoid (submental) muscles on EMG, and chin-tuck-against-resistance (CTAR) is the most suprahyoid-specific exercise. A low or forward hyoid is associated with an obtuse (less defined) cervicomental angle in surgical literature. No study was found that directly measures how habitual head posture changes the cervicomental angle, although head position plainly changes how the submental area looks in photographs.

### Cited Findings
**Mewing / orthotropics**
- **AAO warning, 22 January 2024.** AAO President Dr Myron Guymon: "there is no scientific evidence supporting claims of reshaping the jawline, and the potential risks outweigh any unproven benefits." Listed risks: loosened teeth, bite changes, tooth wear and effects on speech — [Becker's Dental](https://beckersdental.com/dentists/42806-orthodontic-org-warns-against-mewing-tiktok-trend.html); [Dental Products Report](https://dentalproductsreport.com/view/aao-warns-against-unregulated-practice-of-mewing-); [AAO "Risks of Mewing" PDF (Jan 2025)](https://aaoinfo.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Risks-of-Mewing.pdf); [AAO consumer page](https://aaoinfo.org/whats-trending/is-mewing-bad-for-you/)
- Wikipedia describes mewing as an unproven form of oral-posture training. A 2019 J Oral Maxillofac Surg paper reportedly found no research supporting it as an alternative to orthodontics or jaw surgery — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mewing_(orthotropics)); [Discover Magazine](https://www.discovermagazine.com/mewing-may-be-trendy-but-it-has-no-scientific-basis-46023)
- Clinic sources (low quality but consistent): no RCT, cohort or standardized imaging series has tested whether mewing changes jaw shape. Tongue posture matters mainly during childhood growth. The midpalatal suture typically fuses in early adulthood. "Hard mewing" (forceful pressure) can strain the jaw muscles and TMJ and push the front teeth forward — [Acıbadem](https://acibademinternational.com/blog/mewing-what-orthodontists-say-about-tongue-posture-jawlines-and-the-evidence/); [Las Vegas Dentistry](https://lasvegasdentistry.com/articles/mewing/); [Centre for Surgery](https://centreforsurgery.com/mewing/)
- Resting the tongue on the palate with lips sealed and nasal breathing is a legitimate component of orofacial myofunctional therapy. Meta-analyses (reported second-hand) show improvement in snoring and daytime sleepiness, with more equivocal effects on the apnoea index in RCTs — [Las Vegas Dentistry (secondary)](https://lasvegasdentistry.com/articles/mewing/)

**Tongue-to-palate pressure and suprahyoid EMG**
- Japanese study (15 younger, 12 older adults): suprahyoid EMG during the head-raising (Shaker) exercise equalled the EMG produced by 65% of maximum tongue pressure in younger and 50% in older adults. Conclusion: palatal pressure at ≥50% of maximum effectively activates suprahyoids — [PMC3871479 (attribution as returned by search; verify)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3871479)
- Brazilian study (37 adults, IOPI): suprahyoid EMG correlated moderately with tongue pressure only in the **posterior** palate region — [Rev CEFAC](https://new.scielo.br/j/rcefac/a/NMRwcvpdGzdhvfS9KjfRLpb/?lang=en)
- 22 women compared eight isometric tongue tasks (palatal press, apex press, suction) for suprahyoid EMG normalization; results not seen — [Audiol Commun Res (SciELO)](https://scielo.br/j/acr/a/HxnGnrJhwJhqdZG8trdz88d/?lang=en)
- Case series of 5 dysphagia patients doing tongue-palate resistance at home (10-s holds, 30 reps/session): suprahyoid EMG 8.56 → 15.26 → 17.44 µV over 2 weeks — [ASEAN J Rehabil Med](https://he01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/aseanjrm/article/view/249369)

**Chin-tuck against resistance (CTAR) vs Shaker (head lift)**
- Sze et al. 2016 (Dysphagia; 39 healthy adults; sEMG): CTAR targets suprahyoids more specifically than Shaker, with similar or greater suprahyoid fatigue and less sternocleidomastoid activity — [Springer Medicine](https://www.springermedicine.com/chin-tuck-against-resistance-ctar-new-method-for-enhancing-supra/21129036)
- Kılınç et al. 2019 (42 healthy adults): CTAR gave significantly higher normalized suprahyoid activation than Shaker. TheraBand chin tuck was similar to Shaker — [Hacettepe](https://openaccess.hacettepe.edu.tr/handle/11655/24680)
- 8-week training study (36 healthy volunteers): CTAR and TheraBand chin tuck increased maximal suprahyoid activation and tongue pressure; Shaker did not — [Dysphagia 2019, DOI 10.1007/s00455-019-10079-w](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s00455-019-10079-w)
- Systematic review (stroke, 9 studies, 548 patients): CTAR superior to Shaker for swallowing safety — [Front Neurol 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurology/articles/10.3389/fneur.2022.1109140/pdf)

**Head posture, hyoid and cervicomental angle**
- Submental-cervical angle averaged 118.0° in "ideal" subjects vs 158.3° in pre-operative patients (aesthetic contour analysis, J Oral Maxillofac Surg 1994) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0278239194904820)
- In more than a quarter of patients with normal jaw relationships, the obtuse cervicomental angle is attributed to a **low hyoid** rather than excess soft tissue (ASAPS 2022 abstract, Minelli et al.; digastric plication) — [ASAPS](https://asaps.confex.com/asaps/2022/webprogrampreliminary/Paper10001.html)
- Cadaver neck-lift study (12 cadavers): a low hyoid was the only pre-operative factor that predicted meaningful angle reduction — [Arch Facial Plast Surg 2002, PSU record](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/a-comparative-study-of-surgical-techniques-on-the-cervicomental-a/)
- Forward head posture (FHP) vs normal posture (n=54, 2026): submental muscle activation during effortful swallowing differed between groups. Correct head and neck posture was associated with greater submental activation — [Dysphagia 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00455-026-10987-8)
- In adult women, a steeper mandibular plane angle was associated with a more anterior head/neck position (weak correlations) — [Appl Sci 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/5/2391)
- Smartphone apps can validly and reliably measure the craniovertebral angle in standing, which is relevant to an in-app posture check — [PMC7559098](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7559098/)

### Inferences
- An honest version of "mewing" for the app: tongue resting on the palate, lips closed, nasal breathing, teeth lightly apart. Present it as a posture/myofunctional habit and light suprahyoid activation, not as bone remodelling. Avoid "hard mewing" and constant force.
- The submental muscles (anterior digastric, mylohyoid, geniohyoid, plus genioglossus) can be trained with graded tongue-to-palate isometrics (for example 50–80% effort, 10-s holds) and CTAR/chin-tuck. That is EMG-supported. Whether stronger suprahyoids raise the hyoid at rest or visibly sharpen the cervicomental angle in a lean young man is *untested*.
- Head posture probably has the largest *immediate* visual effect on the "double chin". Chin-forward/flexed posture bunches submental soft tissue, while a neutral head with the chin slightly projected stretches it. This is geometric and photographic reasoning, not a measured study. Before/after photos in the app must standardize head position (for example Frankfort horizontal), camera height and focal length, or "progress" will be artefact.

### Gaps
- No study was found that directly correlates craniovertebral angle or forward head posture with the cervicomental angle, or shows that posture training changes the resting cervicomental angle.
- No trial of mewing or tongue-posture training with a facial-contour outcome in adults exists.
- No evidence was found that suprahyoid training changes resting hyoid height.

## 5. Skin firmness/tone: high-evidence measures, weak at-home methods, and medical options (reference only)

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence for skin quality is **daily broad-spectrum sunscreen** (RCT: 24% less skin aging over 4.5 years) and **topical tretinoin** (multiple RCTs for photoaging). Avoiding smoking and heavy drinking and getting enough sleep have consistent observational support. Oral collagen is contested: industry-funded trials are positive, while independent and high-quality trials are null. Massage, gua sha and home microcurrent/RF are weak or vendor-driven. For submental fat specifically, deoxycholic acid (Kybella) and cryolipolysis have controlled or pivotal-trial evidence.

### Cited Findings
**Sun protection**
- **Hughes et al. 2013, Ann Intern Med (RCT):** 903 adults under 55 in Nambour, Australia, randomized to daily vs discretionary sunscreen (± β-carotene). Daily users had **24% less skin aging** over 4.5 years (relative odds 0.76, 95% CI 0.59–0.98), measured by skin microtopography on the back of the hand. β-carotene had no overall effect. Missing data and modest power were limitations — [QxMD/Annals abstract](https://read.qxmd.com/read/23732711/sunscreen-and-prevention-of-skin-aging-a-randomized-trial); [Asian Scientist](https://www.asianscientist.com/2013/06/health/study-shows-daily-sunscreen-prevents-skin-aging-2013/); [CosmeticsDesign](https://www.cosmeticsdesign.com/Article/2013/06/05/Research-suggests-daily-sunscreen-use-can-reduce-signs-of-aging-by-24-percent)

**Topical retinoids and vitamin C**
- 2022 systematic review of RCTs of topical tretinoin for photoaging: 7 RCTs (from 180 screened) consistently showed improvement in wrinkling, mottled hyperpigmentation, sallowness and lentigines, as early as 1 month. Formulations and outcomes were heterogeneous — [PubMed 35620028](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35620028/); [Int J Women's Dermatol, DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/2459fefcec8b43368e444eba39e52a13)
- 2024 systematic review (25 studies) of tretinoin vs other topicals: comparators (mostly other retinoids) were better tolerated. Comparator efficacy was greater in 7 studies, equivalent in 13 and lower in 3 — [PubMed 39348007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39348007/)
- 2025 narrative review: RCTs support tretinoin for photoaging, but better-powered standardized trials are still needed — [PMC12653878](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12653878/)
- Topical vitamin C: a 3-month double-blind vehicle-controlled RCT of 10% ascorbic acid improved fine wrinkling (cited in a trade review). The evidence is weaker than for tretinoin — [MDedge review PDF (one of two MDedge PDFs the search returned; verify which one holds this)](https://cdn.mdedge.com/files/s3fs-public/Document/September-2017/019070479.pdf)

**Oral collagen**
- **Myung & Park 2025, Am J Med (DOI 10.1016/j.amjmed.2025.04.034):** 23 RCTs, 1,474 participants. Pooled results were positive for hydration, elasticity and wrinkles. However, **studies without industry funding showed no effect**, and **high-quality studies showed no significant effect in any outcome**. Industry groups (GROW, CSA, Biocell) disputed the paper — [NutraIngredients](https://nutraingredients.com/Article/2025/08/26/industry-reacts-to-meta-analysis-concluding-collagen-supplements-show-no-proven-benefit-for-skin-aging); [CosmeticsDesign](https://www.cosmeticsdesign.com/Article/2025/08/26/industry-reacts-to-meta-analysis-concluding-collagen-supplements-show-no-proven-benefit-for-skin-aging)
- Contrasting 2023 Nutrients meta-analysis: 26 RCTs, 1,721 participants, with significant improvements in hydration and elasticity vs placebo — [as reported in NutraIngredients](https://nutraingredients.com/Article/2025/08/26/industry-reacts-to-meta-analysis-concluding-collagen-supplements-show-no-proven-benefit-for-skin-aging)

**Smoking, alcohol and sleep**
- Guyuron et al. 2009, Plast Reconstr Surg, 186 identical-twin pairs: longer smoking duration and more sun exposure (outdoor activity, no sunscreen) went with an older perceived age, and the sun effect grew with age — [Gwern-hosted PDF](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/longevity/2009-guyuron.pdf); [Stafforini summary](https://stafforini.com/works/guyuron-2009-factors-contributing-facial/)
- Guyuron 2013, 79 twin pairs: a difference of ≥5 years in smoking duration produced visibly identifiable facial aging — [WTVR](https://www.wtvr.com/2013/10/31/twins-study-shows-smoking-ages-your-face-faster); [Consultant360](https://www.consultant360.com/story/smokers-skin-may-age-faster)
- Goodman et al. 2019, J Clin Aesthet Dermatol (cross-sectional survey, 3,267 women aged 18–75, self-rated photonumeric scales, Allergan-funded). Smoking was associated with forehead, crow's-feet and glabellar lines, under-eye puffiness, tear troughs, nasolabial folds, oral commissures, perioral lines and less lip fullness (p≤0.025). **≥8 drinks/week** was associated with upper-face lines, **under-eye puffiness**, oral commissures, midface volume loss and visible vessels — [PMC6715121](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6715121/); [AusDoc](https://www.ausdoc.com.au/news/smoking-and-heavy-drinking-increase-facial-ageing-study)
- Sundelin et al. 2013, Sleep: photos of 10 people after 31 h awake were rated as having more hanging eyelids, red and swollen eyes, dark circles, paler skin, more fine lines and **droopier mouth corners** — [PMC3738045](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3738045/); [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2013/08/130830161323.htm)
- Sleep restriction lowers others' ratings of attractiveness and health (Sundelin 2017, R Soc Open Sci) — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/98e2dc7b6a6d46e39e01920966529f86). Objective facial measures under sleep deprivation gave mixed results (Holding 2019) — [St Andrews repository](https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/publications/the-effect-of-sleep-deprivation-on-objective-and-subjective-measu/)

**Massage and home devices (weak)**
- Gua sha vs roller RCT (Ahn 2025), two active arms, no sham; see Section 1 — [PMC12121324](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12121324/)
- Home NMES device RCT (Kavanagh 2012): muscle thickness only, industry-linked — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jocd.12007)
- A 2024 Clin Cosmet Investig Dermatol paper says the effectiveness and safety of home RF, microcurrent and LED devices remain controversial (abstract only) — [PMC10929553](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10929553)
- A home-RF split-face RCT (Shu 2022, Dermatol Ther, 33 women, 12 weeks) and a 2025 RF systematic review (15 studies, 1,230 participants, firmness improved in 52.9–100%, mostly *clinic-grade* RF) were found only via a device-maker blog and are unverified — [EvenSkyn blog (vendor)](https://www.evenskyn.com/nb/blogs/skin-beautyarticles/at-home-skin-tightening-2026-clinical-evidence-guide)

**Medical options (reference only; for the app's "what else works" screen)**
- **Deoxycholic acid (ATX-101/Kybella), submental fat.** In the REFINE-1 and REFINE-2 phase 3 RCTs, a clinician-rated clinically meaningful response occurred in 70.3% vs 18.7% (REFINE-1) and 66.9% vs 22.4% (REFINE-2), from top-line conference data — [Dermatology Times](https://www.dermatologytimes.com/view/atx-101-studies-yield-favorable-findings-reducing-submental-fat). One registry record reports a composite one-grade response of 66.5% vs 22.2% (n=258 per arm) — [NCT01546142](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01546142). The pooled N was 1,022 at 2 mg/cm² — [ASPS abstract](https://asps.confex.com/asps/2014am/recordingredirect.cgi/id/3414). Response was maintained to 3 years — [MedEsthetics](https://www.medestheticsmag.com/news/news/21295705/kybella-submental-contour-results-are-maintained-for-three-years)
- **Submental cryolipolysis (Kilmer 2016, Lasers Surg Med; pivotal, uncontrolled).** 60 subjects, −10 °C for 60 min, optional second session. Mean ultrasound fat-layer reduction **2.0 mm**. 83% satisfied, 77% saw visible reduction, and blinded photo identification was 91% correct. No serious adverse events. Led to FDA clearance — [PubMed 26607045](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26607045/); [HealthDay](https://consumer.healthday.com/cosmetic-surgery-29/cosmetic-surgery-news-157/submental-fat-reduced-with-cryolipolysis-vacuum-applicator-707210.html)
- **Botulinum toxin for platysma bands:** FDA-approved October 2024 (Section 3).
- **HIFU** (Ulthera) for submental/neck lift: an open-label protocol in Chinese patients exists — [NCT03351335 protocol](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/35/NCT03351335/Prot_000.pdf). A 2026 CCID article on HIFU combinations for double chin was seen as a title only — [Dovepress](https://dovepress.com/double-chin-correction-with-combination-of-high-intensity-focused-ultr-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-CCID)

### Inferences
- Ranked by strength of evidence for skin quality in a young man: (1) daily SPF and sun avoidance (RCT); (2) not smoking (twin studies); (3) tretinoin or another retinoid at night if he wants an active (RCTs, mostly in older photoaged skin); (4) enough sleep and limited alcohol (observational; helps puffiness and "tired" look); (5) vitamin C (weaker); (6) collagen supplements (contested, likely small or no effect independent of funding). Massage, gua sha and microcurrent are "optional, low-certainty, low-risk".
- Submental fat responds to fat-targeted treatments (deoxycholic acid, cryolipolysis), not to muscle exercises. That fits the Cureus jaw-exerciser report's point that chewing muscles do not act on submental fat.

### Gaps
- The Oyetakin-White (2015) sleep-quality and skin-aging study could not be verified in search results.
- No independent RCT of home microcurrent or home RF with a sham arm was found. The vendor-reported trials are unverified.
- No sourced evidence was found on dietary sodium and facial puffiness. That link remains anecdotal.
- No HIFU submental efficacy numbers were obtained.
- The specific primary REFINE-1 and REFINE-2 publications (Dermatol Surg / Br J Dermatol) were not retrieved, so exact final responder figures are provisional.

## 6. Male-specific factors and realistic expectations for a 190 cm / 90 kg athletic man

### Takeaway
For a man of this size (BMI about 24.9), the biggest modifiable drivers of a "tighter oval and less double chin" are probably **facial adiposity** (whole-body fat), **head and neck posture in photos and daily life**, and **grooming** (beard shape). Facial exercise sits well behind these. Observers can read BMI from faces quite accurately, and changes of about 1.3 BMI units are noticeable. Beards amplify perceived jaw size and masculinity. Bone structure, which mainly determines how square the jaw is, is not changed by exercise or mewing in adults.

### Cited Findings
- Meta-analysis (de Jager, Coetzee & Coetzee 2018, Front Psychol): people estimate BMI from facial cues alone with r = 0.71 (n = 458) — [Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.02562/epub)
- Re & Rule (University of Toronto): a change of about **1.33 kg/m²** in BMI is needed for a face to look noticeably different in weight. About **2.59 kg/m² for men** (2.38 for women) is needed to shift attractiveness ratings — [U of T News](https://www.utoronto.ca/news/losing-weight-can-make-you-more-attractive-experts-say-%E2%80%93-theres-catch)
- Face morphs built on body-fat percentage changed ratings of masculinity, dominance, attractiveness and health (274 raters, Sci Rep 2018) — [Sci Rep](https://link.springer.com/10.1038/s41598-018-24911-0)
- Data-driven face-shape and colour models predict BMI better than classic ratios such as cheek-to-jaw width — [PMC4603950](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4603950)
- **Dixson, Lee, Sherlock & Talamas 2017, "Beneath the beard", Evol Hum Behav** (37 men photographed clean-shaven and bearded, plus manipulated composites). Beards amplify the perceived size of the lower face and jaw and raise masculinity and dominance ratings. A larger jaw raised masculinity and dominance in clean-shaven faces but had little effect once a beard was present. Attractiveness was highest for bearded faces with smaller jaws — [St Andrews repository](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/handle/10023/11747); [Glasgow eprints PDF](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/147936/1/147936.pdf)
- Dixson (press interview): a beard "gives the appearance of having a longer face and a bigger jawline, and it frames the mouth" — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/content/4282969/are-men-with-beards-really-more-attractive/)
- 2025 systematic review: lack of male participants limits generalizability of all facial-exercise and massage data — [PMC12658320](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12658320/)
- Higher BMI is associated with a more anterior hyoid, about 2 mm per 5 BMI units — [PRS Glob Open 2024](https://doaj.org/article/2ff23666d17146a3a0f629ac336eafd3)
- ≥8 drinks/week is associated with under-eye puffiness and midface volume loss (women, survey) — [PMC6715121](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6715121/)
- Six months of gum training raised bite force but **did not change masseter thickness or mandibular shape** in healthy adults — [Jung 2024](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/effects-of-gum-chewing-training-on-occlusal-force-masseter-muscle-thickness-and-mandibular-shape-a-randomised-controlled-clinical-trial/). Hard "jawline gum" may damage teeth or the jaw — [Powers Health 2024](https://www.powershealth.org/about-us/newsroom/health-library/2024/08/12/tiktok-craze-extra-hard-gum-wont-chisel-your-jawline-it-might-even-hurt-you)

### Inferences
- Arithmetic for this user: BMI = 90 / 1.90² ≈ 24.9. A just-noticeable facial change (≈1.33 kg/m²) is about **4.8 kg** of body mass at 190 cm. The attractiveness threshold for men (≈2.59 kg/m²) is about **9.4 kg**. For an athletic man, a fat-loss phase (while keeping muscle) is likely the single most visible lever for jawline and submental definition, far more than facial exercise. This extrapolates from BMI-face perception studies; body composition matters more than BMI per se for a muscular person.
- Grooming: a beard or stubble shaped with a crisp neckline about 1–2 finger-widths above the Adam's apple, following the mandibular border, would amplify perceived jaw size (Dixson). The specific neckline rule is a grooming convention, not a studied variable.
- Short-term puffiness (alcohol, poor sleep) affects the eyes and midface, as the cited studies show. Whether it also affects the jawline is not shown. "Morning face" photos should be standardized for time of day.
- Realistic messaging: exercise will not change bone or create a "square" mandible in an adult. It may give a slight gain in muscle tone and possibly in submental support, with uncertain visibility. Posture, leanness, sleep, alcohol, SPF and grooming carry more weight.

### Gaps
- No study of facial exercise, mewing or facial devices in men.
- No study quantifying how much facial or submental fat changes per kg of body-fat loss in lean men.
- No sourced data on sodium or water retention and facial contour.
- No study on stubble length and perceived jaw definition beyond Dixson's full-beard work.

## 7. Muscles worked by commonly recommended "jawline" exercises (for the 3D model), EMG support, and risks

### Takeaway
Only a few lower-face and neck movements have direct EMG or imaging support. These are suprahyoid activation by CTAR/chin-tuck and by tongue-to-palate pressing, sternocleidomastoid co-activation in head-lift (Shaker), zygomaticus major and digastric growth with resistance-mouthpiece exercise, and orbicularis oris and platysma activity in pucker and neck-wrinkle tasks. The muscle maps for the other popular exercises below come from standard functional anatomy and are labelled as such. The main risks are TMJ or masticatory overload (jaw jut, hard gum, hard mewing), accentuated platysmal bands (grimace/neck curls) and, theoretically, expression lines from high-repetition movements.

### Cited Findings
- **CTAR / chin tuck against a ball or TheraBand:** suprahyoid group activated, with more suprahyoid specificity and less SCM activity than Shaker — [Sze 2016](https://www.springermedicine.com/chin-tuck-against-resistance-ctar-new-method-for-enhancing-supra/21129036); [Kılınç 2019](https://openaccess.hacettepe.edu.tr/handle/11655/24680). Eight weeks raised maximal suprahyoid activation and tongue pressure — [Dysphagia 2019](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s00455-019-10079-w)
- **Shaker / supine head lift ("neck curl"):** suprahyoids plus marked **sternocleidomastoid** activity — [Sze 2016](https://www.springermedicine.com/chin-tuck-against-resistance-ctar-new-method-for-enhancing-supra/21129036). Its suprahyoid EMG equals about 50–65% of maximal tongue-palate pressure — [PMC3871479](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3871479)
- **Tongue press to palate:** suprahyoid EMG rises with palatal pressure, most clearly with posterior-palate pressure — [Rev CEFAC](https://new.scielo.br/j/rcefac/a/NMRwcvpdGzdhvfS9KjfRLpb/?lang=en); [PMC3871479](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3871479)
- **Submandibular push** (thumb under chin, resist jaw opening) was compared with CTAR and Shaker for selective supra- and infrahyoid activation; results not retrieved — [Sci Rep 2021 (Chang et al.), DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/17e3c4bddd7f41acadf08eea061e816c)
- **Lip pucker ("fish face"/"kiss") and neck-skin wrinkling:** in a 12-subject sEMG reference set, orbicularis oris was active in puckering and platysma in neck-skin wrinkling. Zygomaticus major, frontalis and orbicularis oculi were also recorded — [PMC7647650](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7647650)
- Masseter sEMG strongly cross-contaminates other facial channels, so clenching during "face" exercises blurs which muscle is working — [Tampere Univ. survey](https://trepo.tuni.fi/handle/10024/129023)
- **Resistance-mouthpiece exercise (Pao: hold with lips, move head):** zygomaticus major and **digastric** cross-sectional area increased — [Hwang 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5888959)
- **Jaw jut (mandibular protrusion):** the lateral pterygoid is the primary protruder. The closing muscles (masseter, temporalis, medial pterygoid) are used almost constantly and can become relatively over-strong, which is linked to clenching and TMD spasm cycles (patent background text, not a study) — [US Patent 7775938](https://patents.justia.com/patent/7775938); [Rehab My Patient: lateral pterygoid](https://www.rehabmypatient.com/head/lateral-pterygoid)
- Overuse of jaw exercisers carries a potential TMD risk, and professional supervision is advised (low-quality source) — [Redfish Group article](https://imageserver.redfishgroup.co.uk/do-jaw-exercisers-and-trainers-actually-work.html); [Acıbadem: jaw exercises](https://acibademinternational.com/blog/jaw-exercises-what-they-can-and-cannot-change-jawline-tmj-and-bruxism/). Extra-hard gum may harm teeth and jaw — [Powers Health](https://www.powershealth.org/about-us/newsroom/health-library/2024/08/12/tiktok-craze-extra-hard-gum-wont-chisel-your-jawline-it-might-even-hurt-you)
- Hard mewing can strain the jaw muscles and TMJ and move the incisors forward (clinic source). The AAO lists loosened teeth, bite change and tooth wear — [Acıbadem](https://acibademinternational.com/blog/mewing-what-orthodontists-say-about-tongue-posture-jawlines-and-the-evidence/); [Becker's Dental](https://beckersdental.com/dentists/42806-orthodontic-org-warns-against-mewing-tiktok-trend.html)
- Platysmal contraction produces bands; see Section 3 — [Yonsei](https://ir.ymlib.yonsei.ac.kr/handle/22282913/197744)
- No adverse events were recorded across the 12 trials in the 2025 systematic review — [PMC12658320](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12658320/)

- Programme design implications (inferred): build progressive levels around (a) CTAR/chin-tuck and graded tongue-palate isometrics (EMG-supported submental loading); (b) light midface resistance (Alam/Pao-type) only if cheek fullness is a goal; (c) daily posture cues and craniovertebral-angle self-checks. Keep (d) jaw jut, platysma grimaces and hard chewing at low volume or omit them, with pain/clicking stop rules. Volume reference from the literature: about 10-s holds × 30 reps for tongue-palate in the dysphagia case series; Alam's 30-min sessions are a ceiling, not a requirement.
- Wrinkle risk: repeated forceful facial expression is the mechanism that botulinum toxin targets for dynamic lines. High-volume smile, frown or grimace drills could theoretically deepen expression lines. No study was found that tests this for exercise programmes, and no adverse events were reported in the trials reviewed.

### Gaps
- No EMG data were found for the marketed exercises themselves ("jaw jut", "kiss the ceiling", "vowels", "fish face", cheek puff, smile resistance). The table's non-★ entries are anatomical inference.
- No study of TMJ injury rates from jaw-protrusion exercise programmes. The risk is extrapolated from TMD literature and expert or clinic advice.
- No study tested whether facial exercise programmes cause or worsen expression wrinkles.
