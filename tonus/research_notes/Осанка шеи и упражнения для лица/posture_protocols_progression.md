# Progressive equipment-free exercise protocols and home self-assessment for forward head posture (FHP) and rounded shoulders (RSP)

> Method note for the report writer: all web-page fetching (PubMed, PMC, BMC, Dovepress, Europe PMC, university repositories) failed in this session with DNS errors, so every finding below comes from search-engine abstracts and summaries of the cited pages, not from full texts I read myself. Exact numbers marked "verified" appeared consistently in abstract-level text. Anything I could not confirm is listed under **Gaps**, sometimes with a recalled value clearly labelled "unverified, check before use". Population flag: most trials enrolled adolescents, students, office workers or neck-pain patients. Only Lynch 2010 (NCAA D1 swimmers) and Seidi 2020 (men 18–28 y with upper crossed syndrome) are close to a healthy, athletic 190 cm / 90 kg man.

## 1. Dosage in trials that changed CVA or shoulder posture: time to change and maintenance

### Takeaway
Combined programs (deep neck flexor strengthening, scapular retractor and lower trapezius strengthening, plus pectoral and suboccipital or cervical-extensor stretching) reliably raise the craniovertebral angle (CVA). Pooled effects are large (SMD about −1.5), but heterogeneity is high and many trials are low quality. Typical dosing is 3 sessions/week for 4–12 weeks (some trials use daily home work). Measurable group differences appear from 4 weeks, and gains mostly persist through 1 month to 6 months of follow-up or detraining. The best "athlete" evidence is an 8-week, 3×/week program in elite swimmers.

### Cited Findings
**Meta-analyses**
- Sheikhhoseini, Shahrbanian, Sayyadi & O'Sullivan 2018, *J Manipulative Physiol Ther* 41(6):530–539, systematic review and meta-analysis: 7 RCTs, n = 627. Therapeutic exercise produced a **large** change in CVA (evidence rated strong) and a moderate reduction in pain, with **no meaningful effect on cranial angle**. — [UL Pure record](https://pure.ul.ie/en/publications/effectiveness-of-therapeutic-exercise-on-forward-head-posture-a-s/); [PEDro](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/54380)
- Sepehri, Sheikhhoseini, Piri & Sayyadi 2024, *BMC Musculoskelet Disord* 25:105, systematic review and meta-analysis of upper crossed syndrome: 10 studies, searched to 12 Aug 2023, inclusion required programs of at least 3 weeks. Exercise improved forward head, rounded shoulder **and** thoracic kyphosis angles, and the effect may be slightly smaller in children than adults. Strength work, stretching, shoulder-based work and comprehensive whole-body programs were all suggested. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38302926/); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10832142/)
- 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs in upper crossed syndrome, *BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil* (searched to Nov 2025): forward head angle SMD **−1.49 (95% CI −1.91 to −1.06), I² = 79%**, with significant improvements in forward shoulder angle and hyperkyphosis too. Certainty was rated moderate. Title conclusion: corrective exercise "strongly improve[s] posture but fail[s] to produce consistent clinical or functional benefits". — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13102-026-01707-8)
- Iranian systematic review and meta-analysis (J Paramedical Sci & Rehabil, search to 2025): 28 studies. Most interventions lasted **4–12 weeks**. Forward head angle vs control SMD **−1.52 (95% CI −2.47 to −0.57), p = 0.002, I² = 91%**. Improvements were also seen in CVA, pain, muscular performance and joint position sense, with a caution about heterogeneity and study quality. — [JPSR](https://jpsr.mums.ac.ir/article_27075.html?lang=en)
- Carrasco-Uribarren et al. 2026, *J Man Manip Ther*: a further meta-analysis of therapeutic exercise on CVA in FHP exists, but only the bibliographic record was retrievable. — [UVa portal](https://portaldelaciencia.uva.es/documentos/6a37b55442e7be5794172c4d)

**Landmark and representative RCTs (dose → result)**
- **Harman, Hubley-Kozey & Butler 2005**, *J Man Manip Ther* 13(3):163–176 (RCT, PEDro 4/10: no concealed allocation, no blinding, no intention-to-treat analysis). Normal adults with FHP, exercise group n = 23 vs control n = 17. A **10-week targeted, progressive home program**: strengthen the deep cervical flexors and shoulder retractors, stretch the cervical extensors and pectoralis. Results: significant changes in neck flexion ROM and **one** postural measure vs control. — [PEDro](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/13467); [Elsevier Pure abstract](https://latam-cl.demo.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/effectiveness-of-an-exercise-program-to-improve-forward-head-post)
  - Secondary (unverified) details: compliance 91% and a 3.7° cervical ROM increase (from an academia.edu summary). A 2025 paper reusing the protocol states that difficulty was increased **once the subject could complete 3 sets × 10 correct repetitions with ease**. Registered replications use **4 × 30-min sessions/week × 10 weeks** or **3×/week × 6 weeks (18 sessions)**. — [academia.edu copy](https://www.academia.edu/20657327/Effectiveness_of_an_Exercise_Program_to_Improve_Forward_Head_Posture_in_Normal_Adults_A_Randomized_Controlled_10_Week_Trial); [NCT04959942](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04959942)
- **Lynch et al. 2010**, *Br J Sports Med* 44(5):376–381 (RCT), 28 NCAA Division I swimmers: an **8-week stretching + strengthening program, 3×/week**. Strengthening was **body-weight arm exercises** (exercise ball for support), and foam rolls were used for stretching. Significant group × time interactions (p < 0.05) for **forward head angle and forward shoulder angle**, plus strength gains across the shoulder girdle. — [BJSM](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/44/5/376); [Performance Health summary](https://www.performancehealthacademy.com/article-series/the-effects-of-an-exercise-intervention-on-forward-head-and-rounded-shoulder-postures-in-elite-swimmers-1.html); [PEDro](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/25907)
- **Seidi, Bayattork et al. 2020**, *Sci Rep* 10:20688 (RCT), **men** with upper crossed syndrome (protocol: 22 men aged 18–28; published n = 24). Comprehensive corrective exercise program (CCEP) for **8 weeks**, then **4 weeks of detraining**. Significant group × time interactions, and within-group changes from pre to post **and pre to follow-up** in head, shoulder and thoracic angles, EMG and scapular dyskinesis. **Gains persisted through 4 weeks of detraining.** — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-77571-4); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7692548/); [Trials protocol](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7069207/)
- **Ruivo, Carita & Pezarat-Correia 2016**, *Man Ther* 21:76–82 (RCT, PEDro 6/10), adolescents: a **32-week (8-month) resistance + stretching program delivered in PE classes**, then 16 weeks of detraining. Analysed n = 42 exercise vs 46 control. Cervical and shoulder angles increased significantly after 32 weeks. After 16 weeks of detraining there were **no significant differences** in the three postural angles. Sources conflict on meaning: one summary reads this as "angles largely comparable to control", the ACE report as "maintained". Posture gains did **not** translate into better clinical outcomes. — [PEDro](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/44908); [ACE report](https://myorthoevidence.com/AceReports/Report/8620); [NCT02190331](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02190331)
- **Ruivo et al. 2017**, *J Manipulative Physiol Ther* 40(1):1–10 (RCT, PEDro 7/10): a **16-week** version in PE classes (exercise n = 84, control n = 46, ages 15–17). Significant increase in cervical and shoulder angles (photogrammetry). — [PEDro](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/48929)
- **Diab & Moustafa 2012**, *Clin Rehabil* (RCT), n = 96 with lower cervical spondylotic radiculopathy and **CVA ≤ 50°**: a posture-corrective exercise program plus ultrasound/infrared vs ultrasound/infrared alone, **10 weeks**. CVA, pain and C6/C7 dermatomal SSEP amplitudes differed between groups at 10 weeks **and at 6-month follow-up**. — [PEDro](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/31473); [chiro.org abstract](https://chiro.org/research/ABSTRACTS/The_Efficacy_of_Forward_Head_Correction.shtml)
- **Lee DY et al. 2017**, *J Phys Ther Sci* 29(10):1824–1827 (RCT, PEDro 4/10), n = 28 in 3 arms (McKenzie 9, self-stretch 10, Kendall 9). CVA and scapular index improved **within** every group, with **no between-group difference**. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5684019/); [PEDro](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/51734)
- **Titcomb et al. 2023**, *Int J Exerc Sci* 16(1):954–973 (RCT), 79 young adults with FHP (55 F, 24 M, age 20.1 ± 2.2), 72 completed, **4 weeks**. Arms: postural education; self-myofascial release + stretching; self-myofascial release + stretching + strengthening; control. All three interventions improved CVA, corrective exercise possibly more than education, and **stretching-only ≈ stretching + strengthening** for CVA. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10464763/); [IJES](https://digitalcommons.wku.edu/ijes/vol16/iss1/8)
- **Argyrou et al. 2025**, *Brain Sci* 15:873 (RCT), 55 students aged 18–25 with **CVA < 50°**: a motor-learning-based program (attention-focus elements) of **12 sessions over 4 weeks** (3×/week). Results were not retrievable. — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12384431/)
- **Heydari et al. 2022**, *BMC Pediatr* 22:230 (clinical trial), 103 male students: an **8-week** strengthening + stretching program. Between-group difference for CVA (F = 89.04, effect size 0.47) and shoulder angle (F = 18.83, effect size 0.16). **Minimal clinically important difference (MCID): CVA 1.40°, shoulder angle 1.34°** (distribution method). — [BMC](https://bmcpediatr.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12887-022-03300-7); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35477430/); [correction notice](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9520818/)
- **Fathollahnejad, Letafatkar & Hadadnezhad 2019**, *BMC Musculoskelet Disord* (RCT), 60 women with neck pain and forward head and rounded shoulder posture, **6 weeks**, with a **1-month follow-up**. Stabilizing exercises ± manual therapy improved head and shoulder posture, pain and function, and the manual-therapy combination was better for head posture (effect size 0.432). — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6379958/)
- **Kang 2015**, *J Phys Ther Sci* (RCT), n = 20 students: deep cervical flexor training with vs without a pressure biofeedback unit, **3×/week for 6 weeks**, followed by **4 weeks of detraining**. CVA was measured from a lateral photo. The biofeedback group gained more cervical ROM through the detraining period. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4668167)

### Inferences
- **Time to measurable change:** CVA differences appear in trials as short as **4 weeks (about 12 sessions)**, e.g. Titcomb 2023 and Argyrou 2025. Most programs run 6–10 weeks, and a 32-week school program still worked. For an app, an 8-week block (Lynch, Seidi, Heydari) at 3 sessions/week is the best-supported "core program", with retesting at weeks 4 and 8.
- **Expected magnitude vs measurement noise:** the MCID of about 1.4° (Heydari) is far below the smartphone-app MDC of about 5° (see section 6). A single home photo cannot reliably show a "clinically important" change. Use averaged repeated photos and report trends, not single readings.
- **Maintenance:** gains held for 4 weeks of detraining (Seidi 2020), 1 month (Fathollahnejad 2019) and 6 months (Diab 2012), but were equivocal after 16 weeks of detraining in adolescents (Ruivo 2016). No trial tested a specific maintenance dose. A reasonable but unproven app design is a reduced maintenance phase (for example 1–2 sessions/week plus daily micro-habits) after the 8-week block.
- **Exercise type matters less than doing something structured:** McKenzie, Kendall and self-stretch performed similarly (Lee 2017), as did stretching vs stretching + strengthening (Titcomb 2023). That supports equipment-free versions of the trial programs.
- **Extrapolation:** the swimmers in Lynch 2010 and the young men in Seidi 2020 are the closest analogues to the target user. Pooled effect sizes come mostly from adolescents, students and patients with pain, who likely start with larger deficits, so a healthy athletic adult may see smaller absolute CVA changes.

### Gaps
- Exact sets, reps, holds and progression rules of Harman 2005, Ruivo 2016/2017, Lynch 2010 and Seidi 2020 could not be read because full-text fetch failed.
  - Recalled and **unverified**: Seidi/Bayattork CCEP followed an "inhibit → lengthen → activate → integrate" corrective-exercise continuum, 3 sessions/week.
  - Recalled and **unverified**: Ruivo's program used about 2 PE sessions/week, with exercises in about 3 sets of 15–20 repetitions.
  - Check the PMC full texts (PMC7069207, PMC7692548) and the DOIs before using these numbers.
- Moderator analyses (duration, frequency, exercise type) of Sheikhhoseini 2018 and Sepehri 2024 were not retrievable. Sepehri reported only a possible age moderator (smaller effects in children).
- CVA change in degrees for Harman 2005, Titcomb 2023 and Argyrou 2025 was not retrieved.
- No trial in healthy athletic men specifically with a CVA endpoint and an equipment-free protocol was found.
- No trial was found that defines a minimal maintenance dose.

## 2. Deep cervical flexor (DCF) training progression, the craniocervical flexion test (CCFT), and the deep neck flexor endurance test

### Takeaway
DCF training is a low-load, precision task: a gentle upper-cervical "nod", not a forceful retraction or head lift. It progresses through the CCFT pressure stages (22→30 mmHg, 2-mmHg steps), aiming for **10 × 10-s holds** at each stage before moving up. It then moves from supine to sitting and standing, and to endurance-strength (head-lift) work. Without a pressure biofeedback unit, use a folded towel under the head and visual and tactile cues. Biofeedback seems to add some benefit, so an app could substitute stage-like progression with time-based cues. The deep neck flexor endurance (DNF) test is a simple home endurance test: healthy men average about 39 ± 20 s, with moderate-to-good reliability.

### Cited Findings
**CCFT and training stages**
- CCFT protocol: supine, pressure biofeedback unit (PBU) inflated to a **20 mmHg** baseline under the neck. Slow, controlled nods to **22, 24, 26, 28 and 30 mmHg**, each **held 10 s**. The last stage held correctly is the "activation score". The "performance index" (secondary-source definition) is the increment above 20 mmHg × the number of successful 10-s holds (max 10). — [Pabau CCFT template](https://pabau.com/templates/cranio-cervical-flexion-test/); [Carepatron CCFT sheet](https://carepatron.com/files/cranio-cervical-flexion-test.pdf)
- Stop or failure criteria used in CCFT trials: inability to hold the level, head **retraction**, thoracic extension, superficial muscle substitution (sternocleidomastoid or anterior scalene), and pressure drop greater than 20% or substitution during the nod. — [NCT04173143](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04173143); [NCT06595927](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06595927)
- Performance norms: Chiu et al. 2005 (JOSPT) found chronic neck pain subjects reached a **median of 24 mmHg** vs **28 mmHg** in asymptomatic subjects (p < 0.001). A secondary source states most healthy adults hold 28–30 mmHg. — [JOSPT/Ovid](https://www.ovid.com/journals/jospt/pdf/00001218-200509000-00003~performance-of-the-craniocervical-flexion-test-in-subjects); [Pabau](https://pabau.com/templates/cranio-cervical-flexion-test/)
- Reliability of craniocervical flexion ROM at each CCFT stage: intra-rater ICC about **0.81–0.95**. — [PMC table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7678052/table/Tab2)

**Home (no-PBU) set-up and progression**
- A 2017 neck exercise algorithm sets the craniocervical flexion goal at **10 × 10-s holds**. Start supine in hook-lying with the neck in neutral, supported by a **folded towel if necessary**, and cue "nod the chin". The next step is to lift the head while keeping the chin tucked. — [PMC5719229, table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5719229/table/table3)
- A 2021 progression table (IJERPH):
  1. Supine nodding, with eye-movement facilitation.
  2. Sustained holds × 10 repetitions.
  3. **Sitting**: controlled head movement into extension and back to neutral.
  4. Co-contraction of deep neck flexors and extensors.
  - [PMC8535440 table 5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8535440/table/ijerph-18-10704-t005)
- Example home chin-tuck dosages used in studies:
  - **3 × 10 supine chin tucks daily, 10-s hold each**: CVA improved in the asymptomatic group (DUT dissertation; design and duration unclear). — [DUT OpenScholar](https://openscholar.dut.ac.za/handle/10321/1210)
  - **10 reps × 10-s holds per session, 3×/week for 6 weeks** (Iranian RCT protocol, Tai Chi vs chin tuck; results not found). — [IRCT 20463](https://www.irct.ir/trial/20463/pdf)
  - 4-week home progression: chin tuck held 2 s with a 4-s return, ×5; add a towel roll in week 2; add a head lift in weeks 3–4 (a registered RCT protocol in the same search set, which appears to be the Titcomb protocol). — [NCT05321654 protocol](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/54/NCT05321654/Prot_SAP_000.pdf)

**Effects on posture and the value of biofeedback**
- Falla et al. 2007, *Phys Ther* (RCT): 58 people with chronic neck pain randomized to **6 weeks** of craniocervical flexion training vs endurance-strength cervical flexor training (head lift), plus 10 controls. Before training, cervical angle drifted by a mean **4.4°** into a more forward posture over a 10-min computer task. **Only the craniocervical flexion group significantly reduced this drift.** The conclusion was that people with neck pain have a reduced ability to maintain upright posture when distracted. — [Birmingham research portal](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/effect-of-neck-exercise-on-sitting-posture-in-patients-with-chron/); [ACE summary](https://myorthoevidence.com/AceReport/Report/864); [PEDro](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/18081)
- Jull et al. 2007, *J Orthop Res* 25(3):404–412 (RCT): 64 women with chronic neck pain. Craniocervical flexion training and proprioceptive training **both** improved joint position error, pain and disability. — [ACE report](https://myorthoevidence.com/AceReport/Report/368); [PEDro](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/17753)
- Kang 2015 (RCT, n = 20): DCF training with a PBU vs conventional DCF training, 3×/week for 6 weeks. The PBU group retained or gained more cervical ROM through 4 weeks of detraining, and a CVA between-group difference was not confirmed in the abstract. — [PMC4668167](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4668167)
- Ashfaq & Riaz 2021 (RCT, n = 30, chronic mechanical neck pain): PBU-guided training gave statistically and clinically better DNF endurance than conventional training. — [PMC7931293](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7931293/)

**Deep neck flexor endurance test**
- Procedure (Harris/Grimmer style): supine hook-lying, chin tucked (axis about the ear), head and neck lifted **about 2 cm** off the surface (some summaries say 2.5 cm, which was not confirmed). Hold as long as possible. The test **ends when the chin tuck is lost (the anterior neck skin folds flatten)** or when the head rests on the examiner's hand for more than 1 s. — [Physiotutors](https://book.physiotutors.com/?p=146); [Prehab Guys](https://library.theprehabguys.com/?p=17981)
- Harris et al. 2005, *Phys Ther* 85(12):1349: 41 subjects with and without neck pain, 2 raters. Without neck pain, intra-rater ICC(3,1) **0.82–0.91** and inter-rater ICC(2,1) **0.67–0.78**. With neck pain, inter-rater ICC was 0.67, so reliability is too low to separate real change from error in pain patients. — [Regis ePublications](https://epublications.regis.edu/facultypubs/1134)
- Olson et al. 2006: a chin-tuck endurance test in 27 young adults without neck pain had **inter-rater ICC 0.83–0.88 when 2 trials were averaged**. — [Andrews Digital Commons](https://digitalcommons.andrews.edu/pubs/1868)
- **Norms, Domenech et al. 2011, *PM&R* 3:105–110**: 126 healthy adults aged 20–80. Mean hold **men 38.9 ± 20.1 s**, women 29.4 ± 13.7 s. Sex mattered, but **age and activity level did not**. Inter-rater ICC(2,k) **0.66 (95% CI 0.34–0.86)**. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21333948/); [Campbell University repository](https://cufind.campbell.edu/physical_therapy/556)

### Inferences
- **Equipment-free DCF ladder for the app** (built from the CCFT logic and the cited progressions; the ladder itself is not a tested protocol):
  - **L1** supine hook-lying, folded towel under the head: gentle nod (as if saying "yes"), **3–5 s × 5–10**, focusing on quality (no SCM bulge, no jaw clench, no head lift).
  - **L2** supine **10 × 10-s** nods. This is the Jull-style target, and passing it gates the next level.
  - **L3** sitting against a chair back or standing with the back to a wall (head neutral, not pressed): nod holds 10 × 10 s, then add slow eye and head movements and co-contraction.
  - **L4** endurance-strength: supine chin-tucked head lift of about 2 cm. Progress hold time (for example 5→10→20→30+ s) toward or beyond the male norm of about 39 s. Inclined or prone variants are possible later.
  - **L5** integration: hold neutral during a 10-min screen task, to target the Falla "drift" deficit.
- Because a PBU seems to add benefit, the app should give substitute feedback: a fingertip on the front of the neck to detect SCM over-activation, a mirror or phone selfie side-view to check nod vs retraction, and the towel-crease method.
- **DNF test as the home endurance tracker:** for this user, a baseline below about 20 s would be about 1 SD under the male mean. Because inter-rater ICC is only 0.66–0.78 and self-timing adds noise, take the best or average of 2 trials (Olson 2006 averaged 2) and look for changes of about 10 s or more. That threshold is an inference, not a published MDC.

### Gaps
- Jull's original CCFT paper (Jull, O'Leary & Falla 2008, *JMPT*) and the exact "10 × 10 s per stage, then progress 2 mmHg" training rule were confirmed only through secondary sources.
- Falla 2007 dosage (recalled and **unverified**: about 10–20 min, about twice daily, at home for 6 weeks) was not confirmed.
- No validated no-PBU home version of the CCFT was found, and no reliability data for a towel-only or self-administered nod test.
- Harris 2005 group means were not retrieved. Recalled and **unverified**: about 39 s without neck pain vs about 24 s with neck pain.
- No published MDC for a self-timed DNF test was found.
- The O'Leary (Jull group) 6-week craniocervical flexion exercise (n = 27) vs cervical flexion exercise (n = 23) trial in women with mild chronic neck pain was seen only as a mention, without its URL or details.

## 3. Scapular stabilizer progression without equipment (lower and middle trapezius, serratus anterior; low upper trapezius)

### Takeaway
Cools 2007 identified **side-lying external rotation, side-lying forward flexion, prone horizontal abduction with external rotation ("prone T, thumbs up") and prone extension** as the best exercises for lower and middle trapezius activation relative to upper trapezius. They are body-weight or gravity-loaded and so equipment-free. For the serratus anterior, the **standard push-up plus** gives the highest activation (up to 123% MVIC) with the lowest upper trapezius/serratus ratio (< 0.2). Wall and modified versions are easier but have higher upper trapezius/serratus ratios (up to about 2.0), which makes a natural lever-length progression.

### Cited Findings
- **Cools et al. 2007**, *Am J Sports Med* 35(10):1744–1751: EMG in 45 healthy subjects across 12 common scapular exercises.
  - Selected for intramuscular trapezius balance: **side-lying external rotation, side-lying forward flexion, prone horizontal abduction with ER, prone extension**.
  - Lowest UT/LT ratios: side-lying forward flexion, side-lying ER and prone horizontal abduction with ER.
  - A secondary summary says the selected ("category 1") exercises had UT/LT ratios **< 60% in all 3 phases**. Another says prone abduction, side-lying forward flexion and prone horizontal abduction with ER activated **LT up to about 80% MVIC while keeping UT below 50% MVIC**.
  - [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17606671/); [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0363546507303560); [Brookbush review](https://brookbushinstitute.com/article/what-are-the-best-exercises-to-promote-scapular-muscle-balance)
- De Mey et al. 2013, *JOSPT* (overhead athletes): the same 4 Cools exercises show low UT/MT and UT/LT ratios. **Conscious scapular orientation correction did not change these ratios** for any of the 4 exercises. — [JOSPT](https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2013.4283)
- Side-lying ER variations (IJSPT 2023): UT/LT and related ratios varied with arm position and with scapular protraction or retraction setting. — [IJSPT](https://ijspt.scholasticahq.com/article/74969)
- A review found upper trapezius **least active in prone, side-lying and supine exercises**, which supports gravity-minimised or lying positions early in a program. — [Brookbush review](https://brookbushinstitute.com/articles/what-are-the-best-exercises-to-promote-scapular-muscle-balance)
- **Ludewig et al. 2004**, *Am J Sports Med*: the **standard push-up plus** produced the highest serratus activation (**to 123% MVIC**) and the **lowest trapezius/serratus ratios (< 0.2)** in the plus phase. Wall and other modified push-up-plus variants had higher UT/SA ratios (up to about 2.0). The push-up plus is recommended when UT over-activity or imbalance is present. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14977678/)
- Wall slides: serratus activation comparable to the push-up plus and to scaption at 90°, increasing above 90°. Upper trapezius was higher in wall slides at 120° than in scaption. — [Brookbush wall-slide review](https://brookbushinstitute.com/article/increased-serratus-anterior-activation-during-wall-slides-and-scaption)
- Patselas et al. 2021, *J Bodyw Mov Ther*: the wall slide showed the **lowest activation of all muscles** tested, and push-up variants gave lower UT/SA ratios than elevation exercises. — [UTH repository](https://ir.lib.uth.gr/xmlui/handle/11615/77983)
- Lynch 2010 used **body-weight arm exercises** for scapular and shoulder strengthening in elite swimmers and still changed forward head and forward shoulder posture over 8 weeks. — [Performance Health summary](https://www.performancehealthacademy.com/article-series/the-effects-of-an-exercise-intervention-on-forward-head-and-rounded-shoulder-postures-in-elite-swimmers-1.html)

### Inferences
- **Equipment-free scapular ladder** (inference; built from the EMG rankings and lever-length logic):
  - **L1** side-lying ER (towel under the elbow, no weight) and side-lying forward flexion. Prone extension with arms at the sides ("prone W/arms down"). Wall slides to 90°.
  - **L2** prone horizontal abduction with ER ("T, thumbs up"), then prone Y (prone abduction/flexion). Lengthen the lever by straightening the arm from bent to straight elbow, add end-range holds (3–5 s → 10 s), and slow the tempo (time under tension).
  - **L3** push-up-plus progression from wall → incline (table or chair) → knees → full push-up plus. This uses lever length to raise serratus demand, and Ludewig shows the full version also has the best UT/SA ratio.
  - **L4** standing integration: wall angels or wall slides above 90° with scapular control, prone "Y-T-W" circuits with longer holds, and carry-over cues (shoulders "back and down" without shrugging).
- For a 90 kg athlete the full push-up plus is likely accessible from week 1. Prone Y/T holds lengthened to 30–45 s with 1–2 kg household objects (water bottles) are a near-equipment-free overload option if the user accepts it.
- De Mey 2013 suggests conscious "scapular setting" cues do not change the ratios. Exercise selection matters more than verbal cueing for upper trapezius control.

### Gaps
- Exact Cools 2007 per-phase UT/LT, UT/MT and UT/SA percentages and %MVIC tables were not retrieved. The full text is needed.
- Hardwick 2006 (wall slide EMG) could not be located.
- No RCT was found that isolates an equipment-free Cools-style program with CVA or shoulder-angle outcomes in healthy athletes.
- %MVIC for knee push-up plus vs incline push-up plus was not found.

## 4. Stretching parameters (pectoralis minor, upper trapezius, levator scapulae, SCM) and whether stretching alone changes posture

### Takeaway
The **unilateral corner/doorway self-stretch** lengthens the pectoralis minor most: about 2.24 cm vs 1.69 cm for a supine manual stretch and 0.77 cm for a sitting manual stretch (Borstad & Ludewig 2006). A sustained 5-min passive stretch acutely reduced pec minor stiffness and increased scapular external rotation and posterior tilt in healthy men. Evidence that stretching programs alone change resting scapular posture or kinematics is **inconclusive**. For CVA, stretching-focused programs did about as well as combined programs in short trials (Titcomb 2023, Lee 2017, Hassan 2022). Stretching is a useful component but should not be the sole intervention.

### Cited Findings
- **Borstad & Ludewig 2006**, *J Shoulder Elbow Surg* (n = 50 healthy adults, electromagnetic motion capture): the **unilateral self-stretch** (corner type) gave the largest pec minor length change, **2.24 cm**, vs supine manual stretch **1.69 cm** and sitting manual stretch **0.77 cm**. All three differed significantly. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16679233/)
- Commentary: the winning stretch is often called a "doorway" stretch, and clinicians caution that the corner stretch may stress the **anterior shoulder capsule**, so it should be monitored. This is commentary, not a trial finding. — [Mike Reinold blog](https://mikereinold.com/pectoralis-minor-stretch/)
- **Umehara et al. 2018**, *J Shoulder Elbow Surg* 27(7):1214–1220 (healthy men, 15 limbs stretched vs 15 control): passive horizontal abduction at **150° for 5 min**.
  - Pec minor stiffness fell by 3.2 kPa immediately and by 2.5 kPa after arm elevation.
  - Scapular **external rotation +4.8° and posterior tilt +3.3°** in abduction, and **+4.5° and +3.7°** in scaption.
  - Upward rotation was unchanged.
  - [Kyoto Univ. repository](https://repository.kulib.kyoto-u.ac.jp/dspace/handle/2433/244286)
- A registered **6-week daily** standing wall-lean pec minor stretch trial (NCT01956240, completed, n = 97) measured resting length and 3D scapular kinematics. Its results were not found. — [MedPath record](https://trial.medpath.com/zh/clinical-trial/11261bef8d3bd03d/nct01956240-stretching-protocol-pectoralis-minor-muscle-scapula-kinematics-impingement-syndrome)
- An ISB 2013 study of **4- and 8-week** pec minor stretching found **no clinically meaningful change in scapulothoracic rotations**. A 2020 systematic review rated the effects of pec minor stretches on scapular kinematics as **inconclusive**. — [ISB 2013 abstract](https://media.isbweb.org/images/conferences/isb-congresses/2013/oral/cb-shoulder1.03.pdf)
- Stretching vs strengthening for FHP and RSP:
  - Hassan et al. 2022, *Ann Allied Health Sci* (RCT, n = 34, asymptomatic): both arms improved and were judged equally effective, with somewhat larger gains for strengthening. — [AAHS](https://aahs.kmu.edu.pk/index.php/aahs/article/view/161)
  - Titcomb 2023: self-myofascial release + stretching was about equal to self-myofascial release + stretching + strengthening for CVA at 4 weeks. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10464763/)
  - Lee 2017: self-stretch was about equal to McKenzie and Kendall. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5684019/)
- Harman 2005 combined **cervical extensor and pectoralis stretches** with strengthening. — [PEDro](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/13467)

### Inferences
- **Practical stretch dosing for the app** (inference; the trials above do not pin down hold time):
  - Unilateral doorway/corner pec stretch with the arm at about 90° abduction, elbow bent, trunk rotated away.
  - Suboccipital or upper-cervical extensor stretch: a chin-nod stretch, with a hand assist if needed.
  - Upper trapezius and levator scapulae stretches: side-bend, or side-bend + rotation, with the opposite hand anchoring the shoulder.
  - Dose: **2–3 × 30 s per side**, daily or on training days. Use a gentle intensity (stretch sensation without pain, tingling or numbness) to protect the anterior capsule and nerve tissues.
  - Optionally add a **longer 2–5-min "accumulated" pec hold** on rest days, based on the 5-min acute effect in Umehara 2018.
- Because posture changes after stretching alone are inconsistent, the app should pair every stretch block with a strengthening or endurance block (as all the effective RCT programs did).

### Gaps
- No trial found that defines optimal hold time or frequency for upper trapezius, levator scapulae or SCM stretching with a CVA outcome.
- The Rosa/Borstad 2017 (J Hand Ther) 6-week pec minor stretching results were not retrieved.
- Borstad's Pectoralis Minor Index (recalled and **unverified**: resting length ÷ height × 100, with less than about 7.65 considered "short") was not confirmed this session.
- Stretch dosage in Harman, Lynch and Ruivo was not retrieved.

## 5. Thoracic extension mobility drills (seated over chair back, supine on towel roll, quadruped thread-the-needle, wall angels)

### Takeaway
Small RCTs show thoracic-focused interventions improve CVA, sometimes more than cervical-only work in the short term. Trials using **thoracic extension exercise** combined with scapular stabilization (6 weeks) improved CVA, though not more than cervical stabilization + stretching. No trial isolated specific home drills (chair-back extension, towel-roll, thread-the-needle, wall angels). They are reasonable, low-risk components but have little direct evidence.

### Cited Findings
- Cho et al. 2017 (RCT, n = 32 with FHP): **thoracic mobility** treatment beat upper cervical mobilization + stabilization in the short term on **standing CVA**, cervical extension, pain (NPRS), disability (NDI) and global rating of change (GRC). — [PMC5727966](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5727966) (approximate attribution, verify)
- Kang et al. 2021, *Turk J Phys Med Rehabil* (RCT, 32 office workers with FHP): **scapular stabilization + thoracic extension exercises vs cervical stabilization + stretching, 6 weeks**. Both groups improved CVA, FEV1, VAS and NDI. Only the experimental group improved maximal inspiratory and expiratory pressure and FVC. Between-group differences were seen only for FEV1 and VAS. — [TFTRD abstract](https://ftrdergisi.com/abstract.php?id=4251)
- Ongoing registered RCTs: thoracic expansion breathing vs DNS exercise in FHP (**twice daily for 6 weeks**, n ≈ 32), and scapular stabilization vs thoracic extension in "text neck". — [NCT07238660](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07238660); [NCT06108752](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06108752)
- Sepehri 2024: exercise also reduced **thoracic kyphosis** angles in upper crossed syndrome. — [PMC10832142](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10832142/)
- Seidi 2020: thoracic angle improved with the comprehensive program in young men and persisted after 4 weeks of detraining. — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-77571-4)
- Wall slides above 90° demand serratus activation, which makes "wall angel/slide" a combined thoracic extension + scapular drill. — [Brookbush](https://brookbushinstitute.com/article/increased-serratus-anterior-activation-during-wall-slides-and-scaption)

### Inferences
- **Thoracic ladder** (inference):
  - **L1** supine over a rolled towel placed horizontally at mid-thoracic level, arms by the sides then overhead. 5–10 slow breaths, 1–2 positions.
  - **L2** seated extension over the chair back (hands behind the head, 8–10 reps, 2–3 s at end range). Quadruped thread-the-needle (thoracic rotation), 8–10 per side.
  - **L3** standing wall angels (head, upper back and sacrum on the wall; slide only as far as contact holds), 2 × 8–12 slow. Prone thoracic extension ("cobra" without lumbar dominance).
  - **L4** integrate into movement: wall angel → overhead reach while walking, plus 1-min micro-breaks during the workday.
- Rib-cage breathing in the extension positions could be layered in. A respiratory benefit was seen in Kang 2021, and breathing trials are ongoing.

### Gaps
- No trial found that tests the specific drills (chair-back, towel roll, thread-the-needle, wall angels) as stand-alone interventions with CVA outcomes.
- Thoracic dosing details of Cho 2017 and Kang 2021 were not retrieved.
- No reliable home measure of thoracic kyphosis without equipment was validated this session. Occiput-to-wall distance is a candidate (section 6).

## 6. Self-assessment at home with a smartphone (CVA photo, wall tests, shoulder measures)

### Takeaway
CVA from a lateral photo (tragus → C7 line vs horizontal) has good-to-excellent reliability with smartphone apps and image software (ICC about 0.87–0.99). The measurement error matters, though: one validated app has an **SEM of about 1.8° and MDC of about 5.1°**, while the published **MCID for exercise is about 1.4°**. A home app must standardise the photo protocol and average several shots. There is **no consensus cutoff**: FHP thresholds in the literature range from ≤44° to ≤54°, with "normal" often above 53° or 55°. Standing values differ from sitting values. The wall tests (tragus-to-wall, occiput-to-wall) are reliable in ankylosing spondylitis cohorts. The supine pectoralis minor length test (acromion-to-table) is reliable within a rater.

### Cited Findings
**Smartphone and photo CVA reliability**
- Ceballos-Laita et al. 2023, computer-vision app (n = 42; healthy, neck pain, tension-type headache): test–retest **ICC 0.92**, inter-rater **ICC 0.91**, validity vs Kinovea **r = 0.94**, **SEM ≈ 1.83°, MDC ≈ 5.07°**. — [UIC repository](https://repositori.uic.es/handle/20.500.12328/3655); [UVa PDF record](https://uvadoc.uva.es/handle/10324/63749)
- Aafreen et al. 2023, *Heliyon*, Surgimap app (n = 90 with neck pain): intra-rater **ICC 0.972–0.991**, r = 0.95 vs Surgimap desktop. **CVA is lower sitting than standing.** — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/205493a180b74ef29ea985be3b587362)
- CA-SPR app (Korea, n = 48): intra-rater ICC 0.889–0.989, inter-rater 0.800–0.980, but only **r = 0.623 vs X-ray**. — [KoreaScience PDF](https://koreascience.or.kr/article/JAKO202027265523976.pdf)
- Systematic review of smartphone CVA apps (15 studies): intra- and inter-rater ICCs **0.865–0.991**, validity up to 0.944, with small samples throughout. — [GMR](https://geneticsmr.com/index.php/gmr/article/view/3249)
- Caution: a new app in 22 healthy men had ICC 0.89 but only **r = 0.47 vs goniometer** (congress abstract). — [Sport congress abstract](https://sportcongress.ut.ac.ir/paper?manu=57254)
- Standing vs sitting CVA in young adults with and without severe FHP (Titcomb et al. 2024, *Int J Exerc Sci* 17(1):73–85): position matters, so always test in the same position. — [IJES PDF](https://digitalcommons.wku.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=3775&context=ijes)

**Photo protocol details from trials** (sources were grouped in the search summary; attributions approximate)
- Camera on a tripod, **lens top at shoulder height, subject 2.9 m away**, feet on marked positions, **3 photos averaged**. — [NCT07098754 protocol](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/54/NCT07098754/Prot_000.pdf)
- Tripod at **C7 height, 3 m away**, camera orthogonal to the sagittal plane. — [RBCDH (UFSC)](https://periodicos.ufsc.br/index.php/rbcdh/article/download/1980-0037.2011v13n4p299/17905)
- **Lens 110 cm above the floor, 150 cm from the tragus** (MB-Ruler reliability study). — [Thieme](https://www.thieme-connect.de/products/ejournals/html/10.1055/s-0041-1741415)
- Markers on the tragus and the C7 spinous process. CVA is the angle between the tragus–C7 line and the horizontal through C7. A "self-balanced" standing position was part of standardised protocols. — [PMC11957747](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11957747); [PMC11042887](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11042887)
- Photogrammetry reliability: ICC 0.98 inter- and intra-examiner in university students (2025). ICC 0.89–0.99 between 2 raters in women, with marker re-application by one rater ICC 0.71–0.91. — [PMC11957747](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11957747); [PMC8287705](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8287705)

**Normal values and cutoffs**
- FHP cutoffs vary from **≤44° to ≤54°**. "Normal" is variously defined as **> 53°** or **≥ 55°**. — [PMC11042887](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11042887)
- "CVA < 48° should be considered FHP; some use < 50° or < 52°", while noting there are no validated cutoffs. — [Rev Pesq Fisioter (Bahiana)](https://journals.bahiana.edu.br/index.php/fisioterapia/article/view/6225/5601)
- Smaller CVA is associated with neck pain and greater disability. — [JRCRS (Riphah)](https://journals.riphah.edu.pk/index.php/jrcrs/article/download/445/254/1336); [Spine-health](https://spine-health.com/conditions/neck-pain/how-measure-and-fix-forward-head-posture)
- Trials commonly enrolled people with **CVA ≤ 50°** (Diab 2012) or **< 50°** (Argyrou 2025). — [PEDro](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/31473); [PMC12384431](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12384431/)

**Shoulder measures**
- Sagittal shoulder–C7 angle (horizontal through the lateral shoulder or acromion vs the C7–acromion line; smaller = more protracted): moderate-to-high intra-rater and high inter-rater reliability, and valid against radiographs. Normative mean **53.09 ± 6.12°** in 194 college students (Kinovea). — [PMC5446097](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5446097); [Mendeley dataset](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/4gct425czy/1) (approximate attribution: the search summary did not say which link carries the 194-student mean)
- A "front shoulder angle" photogrammetry test: sensitivity 0.81, specificity 0.69, but reported **not to work in males**. — [SciTePress](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2013/45981/45981.pdf)
- **Pectoralis minor length test** (Lewis & Valentine 2007, *BMC Musculoskelet Disord*): supine, linear distance from the table to the **posterior acromion**, 45 symptomatic + 45 asymptomatic subjects, two measurements at least 30 min apart. **Excellent intra-rater reliability** on both sides in both groups. Diagnostic accuracy (sensitivity, specificity) was also examined. — [PMC1934353](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC1934353/)
- In 20 young men with asymptomatic rounded shoulders, mean acromion-to-table distance was **59.3 ± 9.6 mm** (2026 JFMK ultrasound study). — [JFMK PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/jfmk/jfmk-11-00283/article_deploy/jfmk-11-00283.pdf)
- Tragus-to-wall vs occiput-to-wall distance (ankylosing spondylitis, OASIS cohort): test–retest ICC **0.94–0.96 (occiput-to-wall)** and **0.93–0.95 (tragus-to-wall)**, comparable. A review of 39 tragus-to-wall studies found reliability and validity but few normative values. — [CORE](https://core.ac.uk/works/137428390)

### Inferences
- **Home CVA photo protocol for the app:**
  - Phone on a tripod, shelf or stack of books, propped vertically, **lens at shoulder or C7 height**, **1.5–3 m** away, perpendicular to the body side (use the phone's level or gyroscope to keep tilt at 0°). A 2–3 s timer or a partner takes the shot.
  - Same clothing (bare neck), hair off the ear, a small sticker or skin-marker dot on C7 (the most prominent spinous process at the base of the neck when the head is flexed).
  - Stance: march in place 3–5 steps, look straight ahead at a mark at eye level, take 2 relaxed breaths, then shoot (a "self-balanced" habitual posture, not corrected posture).
  - Take **3 photos, use the mean**. Same time of day, same position (standing), with a weekly or biweekly cadence.
  - The app computes the angle from the tragus and C7 points (user-tapped or pose-estimated).
- **Interpretation:**
  - Treat **≥ 50–53°** as "within typical range" and **< 48–50°** as "forward head".
  - Show changes **under about 5° as "within measurement noise"** unless the trend holds across 3+ sessions. This respects the 5.07° MDC while acknowledging the 1.4° MCID.
  - Also log the **shoulder angle** from the same photo (C7–acromion line vs horizontal; normative mean about 53°).
- **Wall test:** standing heels, buttocks and upper back on the wall, chin level. Measure **tragus-to-wall** (forward head) and **occiput-to-wall** (kyphosis; 0 cm = occiput touches) with a tape and a partner, or with a phone photo with a ruler in frame. Reliable in ankylosing spondylitis cohorts, but there are no healthy-adult norms.
- **Pec minor home test:** supine on a firm floor, relaxed. A partner measures floor-to-posterior-acromion with a ruler or carpenter's square (a phone photo with a ruler is also possible). Track side-to-side and over time, and treat only within-person change as meaningful.

### Gaps
- No validated, fully self-administered (no partner) smartphone CVA protocol was found. All reliability studies used examiners placing markers.
- Lewis & Valentine 2007 ICC, SEM and cm values were not retrieved. A cutoff for "short" pec minor in cm for 190 cm men was not found (values scale with body size).
- No healthy-adult normative tragus-to-wall data for men were found.
- The 52° shoulder-angle cutoff is commonly cited but no source was confirmed.
- The tragus–C7 horizontal-distance study (20 asymptomatic adults, ICC 0.81–0.96) was seen only via a search summary without a confirmed URL.

## 7. Habit and integration: ergonomics, "text neck", posture reminders, microbreaks, wearables

### Takeaway
Neck flexion during phone use is large (15–60°) and increases neck load and extensor activity with angle. Raising the device or viewing line (even with prism glasses) reduces flexion and discomfort in lab studies. Active-break or postural-shift prompts **halved the hazard of new neck pain** in a 6-month cluster RCT of office workers. Posture-feedback wearables and reminder apps are mostly in **registered, not yet reported** trials, with only pilot data showing behaviour change. Integration is plausible and low-cost but under-tested for CVA outcomes.

### Cited Findings
- Radiographic data: the cervical spine shows **15–60° flexion during smartphone use**, more when seated and texting. A link to disc degeneration is unknown. — [Eur Spine J](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s00586-019-06022-0)
- *Ergonomics* 2021 (n = 32, standing texting at 0°, 15°, 30°, 45° neck flexion): the gravitational moment and cervical extensor activity rose with flexion. **0° had the lowest moment, the lowest extensor EMG and the least discomfort.** — [KKU record](https://gsmis.gs.kku.ac.th/publish/details/31706?locale=en) (approximate attribution, verify)
- Smartphone use increased head and neck flexion in all postures. **Sitting without back support produced the greatest flexion** (n = 41). — [KoreaScience](https://koreascience.or.kr/article/JAKO201608366219536.pdf)
- *Work* 2021: **90° prism glasses** reduced neck flexion, head tilt and neck extensor activity during texting, and reduced discomfort in symptomatic users (n = 16 symptomatic + 9 asymptomatic). — [SAGE / Work](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/WOR-213463)
- Thai 6-month cluster RCT (193 high-risk office workers): **active breaks, adjusted HR 0.45**, and **postural shifts, adjusted HR 0.41**, for new-onset neck pain. — [JHSMR PDF](https://www.jhsmr.org/index.php/jhsmr/article/download/1389/1614); also see [PEDro 64456](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/64456). The search summary did not say which of these two pages holds this trial, so verify.
- Hourly computer prompts to interrupt sitting (pragmatic RCT, NCT05790837): neck pain was assessed at 3 and 6 months, and outcomes were not found. — [WCPT abstract](https://world.physio/congress-proceeding/computer-prompt-reduce-sitting-time-and-improve-musculoskeletal-neck-pain)
- Abadiyan et al. 2021, *Trials*: global postural re-education ± a **smartphone app** vs control in 60 office workers with chronic neck pain. Posture and endurance were secondary outcomes, and results were not retrieved. — [PMC8042925](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8042925/)
- App-reminded **1-min active breaks 7–8×/workday for 6 weeks** vs ergonomic education (registered, no results). — [NCT07791706](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07791706)
- Wearable feedback, registered RCTs without results:
  - Wearable feedback + exercise in office workers with chronic neck pain, CVA at 8 weeks. — [NCT06455774](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06455774)
  - Wearable vs app reminder vs exercise alone in university students, 6 weeks with 12-week follow-up, n ≈ 87. — [NCT07763327](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07763327)
  - Vibrotactile sensor + Kendall exercises, 3 days/week × 30 min, n = 63. — [NCT07163533](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07163533)
- Pilot (non-randomized, 10 men, office): a neck-worn inertial sensor with real-time visual alerts made participants recognise the need to raise their average CVA. — [KNU](https://research.knu.ac.kr/en/publications/real-time-forward-head-posture-detection-and-correction-system-ut/)
- UVA thesis pilot: real-time biofeedback reduced time spent in FHP. — [UVA](https://libraetd.lib.virginia.edu/public_view/sf268532d)
- Falla 2007: people with neck pain drift about 4.4° into FHP over a 10-min distracted computer task, and craniocervical flexion training reduced the drift. This supports training "posture endurance under distraction". — [Birmingham](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/effect-of-neck-exercise-on-sitting-posture-in-patients-with-chron/)

### Inferences
- **App integration layer:**
  1. Screen at eye level and phone raised toward eye level (aim for under about 15° neck flexion). Use back support when seated.
  2. Prompts every 30–60 min for a 1-min micro-break: 5 chin nods, 5 scapular retractions or wall angels, 1 thoracic extension over the chair back.
  3. A "posture check" cue tied to existing habits (unlocking the phone, starting a meeting, walking through a doorway → doorway pec stretch).
  4. Walking cue: "tall crown, eyes on the horizon, ribs down, shoulders wide".
  5. Optional camera-based or phone-tilt-based feedback (the phone's tilt sensor approximates viewing angle).
- Expect reminders to change **behaviour and symptoms** more than static CVA. Pair them with the strength and endurance program, which drives structural and postural capacity changes.

### Gaps
- No completed RCT shows that a reminder app or wearable alone improves CVA. Several are registered (2024–2026) and results are pending.
- No trial isolates screen-height adjustment as an intervention with CVA outcomes.
- Hansraj 2014's head-load estimates (recalled and **unverified**: about 4.5–5.4 kg at 0° rising to about 27 kg at 60°) come from a model, not measurement, and were not confirmed this session.
- No data were found on walking cues.

## 8. Safety: contraindications, red flags, technique limits, acceptable pain response

### Takeaway
Before an app-based neck program, screen for serious pathology (APTA neck pain CPG 2017, Grade A):
- infection, cancer or cardiac involvement
- cervical arterial insufficiency or dysfunction
- upper cervical ligamentous instability
- unexplained cranial nerve dysfunction
- fracture or trauma
- radicular or myelopathic signs

Refer these out. IFOMPT's 2020 cervical framework addresses vascular pathologies, though it is aimed mainly at manual therapy. DCF training is by design low load. Failure criteria in the CCFT include retraction, superficial muscle substitution and loss of control, which argues for gentle nods, not forceful chin tucks. No source was found that defines an acceptable pain response for posture exercise.

### Cited Findings
- **Blanpied et al. 2017**, "Neck Pain: Revision 2017", *JOSPT* 47(7):A1–A83 (APTA Orthopaedic Section CPG; 748 papers reviewed, restricted to systematic reviews and meta-analyses). **Grade A:** assess for serious pathology (infection, cancer, cardiac involvement, arterial insufficiency, upper cervical ligamentous instability, unexplained cranial nerve dysfunction, fracture) and refer for consultation when indicated. Imaging referral follows existing appropriateness criteria. — [Orthopt PDF](https://www.orthopt.org/uploads/content_files/files/Neck_Pain_Revision_2017%282%29.pdf); [PEDro](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/51144); [URI news](https://www.uri.edu/news/?p=73167)
- **IFOMPT International Cervical Framework** (adopted 5–6 Oct 2020; revision of the 2012 version): a consensus guide to assessing for **vascular pathologies of the neck** (which are rare but important) before planned interventions. It is "informative, not prescriptive", and dropped craniovertebral ligament testing. A separate IFOMPT red-flag framework exists for serious spinal pathology. — [IFOMPT resources](https://www.ifompt.org/Research+and+Resources/OMPT+Frameworks+and+Clinical+Resources.html); [Western University project page](https://uwo.ca/fhs/canspine/projects/ifompt-cervical-framework.html)
- CCFT technique guardrails: stop on retraction, thoracic extension, sternocleidomastoid or anterior scalene substitution, or more than 20% pressure loss. The task is a slow, measured nod. — [NCT04173143](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04173143); [Pabau](https://pabau.com/templates/cranio-cervical-flexion-test/)
- Diab & Moustafa 2012 safely applied posture-corrective exercise in people with **cervical radiculopathy**, with improved CVA, pain and SSEP. Radicular symptoms are therefore not an absolute contraindication under supervision, but that was a clinical setting. — [PEDro](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/31473)
- Corner/doorway pec stretch: clinician caution about anterior capsule stress. — [Mike Reinold](https://mikereinold.com/pectoralis-minor-stretch/)

### Inferences
- **App pre-screen (stop and see a clinician if yes):**
  - recent trauma
  - arm pain, numbness or weakness, or night pain
  - dizziness, drop attacks, double vision, trouble speaking or swallowing, facial numbness ("5 Ds / 3 Ns"-type symptoms)
  - severe or unusual headache
  - fever, unexplained weight loss or cancer history
  - balance or gait problems, clumsy hands
  - rheumatoid arthritis or Down syndrome (upper cervical instability risk)
- During exercise, stop if dizziness, visual disturbance or peripheral (arm) symptoms appear.
- **Technique rules:**
  - Chin nods at about 10–20% effort, without pushing the head back into the wall.
  - Head lifts only after 10 × 10-s supine nods are clean.
  - Stretches to mild tension, with no tingling.
  - Mild muscular fatigue or discomfort that settles within 24 h is likely acceptable, consistent with general exercise practice, but this is not sourced here.
