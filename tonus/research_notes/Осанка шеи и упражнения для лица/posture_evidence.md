Abbreviations: CVA = craniovertebral angle (tragus–C7 to horizontal; smaller = more forward head); SA = shoulder angle (photogrammetry); FHA/FSA = forward head/forward shoulder angle; DNF = deep neck (cervical) flexors; CCF = craniocervical flexion; LT/MT/UT = lower/middle/upper trapezius; SA (muscle) = serratus anterior, written out in full to avoid confusion; MVIC = maximal voluntary isometric contraction; UCS = upper crossed syndrome.

## 1. What do meta-analyses and systematic reviews say? Effect sizes, time to change, persistence

### Takeaway
Every meta-analysis from 2018 to 2026 finds that therapeutic or corrective exercise improves CVA (FHP) and shoulder angle more than control. Pooled effects are large in standardized units (SMD about 1.0–1.5). In absolute terms the change is modest: about **3–4° of CVA** (MD 3.38°, 95% CI 2.10–4.65, in neck-pain patients), against an MCID of **about 1.4° CVA and about 1.3° shoulder angle**. Changes appear after 4–10 weeks. Programs of at least 8 weeks and combined (multi-component) programs tend to do better. Certainty is low to moderate, heterogeneity is high (I² 79–91%), and few trials follow up beyond the end of the intervention.

### Cited Findings

**Meta-analyses / systematic reviews (newest first)**

**Landmark / representative RCTs (design, sample, population, quality)**

- **Harman K, Hubley-Kozey CL, Butler H. J Man Manip Ther 2005;13(3):163–176.** RCT, 10-week, targeted and progressive **home** program in **normal (asymptomatic) adults** with FHP; exercise n = 23, control n = 17. The program was **neck-extensor and pectoralis major stretches plus deep neck flexor and shoulder retractor strengthening**. The exercise group changed significantly in neck ROM and **one** postural measure, and differed from controls on those. **PEDro 4/10**: random allocation yes; no concealment, blinding, adequate follow-up or ITT. — [PEDro record](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/13467); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233642307_Effectiveness_of_an_Exercise_Program_to_Improve_Forward_Head_Posture_in_Normal_Adults_A_Randomized_Controlled_10-Week_Trial)
- **Ruivo RM, Carita AI, Pezarat-Correia P. The exercise group significantly increased cervical and shoulder angles at 32 weeks. A secondary summary gives **cervical angle about 45.0° → 46.9° and shoulder angle about 45.8° → 49.5° [unverified]**. Check the full text. — [PubMed 26028408](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26028408/); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1356689X15001095); [Academia copy](https://www.academia.edu/22374632/The_effects_of_training_and_detraining_after_an_8_month_resistance_and_stretching_training_program_on_forward_head_and_protracted_shoulder_postures_in_adolescents_randomised_controlled_study); [NCT02190331](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02190331)

Cervical and shoulder angles improved significantly in the intervention group; no between-group difference in pain or function at the end. — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Effects-of-a-Resistance-and-Stretching-Training-on-Ruivo-Pezarat-Correia/653613449df792dfa09aacce84daf4f44b55acd5); [e-JER systematic review](https://www.e-jer.org/journal/view.php?number=2013600779)

Control (n = 48): ultrasound + infrared. Exercise (n = 48): the same plus a **posture-corrective exercise programme**. Outcomes at 10 weeks and **6-month follow-up**. Significant between-group differences (baseline-adjusted) at 10 weeks for CVA, pain, and C6/C7 dermatomal SSEP amplitude, still significant at 6 months. Exact means are not available. — [Ovid abstract](https://www.ovid.com/journals/clrb/abstract/10.1177/0269215511419536~the-efficacy-of-forward-head-correction-on-nerve-root?redirectionsource=fulltextview); [chiro.org abstract](https://chiro.org/research/ABSTRACTS/The_Efficacy_of_Forward_Head_Correction.shtml)

Outcomes: periscapular EMG, movement pattern, and head/shoulder/thoracic posture. Significant group × time interactions; within-group improvements from pre-test to post-test and follow-up "in all outcomes"; between-group differences favoring CCEP at post-test and follow-up in three outcomes. SMRS gave CVA gains similar to SMRSS. - **Abdollahzade Z, Shadmehr A, Malmir K, Ghotbi N. J Mod Rehabil 2017;11(2):85–92.** **4-week** postural corrective exercise in college-aged women with FHP. CVA improved significantly (P = 0.0001), as did active neck flexion ROM (P = 0.04) and **lower trapezius strength (P = 0.03)**; middle trapezius strength did not change. Secondary sources describe the arms inconsistently, and a later review reports within-group CVA MD 3.1–4.4° across arms **[unverified]**. — [JMR](https://jmr.tums.ac.ir/index.php/jmr/article/view/81); [review on ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/395291476_Effectiveness_of_Strengthening_and_Stretching_Exercises_on_Forward_Head_Posture_A_Systematic_Literature_Review)

**Measurement and thresholds**
- CVA ≤ 50° is a common FHP cut-off; some studies use < 53°. — [Diab & Moustafa abstract](https://chiro.org/research/ABSTRACTS/The_Efficacy_of_Forward_Head_Correction.shtml); [Mahmoud 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12178-019-09594-y)
- CVA is a valid and reliable FHP measure, but rounded-shoulder posture "has no equivalent gold standard." Acromion-to-table distance ≥ 3 cm has been used as a rounded-shoulder cut-off. — [4-week sedentary-worker RCT, PMC10065112](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10065112/)
- A smartphone app measuring CVA showed intra-rater ICC 0.88, with 94.4% sensitivity and 84.6% specificity against photogrammetry. — [PMC7559098](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7559098/)

### Inferences
- A realistic target to communicate in the app is "a few degrees of CVA over 1–3 months," not a dramatic profile change. Pooled MD is about 3.4° in neck-pain populations, and MCID is about 1.4°. Users seeing roughly +2–4° CVA after 8–12 weeks would match the trial evidence.
- Time course: measurable change at 4 weeks (Titcomb; Abdollahzade). Most trials run 8–10 weeks (Heydari; Harman; Diab). ≥ 8 weeks is weakly favored (Xing subgroup). Long school programs (16–32 weeks, Ruivo) give small but significant gains in adolescents.
- Persistence evidence is thin. Maintenance is reported at 6 months (Diab, a patient population with ongoing co-interventions) and after 12 weeks of detraining (Seidi). Ruivo's 16-week detraining result is ambiguous. Without ongoing practice or habit change, assume gains need maintenance; this is inference, since no trial tests "maintenance dose."
- **Extrapolation to the target user (healthy, athletic, 190 cm/90 kg man):** most evidence comes from adolescents (Ruivo), students and young women (Titcomb, Abdollahzade, Heydari), office workers, and neck-pain or radiculopathy patients (Xing, Diab). Only Harman 2005 (asymptomatic adults, low quality) and Seidi 2020 (men with UCS) are close. All trials measure **static standing** CVA by photograph; none measures head position **while walking**, which is the user's complaint. Athletic men may start with a less extreme CVA and more strength, so absolute gains may be smaller.

### Gaps
- Full-text pooled SMDs and subgroup tables for Sepehri 2024 and Khorramroo 2026 could not be retrieved, and Sepehri's CIs appear garbled in the abstract.
- Exact CVA/SA means for Ruivo 2016/2017, Diab 2012, Seidi 2020 and Harman 2005 were not obtained. Ruivo detraining interpretation is unresolved.
- No trial found in healthy athletic adult men specifically. No follow-up beyond 6 months found.
- Sheikhhoseini 2018 pooled continuous outcomes as odds ratios, which is unusual; the method could not be checked.

## 2. Which components work best (DNF/chin-tuck, scapular retractors, serratus, pec stretching, upper-trap/levator/SCM/suboccipital stretching, thoracic mobility, combined/NASM, Pilates/yoga, ergonomics, feedback devices/apps)?

### Takeaway
Head-to-head evidence is weak. Most trials show **no clear winner between single approaches**: McKenzie, Kendall and self-stretch all improved CVA equally (Lee 2017). The most consistent signal favors **combined multi-component programs**: DNF/chin-tuck + scapular retractor/lower-trap strengthening + pec/neck stretching, ideally with thoracic work and ≥ 8 weeks. Combined programs are the only category that also reduced pain in the 2026 JMMT meta-analysis. NASM-labelled programs did not beat control in one 2025 meta-analysis. Posture-feedback apps and wearables have **no completed RCT results** yet (only registered protocols).

### Cited Findings
- Combined neck–thoracic programs and neck-focused programs both improve CVA short-term; only combined programs also reduced pain (13 RCTs, 819 participants; low to very low certainty). — [Carrasco-Uribarren 2026, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42281352/)
- Combined exercise trended toward larger functional (β −5.607, p = 0.070) and pain (β −1.489, p = 0.086) benefits than single-mode exercise (exploratory). — [Xing 2026, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13361434/)
- NASM-based corrective programs did not differ significantly from control for forward head angle (2025 meta-analysis), even though corrective exercise overall did (SMD −1.52). — [JPSR 2025](https://jpsr.mums.ac.ir/article_27075.html?lang=en)
- **DNF / chin-tuck component.** Harman 2005 combined deep neck flexor strengthening with retractor strengthening and stretches; a secondary summary attributes the change to "stretching (chin drop) and strengthening (chin tuck)". — [PEDro](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/13467); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233642307_Effectiveness_of_an_Exercise_Program_to_Improve_Forward_Head_Posture_in_Normal_Adults_A_Randomized_Controlled_10-Week_Trial). A 4-week DNF training trial with a pressure biofeedback unit reported improved CVA (degrees not retrieved). — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/379882145_Effectiveness_of_strengthening_deep_cervical_flexors_using_pressure_biofeedback_on_vertical_mandibular_opening_craniovertebral_angle_in_young_adults_with_forward_head_posture_-_An_experimental_study). DNF training has also been trialled in adolescents who use computers regularly. — [PMC7559831](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7559831/)
- **Scapular / lower-trap strengthening + pec minor stretching.** RCT in 60 young Saudi women with RSP: lower-trap strengthening + pec minor stretching was "more beneficial than" pec minor stretching alone for rounded-shoulder correction and shoulder flexion ROM. — [PMC9956189](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9956189/). Abdollahzade 2017 found lower-trap strength rose (P = 0.03) alongside CVA gains. — [JMR](https://jmr.tums.ac.ir/index.php/jmr/article/view/81)
- **Pec minor stretch alone.** Adding a unilateral corner stretch to an exercise program gave no significant short-term clinical benefit in subacromial pain syndrome (a pain outcome, not posture). — [ScienceDirect 2019 RCT](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2468781219301596). Stretching pec minor through **active scapular retraction** was tested against upright sitting for pec minor length and acromial distance. — [PubMed 28167190](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28167190/)
- **Thoracic mobility / extension.**
  - Office workers (n = 32): scapular stabilization + thoracic extension vs comparator. Both groups improved CVA, FEV1, VAS and NDI within-group; between-group differences only for FEV1 and VAS. — [ResearchGate 2021 RCT](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/356840429_Effects_of_a_combination_of_scapular_stabilization_and_thoracic_extension_exercises_for_office_workers_with_forward_head_posture_on_the_craniovertebral_angle_respiration_pain_and_disability_A_randomiz)
  - Upper-thoracic mobilization + mobility exercise vs upper-cervical mobilization + stabilization (n = 32, 6 weeks); CVA was an outcome (results not retrieved). — [PMC5727966](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5727966/)
  - Thoracic flexibility exercise vs cervical exercise in neck-pain patients with FHP (n = 24, assessor-blinded, 2026). — [Medicina 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/medicina62091666)
- **Postural education / habit training.** Postural education alone improved CVA within-group, but corrective-exercise programs gave greater CVA change than control and "may provide superior outcomes than PE alone." — [Titcomb 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37649869/)
- **Motor-learning-based program.** An RCT of a motor-learning-principles exercise program for FHP exists (Brain Sci 2025;15(8):873); results not retrieved. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3425/15/8/873); [PMC12384431](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12384431/)
- **Feedback devices / apps.** No completed RCT results found. Registered or protocol trials:
  - PostureAlert smartphone vibrotactile app: vibrates when cervical flexion exceeds 30°; 6 weeks vs postural education + exercise; n = 48 + 48; primary outcomes neck pain and CVA. — [ResearchGate protocol](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/403601338_Effectiveness_of_Smartphone_Vibrotactile_Biofeedback_on_Postural_Correction_and_Neck_Pain_Reduction_in_Desk_Workers_with_Chronic_Neck_Pain_A_Randomized_Controlled_Trial)
  - Wearable biofeedback + exercise vs app reminders + exercise vs exercise alone (n ≈ 87, 12-week follow-up). — [NCT07763327](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07763327)
  - Wearable vibrotactile sensor + Kendall exercises (n = 64, CVA < 50°). — [NCT07163533](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07163533)
- **Digital / telerehab delivery.** A cluster RCT of a digital-health corrective posture exercise program in adolescents (Medicine 2025) and an RCT of internet telerehab vs in-person exercise in young women with neck pain + FHP (JMIR Rehabil 2025) exist; results not retrieved. — [Medicine (Ovid)](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/md-journal/fulltext/10.1097/md.0000000000041893~effect-of-digital-health-corrective-posture-exercise-program); [JMIR Rehab](https://rehab.jmir.org/2025/1/e74979/PDF); [PMC12296222](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12296222/)
- **Comprehensive program in men with UCS.** The Seidi 2020 CCEP (8 weeks) improved alignment, muscle activation and movement pattern, with effects at 12-week follow-up. — [Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-77571-4)

### Inferences
- For an app, the best-supported design is a **combined routine**:
  - (a) DNF/chin-tuck activation
  - (b) scapular retractor / lower–middle trap strengthening (prone Y/T, side-lying ER)
  - (c) pec minor/major stretch (corner/doorway)
  - (d) optional upper trap / levator / SCM stretch
  - (e) thoracic extension mobility
  - (f) frequent posture-habit cues

  Run it for ≥ 8 weeks. This mirrors Harman, Ruivo, Seidi and the "combined" subgroup signals. It is synthesis, not a tested app protocol.
- An app's own reminders and voice cues fall under "postural education/habit/feedback," which has the weakest direct evidence. Pairing cues with exercise is better supported than cues alone (Titcomb).
- Because the user's complaint is head position **during gait**, motor-control/habit practice (cueing a neutral head while walking) is plausible but untested. No trial measures gait head posture as the outcome.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head RCT isolates DNF-only vs scapular-only vs pec-stretch-only on CVA in healthy adults with adequate power.
- No retrieved RCT results for Pilates, yoga or Schroth-type programs specifically on CVA/RSP. Searches did not surface them; this is not evidence of absence.
- No completed RCT of app or wearable posture feedback on CVA was found as of Oct 2026.
- Exact dose (sets, reps, holds, sessions per week) per component is not reported in the retrieved abstracts, except Ruivo (2×/week, 15–20 min).

## 3. Stretching vs strengthening vs combined; status of Janda's upper crossed syndrome

### Takeaway
Direct comparisons are few and small. One 4-week RCT (Titcomb 2023) found **self-myofascial release + stretching gave CVA gains similar to the same program with strengthening added**. Lee 2017 found no difference between stretch-based and strengthening-based (Kendall/McKenzie) approaches. For rounded shoulders, **adding lower-trap strengthening to pec minor stretching beat stretching alone** (2023 RCT). Meta-analytic subgroups mildly favor combined programs. UCS is a **clinical observation pattern, not a validated diagnosis**. Exercise reliably changes UCS posture angles but not pain or function (2026 meta-analysis).

### Cited Findings
- SMR + stretching ≈ SMR + stretching + strengthening for CVA at 4 weeks; both > control (n = 79 young adults). — [Titcomb 2023, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37649869/); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/373546731_The_Effects_of_Postural_Education_or_Corrective_Exercise_on_the_Craniovertebral_Angle_in_Young_Adults_with_Forward_Head_Posture_A_Randomized_Controlled_Trial)
- McKenzie vs self-stretch vs Kendall (n = 28): all improved CVA and scapular index; no between-group difference. — [Lee 2017, J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jpts/29/10/29_jpts-2017-214/_article/-char/en)
- Lower-trap strengthening + pec minor stretch > pec minor stretch alone for RSP (n = 60 women). — [PMC9956189](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9956189/)
- Combined programs trend toward greater benefit (Xing 2026 exploratory subgroups); only combined neck–thoracic programs reduced pain (Carrasco-Uribarren 2026). — [PMC13361434](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13361434/); [PubMed 42281352](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42281352/)
- A 2026 systematic review of strengthening and stretching exercises for FHP exists; its conclusions were not retrieved. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/395291476_Effectiveness_of_Strengthening_and_Stretching_Exercises_on_Forward_Head_Posture_A_Systematic_Literature_Review)
- **UCS status.**
  - Physiopedia describes UCS as "a clinical observation pattern rather than a validated medical diagnosis." Janda's foundational work was "primarily observational and based on clinical experience rather than controlled empirical investigation." — [Physiopedia](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Upper-Crossed_Syndrome)
  - Janda's proposed pattern: tight or overactive pectoralis major and minor, upper trapezius and levator scapulae; weak or inhibited deep neck flexors, serratus anterior, rhomboids, and middle and lower trapezius. — [Physiopedia](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Upper-Crossed_Syndrome)
  - A systematic review concludes Janda's approach has significant effects in UCS, but it rests on small trials (e.g., 52 medical students, 36 office workers). — [Medsciencegroup ASMP-10-121](https://www.medsciencegroup.us/articles/ASMP-10-121.php)
  - Corrective exercises in UCS improved FHA (SMD −1.49) and FSA (SMD −1.53), but not pain (low certainty). — [Khorramroo 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13102-026-01707-8)

### Inferences
- A practical reading: stretching alone can move CVA in the short term. Strengthening (DNF, lower/middle trap) seems more important for shoulder protraction and possibly for durability. Combined programs are the safe default. The evidence does not show strengthening is superior for CVA specifically.
- The app can use the UCS "tight vs weak" map as a **teaching model** for which muscles to stretch and which to strengthen. It should not claim that the user has a muscle imbalance that causes the posture; that causal mechanism is unproven.

### Gaps
- No retrieved EMG or imaging study confirming that people with FHP/RSP actually have the Janda tight/weak pattern.
- No adequately powered stretching-only vs strengthening-only RCT with follow-up.

## 4. Does FHP correction change pain, breathing or appearance? FHP–neck pain relationship

### Takeaway
**Pain:** FHP is modestly associated with neck pain and disability in adults and older adults, but **not in adolescents**, and age is a confounder. Exercise programs reduce pain in neck-pain populations. In UCS, corrective exercise did not reduce pain versus control. **Breathing:** a forward head acutely lowers spirometry values in healthy men (n = 15). One RCT showed a between-group FEV1 gain with thoracic extension + scapular work. **Appearance:** no study directly tests whether FHP correction changes perceived chin or jawline attractiveness. Profile studies suggest straight-neck profiles are rated most attractive, and the cervicomental angle (about 90–105°) drives perceived jawline.

### Cited Findings
- **FHP–pain association (Mahmoud NF, et al. Curr Rev Musculoskelet Med 2019;12(4):562–577; 15 cross-sectional studies).**
  - Asymptomatic vs neck-pain groups: CVA MD 4.84° (95% CI 0.14–9.54), i.e., more FHP in people with neck pain.
  - Adolescents: no difference (MD −1.05°, 95% CI −4.23 to 2.12).
  - In adults and older adults, CVA correlated with pain intensity (r = −0.55; 95% CI −0.69 to −0.36) and disability (r = −0.42; −0.54 to −0.28). Smaller CVA (more FHP) goes with more pain.
  - "Age played an important role as a confounding factor."
  — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12178-019-09594-y); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/The-Relationship-Between-Forward-Head-Posture-and-a-Mahmoud-Hassan/a7d530caac3117687aa91afb563d28a5102b6f87)
- **Exercise effect on pain.** In neck-pain patients exercise reduced pain intensity (Xing 2026). Pain OR 0.3 (0.13–0.42) in Sheikhhoseini 2018. Only combined neck–thoracic programs reduced pain in Carrasco-Uribarren 2026. Diab 2012 found reduced pain in radiculopathy at 10 weeks and 6 months. No pain or function difference in adolescents (Ruivo 2017). No pain difference in UCS (Khorramroo 2026). — [PMC13361434](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13361434/); [PubMed 30107937](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30107937/); [PubMed 42281352](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42281352/); [Diab abstract](https://chiro.org/research/ABSTRACTS/The_Efficacy_of_Forward_Head_Correction.shtml); [Ruivo 2017](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Effects-of-a-Resistance-and-Stretching-Training-on-Ruivo-Pezarat-Correia/653613449df792dfa09aacce84daf4f44b55acd5); [Khorramroo 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13102-026-01707-8)
- **Breathing.**
  - Koseki T, et al. J Phys Ther Sci 2019;31(1):63–68: **15 healthy males** breathing seated in forward vs neutral head posture. FHP shifted the upper thorax forward and the lower thorax forward/inward, reduced lower-thorax mobility, and significantly lowered **FVC, ERV, IRV, FEV1.0 and PFR**. This is an acute within-subject posture manipulation, not a training study. — [J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jpts/31/1/31_jpts-2018-328/_article); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30774207/)
  - Thoracic extension + scapular stabilization in office workers gave a significant between-group FEV1 difference. — [ResearchGate RCT](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/356840429_Effects_of_a_combination_of_scapular_stabilization_and_thoracic_extension_exercises_for_office_workers_with_forward_head_posture_on_the_craniovertebral_angle_respiration_pain_and_disability_A_randomiz)
  - A systematic review/meta-analysis of rehab programs that include breathing interventions for chronic neck pain with FHP exists (results not retrieved). — [PMC12467365](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12467365/)
- **Gait / postural control.** A systematic review on FHP, postural control and gait exists (Gait & Posture 2022); findings not retrieved. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0966636222006099)
- **Appearance.**
  - In a silhouette study, profiles with straight necks and foreheads were rated most attractive; forward/protruded combinations were rated least attractive (sample details not retrieved). — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233412438_The_effects_of_forehead_and_neck_positions_on_profile_esthetics)
  - In Naini et al., a submental–cervical (cervicomental) angle of about **90–100° was most attractive, and up to 105° acceptable**, judged by laypeople, patients and clinicians. — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Submental-Cervical-Angle:-Perceived-Attractiveness-Naini-Cobourne/96009b5dc609409e210626c2d530c4fcbf0bd274)
  - A systematic review reports post-mandibular-advancement cervicomental angles of 95–125°, so cut-offs vary. — [APOS](https://apospublications.com/cervicomental-angle-changes-observed-after-mandibular-advancement-surgery-a-systematic-review/)

### Inferences
- For a pain-free athletic user, the realistic benefits are posture and appearance plus possible prevention. Pain benefit cannot be promised. The pain evidence comes from symptomatic populations, and in pain-free adolescents and UCS cohorts pain did not change.
- Appearance claims should be framed carefully. A more upright head plausibly changes the visible neck–jaw line in profile, because the cervicomental angle depends on head and neck position. No study measured jawline perception before and after FHP exercise. Any "jawline" benefit in the app is inference.
- Breathing: claims beyond "a forward head acutely reduces measured lung volumes in healthy men" are not supported. The training evidence is a single small office-worker RCT.

### Gaps
- No RCT measuring perceived attractiveness or jawline after FHP correction.
- No training RCT in healthy athletes measuring respiratory outcomes after FHP correction.
- No data on whether correcting static CVA changes head position during walking.

## 5. Muscles worked or stretched by each core exercise (for the 3D model)

### Takeaway
- **DNF exercises** (supine head nod / CCF, chin tuck): longus colli, longus capitis, rectus capitis anterior and lateralis. The cue is to minimize SCM.
- **Scapular strengthening**:
  - prone "Y" (arm overhead in line with LT fibres) peaks lower trapezius
  - prone "T" with external rotation (thumbs up) peaks middle trapezius with a low UT ratio
  - side-lying ER and side-lying forward flexion favor LT/MT over UT
  - serratus anterior is driven by push-up plus, wall slides and scapular-plane elevation above about 120°
- **Stretches**: corner/doorway stretch lengthens pectoralis minor (largest change) and major. Neck stretches target upper trapezius, levator scapulae, SCM and suboccipitals; these mappings are anatomical/clinical with little EMG data.

### Cited Findings
- **Deep neck flexors = longus colli, longus capitis, rectus capitis anterior, rectus capitis lateralis.** They can flex the cervical spine without extending the upper cervical spine and head, which suits retraction/nodding. — [Brookbush Institute](https://brookbushinstitute.com/courses/integrated-functional-anatomy-deep-neck-flexors). Longus colli flexes the neck (simplified attachments: transverse processes C5–T3 → anterior arch of atlas). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longus_colli_muscle)
- **Longus colli in supine vs sitting.** 2025 ultrasound study, 24 people without neck pain: CCF in supine and sitting **activated longus colli equally (p = 0.758)**. In sitting, **ipsilateral oblique resistance** activated longus colli most robustly. Straight lateral force raised **SCM** activation (unwanted). — [PubMed 39862671](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39862671/); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2468781225000177)
- People with mechanical neck pain can activate DNF without superficial flexors during a supine CCF exercise series (real-time ultrasound). — [Daly & Prodoehl 2024](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/BMR-230002)
- The original CCF test measured DNF with oropharyngeal electrodes, because surface EMG cannot reach longus colli/capitis. — [Jull et al., JMPT CCF test](http://www.scottsevinsky.com/pt/reference/spine/cervical/jmpt_craniocervical_flexion_test.pdf); [PMC9316545](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9316545)
- **Trapezius and serratus anterior (Ekstrom RA, Donatelli RA, Soderberg GL. JOSPT 2003;33(5):247–258; surface EMG, 30 healthy subjects, 10 exercises).**
  - **Lower trapezius:** maximal with the **prone overhead arm raise in line with LT fibres** (= prone "Y").
  - **Middle trapezius:** **prone horizontal extension (abduction) with external rotation** (= prone "T", thumbs up), and the prone overhead raise.
  - **Serratus anterior:** maximal with exercises requiring large upward rotation, i.e., scapular-plane abduction > 120° and a diagonal flexion + horizontal flexion + ER.
  - **Upper trapezius:** maximal with the shrug.
  - Exact %MVIC values (e.g., the often-quoted 97% for prone Y) could not be verified **[unverified]**.
  — [JOSPT](https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2003.33.5.247); [Missouri State repository](https://bearworks.missouristate.edu/articles-chhs/1184/)
- **Cools AM, et al. Am J Sports Med 2007 (PMID 17606671).** Of 12 trapezius exercises, **side-lying external rotation, side-lying forward flexion, prone horizontal abduction with ER, and prone extension** had the best intramuscular balance (high LT/MT relative to UT). **None met criteria for UT/serratus anterior balance.** — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17606671/); [Brookbush summary](https://brookbushinstitute.com/articles/what-are-the-best-exercises-to-promote-scapular-muscle-balance)
- 2024 EMG study of five rehab exercises: prone horizontal abduction with ER had UT/MT = 0.43 and UT/LT = 0.30 (excellent balance). Side-lying ER had UT/serratus = 0.26. — [PubMed 38261765](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38261765/)
- Fine-wire EMG of the Cools exercises: **levator scapulae and rhomboid major** at moderate activity (25–45% MVIC); **pectoralis minor** low in the side-lying exercises (13–18% MVIC). — [Berckmans et al. AJSM 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/03635465211025002)
- Conscious scapular correction increased trapezius activation in prone extension and side-lying ER, but not in side-lying forward flexion or prone horizontal abduction with ER. — [De Mey et al. JOSPT 2013](https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2013.4283)
- **Wall slide** (Hardwick et al. JOSPT 2006; 20 healthy adults): serratus anterior activity did **not** differ between wall slide, wall push-up plus and scaption at 90° (P = .40). The wall slide lets the humerus go well above 90°. LT was "not activated much above resting levels" in the sample subject. — [JOSPT](https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2006.2306)
- Side-lying ER and the wall slide are recommended for targeting LT below horizontal in early rehab. — [J Electromyogr Kinesiol 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1050641123000366)
- **Pectoralis minor stretch (Borstad JD, Ludewig PM. J Shoulder Elbow Surg 2006).** Mean pec minor length change: **unilateral self-stretch (corner/doorway) 2.24 cm > supine manual 1.69 cm > sitting manual 0.77 cm** (F(2,98) = 39.09, P < .00001). This is the only head-to-head pec minor stretch comparison and measured acute length, not posture over time. — [JSES](https://www.jshoulderelbow.org/article/S1058-2746(05)00267-3/abstract); [Reinold summary](https://mikereinold.com/pectoralis-minor-stretch/)
- **Pectoralis major** stretching was part of Harman 2005; **neck extensor** stretching was also used. — [PEDro](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/13467)
- **"Tight" muscles targeted by UCS stretching:** pectoralis major/minor, upper trapezius, levator scapulae; SCM and suboccipitals are commonly added. **"Weak" targets:** deep neck flexors, serratus anterior, rhomboids, middle/lower trapezius. — [Physiopedia](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Upper-Crossed_Syndrome)

### Inferences
- For a 3D model, the most defensible "hot" muscles per exercise are those with EMG or ultrasound data:
  - longus colli for CCF
  - lower trapezius for prone Y
  - middle trapezius for prone T with ER
  - serratus anterior for push-up plus / wall slide
  - infraspinatus/teres minor + LT for side-lying ER
  - pectoralis minor (stretched) for the corner stretch

  Secondary highlights (rhomboids, erector spinae, suboccipitals) should be shown at lower intensity, since they rest on anatomy rather than measurement.
- Since none of the Cools exercises balances UT/serratus, the program likely needs a dedicated serratus exercise (push-up plus or wall slide) alongside the trapezius exercises.

### Gaps
- No EMG data retrieved for the back-to-wall "wall angel," prone "W," prone cobra or seated thoracic extension.
- %MVIC values for Ekstrom 2003 and Cools 2007 not verified from full texts.
- Suboccipital stretching during chin tucks is asserted by clinical and blog sources only. No measurement study was found.

## 6. Equipment-free variants and best positions

### Takeaway
Every core exercise has an equipment-free version (floor, chair, wall, doorway). The only position-specific evidence is for DNF: **supine and sitting CCF activate longus colli equally**, and sitting with self-applied oblique resistance (own hand) is strongest. For pec minor, the **unilateral corner/doorway self-stretch** outperformed partner stretches. Prone Y/T and side-lying ER need no equipment and have the best trapezius selectivity data. Wall slides give serratus activation comparable to push-up plus at 90°.

### Cited Findings
- Supine vs sitting CCF: equal longus colli activation (p = 0.758). Ipsilateral oblique resistance in sitting was most effective; straight lateral force drew SCM. — [PubMed 39862671](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39862671/)
- Supine flexion against head weight produced marked synchronous longus colli and SCM activity in deep-electrode work. In surface EMG, supine cervical flexion recruited all measured flexors earlier and longer than other positions (repetitive flexion, not isometric CCF). — [PMC9316545](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9316545)
- Unilateral corner/doorway self-stretch (2.24 cm) > supine manual (1.69 cm) > sitting manual (0.77 cm) for pec minor length. — [Borstad & Ludewig 2006](https://www.jshoulderelbow.org/article/S1058-2746(05)00267-3/abstract)
- Prone horizontal abduction with ER, prone extension, side-lying ER and side-lying forward flexion (all doable with body weight or a water bottle) gave the best LT/MT-to-UT balance. — [Cools 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17606671/)
- Wall slide ≈ wall push-up plus ≈ scaption for serratus anterior at 90°; the floor push-up plus is described as giving greater serratus activation than the wall version (secondary). — [Hardwick 2006](https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2006.2306); [Brookbush](https://brookbushinstitute.com/articles/increased-serratus-anterior-activation-during-wall-slides-and-scaption)
- Overhead wall push-up-plus positions may place the scapula in a position potentially associated with impingement. — [PMC8430934](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8430934/)
- Successful trials were largely equipment-free or minimal-equipment:
  - Harman 2005: home program
  - Ruivo 2016/2017: within school PE classes
  - Titcomb 2023: self-myofascial release + stretching + strengthening
  — [PEDro](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/13467); [e-JER review](https://www.e-jer.org/journal/view.php?number=2013600779); [Titcomb](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37649869/)

### Inferences
- A suggested equipment-free sequence for an app session of about 10–15 min, synthesized from the trial components and not a tested protocol:
  1. Supine head nod (CCF) holds
  2. Seated or wall chin tuck, progressing to seated tuck with own-hand oblique resistance
  3. Prone Y and prone T (thumbs up)
  4. Side-lying ER
  5. Wall slide or push-up plus for serratus
  6. Unilateral doorway pec stretch
  7. Seated thoracic extension over the chair back
  8. Optional upper trapezius / levator / SCM stretches
  9. Walking posture cue practice
- For a 190 cm/90 kg athletic man, prone Y/T with body weight may become easy quickly. Progression (longer holds, slower tempo, light household weights) is needed to stay in a strengthening stimulus. This is inference; no trial in athletes defines progression.
- Wall chin tucks ("back of head to wall") are practical for cueing, but standing against a wall can let the user cheat with lumbar or thoracic extension. Supine CCF is the cleanest teaching position; seated variants transfer to daily posture. Both are justified by the equal longus colli activation finding.

### Gaps
- No evidence comparing wall-based vs floor-based chin tucks on CVA outcomes.
- No EMG or outcome data for the seated thoracic extension over a chair back or for the back-to-wall wall angel specifically.
- No studies on optimal hold times or repetitions for posture change (as opposed to pain) were retrieved.
