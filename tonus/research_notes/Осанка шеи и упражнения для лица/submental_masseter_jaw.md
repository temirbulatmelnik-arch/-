# Submental fullness, suprahyoid/tongue training, masseter hypertrophy and cheekbones: exercise and at-home methods, evidence and risks

## 1. Submental fullness (double chin): causes, measurement, and what changes it (spot reduction, weight loss, posture)

### Takeaway
A double chin has several causes: pre-platysmal (subcutaneous) fat, post-platysmal (deep) fat, a low hyoid, a retruded chin or short mandible, enlarged submandibular glands, skin laxity, and head and neck posture. Exercise cannot remove fat from the chin in particular (spot reduction fails in controlled trials), so the levers a lean young man actually controls are overall body fat, head posture at rest and in photos, and possibly a small amount of muscle tone, which has not been tested for appearance. Fat in the submental area responds to drug treatment (deoxycholic acid) and to surgery, not to chin exercises.

### Cited Findings
**Anatomy and causes**
- Patients with submental fullness present with a blunted cervicomental angle, often greater than 120°, and a loss of jawline and mandibular-angle contour. These features "can be due to other anatomic concerns such as a low hyoid position or enlarged submandibular glands" as well as fat — [JDD: Neck Contouring and Treatment of Submental Adiposity](https://jddonline.com/articles/dermatology/S1545961616P0054X)
- The pre-platysmal fat pad lies superficial to the platysma and is the usual target of minimally invasive treatment. No clinically significant vessels or nerves lie superficial to the platysma, but the marginal mandibular branch of the facial nerve must be considered — [JDD review](https://jddonline.com/articles/dermatology/S1545961616P0054X)
- A study of a Korean population: "Even in cases without Type III platysma, a wide muscle bifurcation can facilitate the protrusion of deep fat layers, resulting in double chin deformity." The authors group causes into congenital (underdeveloped mandible, "turkey neck" look), iatrogenic (after two-jaw surgery or genioplasty) and excess submental fat — [Yonsei repository PDF](https://ir.ymlib.yonsei.ac.kr/bitstream/22282913/208407/1/88930.pdf)
- A chin that projects too little shortens the anterior–posterior length of the mandible from the most prominent chin point to the cervical point. This concentrates soft tissue, including fat, and contributes to submental fullness. The ideal mentocervical angle is cited as 80–95° — [PMC12163971](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12163971/). Caveat: the search tool returned this source in a set with the Yonsei PDF, so check which of the two holds this passage.

**Measurement**
- Cervicomental angle (CMA): one 3D analysis defines it with the hyoid, the mandibular gnathion and the sternal notch and gives an ideal range of 105–120°. The angle is usually measured from profile photos or 3D scans — [J Clin Med 2023;12(3):1226 (MDPI)](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0383/12/3/1226)
- For forward-head-posture photos, a systematic review of 21 studies found the best reliability evidence for classic photogrammetry (craniovertebral angle, CVA), but validity evidence is not conclusive. Lateral X-ray remains the reference method — [Reliability and validity of non-radiographic FHP measurement, systematic review (PMC9354067)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9354067/)
- CVA separated moderate-to-severe forward head posture from normal better than head-tilt or head-position angles. Measurement errors occur when photos are taken from one side only. One protocol placed the camera about 5 ft away at shoulder height, with the subject barefoot in anatomical posture — [Int J Physiother 2016 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/d312c9af36034608a516ada2f9bad42e); [J Back Musculoskelet Rehabil, photogrammetry in females](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3233/BMR-130426)

**Spot reduction does not work (landmark RCTs)**
- Vispute SS et al., *J Strength Cond Res* 2011;25(9):2559-64 (RCT, n=24 healthy sedentary adults). The exercise group did 7 abdominal exercises, 2×10 reps, 5 days/week for 6 weeks on an isocaloric diet. There was no significant effect on body weight, body-fat %, android fat %, android fat, abdominal circumference, or abdominal and suprailiac skinfolds. Abdominal muscular endurance did improve — [summary of the abstract (nutrition-evidence.com)](https://nutrition-evidence.com/article/161088/the-effect-of-abdominal-exercise-on-abdominal-fat); [Wikipedia: Abdominal exercise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdominal_exercise)
- Ramírez-Campillo R et al., *J Strength Cond Res* 2013;27(8):2219-24 (within-subject design, n=11: 7 men and 4 women, about 23 years old). The non-dominant leg trained for 12 weeks, 3 sessions/week, 1 set of 960–1,200 leg-press reps at 10–30% 1RM. Total fat mass fell 5.1%, but the trained leg showed no significant fat change. Fat fell in the upper extremities (−10.2%) and the trunk (−6.9%), more than in the trained leg — [UNAB repository](https://repositorio.unab.cl/xmlui/handle/ria/53433); [UNAVARRA record](https://observatorio-investigacion.unavarra.es/documentos/5f8e8e832999527b469fece3?lang=en). A 2021 review on spot reduction exists — [Human Movement 2021 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/a899e329b2554b45b1e815fae25c8ddf)
- A dermatology review notes the submental area is "many-a-time resistant to improvement with diet and exercise" — [Cosmoderma: Tackling submental fat](https://cosmoderma.org/tackling-submental-fat-a-review-of-management-strategies/)

**Weight / facial fat**
- 3D face study (Re et al. 2013): observers noticed changes in facial weight at about 1.3 kg/m² BMI in men's faces and 1.6 kg/m² in women's. The authors propose "facial weight" as a better term than "facial adiposity" — [Aberystwyth research portal](https://research.aber.ac.uk/en/publications/wie-viel-gewicht-liegt-im-gesicht/); [St Andrews portal](https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/publications/wie-viel-gewicht-liegt-im-gesicht-wahrnehmungsschwellen-f%C3%BCr-k%C3%B6rpe/)
- 3D geometric facial measurements correlate strongly with weight, BMI and neck circumference — [Lancashire repository](https://knowledge.lancashire.ac.uk/id/eprint/15388/)
- How individual facial fat compartments change after massive (bariatric) weight loss "remains unclear" — [NCN (Poland) project description](https://projekty.ncn.gov.pl/opisy/532814-en.pdf). A registered trial is measuring cheek and jowl changes after GLP-1 weight loss with 3D photogrammetry — [NCT07419854](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07419854)

**Head posture and hyoid position (does forward head posture worsen the double chin?)**
- Tallgren & Solow (Acta Odontol Scand, long-term denture wearers): hyoid position followed two postural systems. Vertical hyoid changes tracked mandibular inclination, and horizontal changes tracked cervical inclination and craniocervical angulation — [Acta Odontologica Scandinavica](https://medicaljournalssweden.se/actaodontologica/article/view/38304)
- In 36 adult women, the craniovertebral angle correlated significantly with the hyoid–mentum distance (p=0.02) and the hyoid–mandible distance (p=0.03) — [Braz J Oral Sci 2015 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/5f9bb335a92944f4ad2f17d7968d9e91); [UNICAMP PDF](https://periodicos.sbu.unicamp.br/ojs/index.php/bjos/article/download/8641445/15023/24312)
- Conflicting null result: a 2005 study of 50 young adults grouped by craniovertebral angle found no significant association between head posture and anteroposterior or vertical hyoid position — [Universidad de Chile repository](https://repositorio.uchile.cl/handle/2250/158916)

**Medical reference only (not at-home)**
- ATX-101 (deoxycholic acid), REFINE-2 phase 3 RCT: 2 mg/cm², up to 6 sessions about 28 days apart. At 12 weeks after the last treatment, a ≥1-grade composite improvement occurred in 66.5% vs 22.2% (placebo), and a ≥2-grade improvement in 18.6% vs 3.0%. MRI-confirmed submental volume reduction. Injection-site adverse events: 85.7% vs 76.9% — [PubMed 27430612](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27430612/); [REFINE-1 registry NCT01542034](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01542034)
- Surgical correction of an obtuse CMA, such as submental muscular medialization and suspension, exists — [Aesthet Surg J 2013;33(7):953](https://academic.oup.com/asj/article/33/7/953/2801339). A necklift abstract notes that success depends more on skin laxity than on how severe the CMA is — [ASPS 2018 abstract](https://asps.confex.com/asps/2018am/webprogram/Paper35730.html)

### Inferences
- For a 190 cm / 90 kg athletic man, the size of the submental fat pad mostly tracks whole-body fat. The app should frame "double chin" goals around body-fat trends and posture and say plainly that chin exercises do not burn chin fat (Vispute and Ramírez-Campillo).
- Because hyoid position and the hyoid–mentum distance appear linked to craniocervical posture (most but not all studies), forward head posture plausibly makes the submental contour look worse at rest and in photos. This is anatomically plausible, and the posture/hyoid cephalometric data support it, but no study measured "double chin appearance" against posture.
- A standardized progress photo protocol is the most defensible app metric: profile, Frankfort horizontal, fixed camera distance and height, neutral jaw, same lighting, measuring CMA (and CVA for posture). Head posture, camera angle and lighting can change CMA by far more than any training effect.
- Retrognathia, a low hyoid and large submandibular glands are structural. Exercise cannot change them, and the app should not promise it can.

### Gaps
- No controlled study was found that measures CMA or submental appearance after any exercise program (suprahyoid, tongue or platysma training) in any population.
- No MRI study of submental fat-volume change with diet-induced weight loss was found.
- The classic CMA ideal (often cited as Ellenbogen & Karlin, Plast Reconstr Surg 1980) was not retrieved in this session.
- No quantitative study links forward head posture to measured CMA or rated double-chin severity.

## 2. Suprahyoid and tongue muscle training: protocols, strength gains, hypertrophy, and any effect on submental contour

### Takeaway
Suprahyoid and tongue resistance training (Shaker head lift, CTAR, JOAR, IOPI tongue presses) reliably increases strength and activation within 4–8 weeks. Most of the data come from older adults and dysphagia patients, plus a few small healthy-adult samples. Hypertrophy evidence is thin: one MRI study found suprahyoid volume rose after 6 weeks of CTAR+JOAR+effortful swallow in 10 healthy seniors, and lingual volume rose about 5.1% in 4 older adults. No study shows a change in submental appearance or cervicomental angle. For an app, seated CTAR is the most muscle-specific option and loads the sternocleidomastoid (SCM) less than the Shaker head lift.

### Cited Findings
**Shaker head-lift exercise (HLE)**
- Protocol: lying supine, three sustained 60-s head lifts with 60-s rest between them, then 30 consecutive non-sustained head lifts. The head is raised high and far enough forward to see the toes without lifting the shoulders. Done 3×/day for 6 weeks. Patients should work up gradually to the goals according to their fatigue — [PMC6802897](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6802897); [PMC4811359](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4811359); [MCW Shaker exercise page](https://www.mcw.edu/departments/medicine/divisions/gastroenterology-and-hepatology/patient-care/shaker-exercise)
- Shaker et al. 2002 (Gastroenterology): after 6 weeks, 11 tube-fed older adults with dysphagia from abnormal UES opening resumed oral feeding. Videofluoroscopy showed significant increases in the anteroposterior diameter of the UES opening and in maximum anterior laryngeal excursion. The exercise also increased these measures in healthy older adults (Shaker 1997) — summarized in [PMC links above](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5610851) and [Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/978-1-4614-3779-6_13)
- Logemann et al., 7-site RCT (n=19, UES dysphagia): aspiration was significantly lower after Shaker therapy than after traditional therapy, with no difference in residue — [NCT00059670](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00059670)
- Fatigue and tolerability: during the isometric hold the suprahyoid, infrahyoid and SCM all fatigue, with the SCM fatiguing first and as fast as or faster than the hyoid muscles. Fatigue changes already appear in a 20-s hold (comparison of 20/40/60-s holds) — [Dysphagia 2008, doi 10.1007/s00455-008-9155-2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007%2Fs00455-008-9155-2). Patients have reported back and neck pain, dizziness and fatigue, and Easterling et al. found high dropout from HLE even in healthy subjects — [Sci Rep 2020 (s41598-020-68738-0)](https://link.springer.com/10.1038/s41598-020-68738-0); [PMC7366651](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7366651)
- Small RCT in healthy adults (n=24, 2 weeks, no dropouts): cervical flexor strength rose significantly only in the repetitive peripheral magnetic stimulation group, not the head-lift group — [Fujita Health University record](https://pure.fujita-hu.ac.jp/en/publications/repetitive-peripheral-magnetic-stimulation-for-strengthening-of-t/)

**Chin Tuck Against Resistance (CTAR)**
- Yoon WL et al. 2014, *Dysphagia* 29:243-8, "CTAR: new method for enhancing suprahyoid muscle activity using a Shaker-type exercise." Seated chin tuck compressing an inflatable rubber ball, compared with supine Shaker, sEMG, isometric and isokinetic tasks — [Springer Medicine](https://www.springermedicine.com/chin-tuck-against-resistance-ctar-new-method-for-enhancing-supra/21129036)
- Sze et al. 2016 (*Dysphagia*, 39 healthy adults): CTAR targeted the suprahyoid muscles more specifically than Shaker, and SCM activation was significantly lower during CTAR — [Springer Medizin](https://www.springermedizin.de/evaluating-the-training-effects-of-two-swallowing-rehabilitation/8525874) (cited via search summary)
- Kilinc et al., *Dysphagia* 2020;35(4):717-724 (36 healthy adults aged 18–40, 8 weeks). CTAR and chin tuck with a Theraband increased maximum suprahyoid sEMG activation, and Shaker did not. Dysphagia limits did not differ between groups — [Hacettepe AVESIS](https://avesis.hacettepe.edu.tr/yayin/cb6f0f09-2eaf-47bc-9baa-8a93733dbbb2/the-effects-of-different-exercise-trainings-on-suprahyoid-muscle-activation-tongue-pressure-force-and-dysphagia-limit-in-healthy-subjects); [doi 10.1007/s00455-019-10079-w](https://dx.doi.org/10.1007/s00455-019-10079-w)
- A three-arm acute comparison in 42 healthy subjects favored CTAR over Shaker (p<0.001) and over Theraband (p=0.040) for suprahyoid activation — [Hacettepe open access](https://openaccess.hacettepe.edu.tr/handle/11655/24680)
- Stroke meta-analysis (*Front Neurol* 2022): CTAR was better than Shaker for swallowing safety (MD −0.49), but the authors call for more high-quality RCTs — [Frontiers PDF](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurology/articles/10.3389/fneur.2022.1109140/pdf)
- Resistance can be an inflatable ball or a plastic bar compressed between the chin and the manubrium. The "submandibular push" exercise moves the hyoid without neck flexion — [PMC7366651](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7366651)
- Hypertrophy (healthy, preliminary): progressive CTAR, 15 adults aged 21–55, 5 days/week, ultrasound geniohyoid cross-sectional area. CSA rose from baseline to week 4 and then declined by week 6. This is an unreviewed undergraduate poster — [UWM Undergraduate Research Symposium 2025](https://sites.uwm.edu/ug-research-symposium-25/?p=4679)
- A multidirectional-CTAR RCT in 34 older adults measured tongue pressure, suprahyoid activity and muscle thickness; results were not retrieved — [Sci Rep 2024 (PMC11615205)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11615205); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/719d44cae2a7453fae2d90d0e1e970a2). A National Taiwan University Hospital trial is measuring suprahyoid CSA by ultrasound across 10 CTAR sessions over 2 weeks and has no published results — [trial listing](https://decentrialz.com/clinical-trials/effect-direct-chin-tuck-against-resistance-07479680)

**Combined CTAR + JOAR + effortful swallow (Swallow Exercise Aid), with MRI**
- Kraaijenga SA et al., *Dysphagia* 2015;30(4):392-403, doi 10.1007/s00455-015-9611-8. Prospective effectiveness and feasibility study in 10 healthy senior volunteers doing CTAR, JOAR and effortful swallows with the Swallow Exercise Aid, 3×/day for 6 weeks. Mean chin tuck strength, jaw opening strength, anterior tongue strength, **suprahyoid muscle volume (MRI)** and maximum mouth opening all rose significantly (p<.05). Median adherence was 86% (range 48–100%) — [UvA DARE](https://dare.uva.nl/id/15093d06-2287-4238-a1b0-378fa402bd91)
- MRI T2 mapping (3 volunteers): Swallow Exercise Aid exercises raised T2 signal in the suprahyoid, infrahyoid, sternocleidomastoid and lateral pterygoid muscles, which is useful for deciding which muscles the 3D model highlights — [Amsterdam UAS record](https://www.amsterdamuas.com/subsites/en/urban-vitality/publications/pure-import/mri-assessment-of-swallow-muscle-activation-with-the-swallow-exercise-aid-and-with-conventional-exercises-in-healthy-volunteers-an-explorative-biomechanical-study.html)
- In 20 laryngectomized patients using SEA 2.0, CTAR strength rose 27.4 N and JOAR strength 20.1 N — [Dysphagia 2024, s00455-024-10673-7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00455-024-10673-7)

**Jaw Opening Against Resistance / jaw-opening exercise**
- Wada S et al., *Arch Phys Med Rehabil* 2012;93(11):1995-9 (n=8 dysphagia patients, 7 men, 70.5±11.3 years). Open the jaw maximally and hold 10 s, 5 reps/set, 2 sets/day, for 4 weeks (a secondary source adds 10-s rest between holds). Upward hyoid movement, UES opening and pharyngeal transit time all improved significantly — [PubMed 22579648](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22579648/); [dysphagiacafe summary](https://dysphagiacafe.com/?p=423)
- A high-speed jaw-opening variant has been studied for suprahyoid fast-twitch fibers — [Clin Interv Aging 2018 (Dovepress)](https://www.dovepress.com/high-speed-jaw-opening-exercise-in-training-suprahyoid-fast-twitch-mus-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-CIA)

**Tongue strength training (IOPI / tongue-to-palate isometric presses)**
- Robbins J et al., *J Am Geriatr Soc* 2005;53(9):1483-9 (prospective cohort, 10 healthy adults aged 70–89). 8 weeks of compressing an air-filled bulb between tongue and hard palate. Isometric and swallowing pressures rose significantly in all subjects. In the 4 subjects with oral MRI, **lingual volume rose by an average of 5.1%** — [UMN Experts](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/the-effects-of-lingual-exercise-on-swallowing-in-older-adults/); [NCT02322411 protocol background](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/11/NCT02322411/Prot_SAP_000.pdf)
- Oh JC, *Dysphagia* 2015 (10 healthy young adults aged 21–35). IOPI training 8 weeks, 3 days/week, 30-min sessions, then 28 weeks of detraining. After detraining all tongue variables were significantly below post-training but still significantly above pre-training. The author concludes maintenance programs are needed — [Springer Medizin](https://www.springermedizin.de/effects-of-tongue-strength-training-and-detraining-on-tongue-pre/8525710)
- Yano et al. 2019: maximum tongue pressure rose significantly after 8 weeks and showed no significant drop 1–3 months after training ended — [UT Dallas PDF](https://labs.utdallas.edu/speech-production-lab/files/2021/06/Yano-et-al-2019.pdf)
- Van den Steen et al. (Antwerp, 16 healthy older adults, 8 weeks IOPI): anterior and posterior tongue pressures rose significantly, with detraining tested 4 weeks after the last session — [UAntwerp repository](https://repository.uantwerpen.be/link/irua/151476)
- A registered trial compares high-volume and high-intensity tongue training in healthy older adults — [ISRCTN98899535](https://www.isrctn.com/ISRCTN98899535)

### Inferences
- **Equipment-free variants**: CTAR with a rolled towel or a fist under the chin is a reasonable stand-in for the ball. The literature supports the ball or bar and Theraband variants, but a fist variant was not found in a published study. The tongue-to-palate press without IOPI is the same motion as the Robbins and Oh protocols but cannot be quantified or progressed objectively. Use RPE or hold time as the progression variable.
- For a strong young man the suprahyoids will probably gain strength fast (4–8 weeks, by analogy with the elderly and young-adult data). Hypertrophy is likely small (single-digit %, by analogy with Robbins' 5.1% lingual volume) and may plateau or regress (UWM poster). The suprahyoid muscles are thin and lie deep to the platysma and submental fat, so any visible contour change is speculative. The app should present these as "tone/strength and posture" exercises, not fat or double-chin removal.
- Prefer CTAR (seated, SCM-sparing) over Shaker HLE for consumer use. HLE loads the SCM, fatigues it first, and causes neck and back pain, dizziness and dropout even in healthy people.
- Detraining data (Oh 2015) mean the app needs a maintenance phase (for example 1–3 sessions/week) after the initial 6–8-week block.

### Gaps
- The exact % or cm³ increase in suprahyoid volume in Kraaijenga 2015 and the % strength gains were not retrievable (full text inaccessible).
- Yoon 2014's own numerical sEMG results and Park 2018 (CTAR vs Shaker in stroke) details were not retrieved.
- No study reports a change in submental appearance or cervicomental angle after suprahyoid or tongue training, in any population.
- No published study of a "fist CTAR" or of effortful swallow or Mendelsohn as standalone strength builders in healthy young men was found.
- A 4-week study reporting tongue-strength gains of about 9–10% (G1 10.3±10.4%, G2 8.7±9.45%, G3 9.9±12.6%) appeared in search results, but its source could not be confirmed.

## 3. Tongue-to-palate posture ("mewing") and isometric tongue presses: evidence vs hype

### Takeaway
Measured tongue-to-palate isometric resistance training builds tongue strength (section 2). "Mewing", a constant tongue posture said to reshape the adult jaw or chin, has no controlled human trial behind it. Adult bone reshaping by tongue posture is implausible because the midpalatal suture fuses in early adulthood, and forceful "hard mewing" may strain the TMJ and tip the incisors. All the mewing-specific sources found are low quality (clinic blogs).

### Cited Findings
- No randomized trial, cohort study or standardized imaging series has tested whether mewing changes jaw shape. The claims rest on anecdotes and before/after photos. Age (15–22 years), weight change, camera angle and lighting explain most before/after "transformations." The midline palatal suture usually fuses in early adulthood, so adult palatal expansion needs surgery or bone-anchored appliances — [Acibadem International (clinic blog)](https://acibademinternational.com/blog/mewing-what-orthodontists-say-about-tongue-posture-jawlines-and-the-evidence/)
- Clinicians link forceful pressing ("hard mewing") to jaw-joint soreness and unwanted forward tipping of the front teeth. Supervised myofunctional therapy has low-to-moderate evidence for reducing sleep apnea severity and stabilizing open-bite correction, which is a different use from jawline shaping — [Acibadem International](https://acibademinternational.com/blog/mewing-what-orthodontists-say-about-tongue-posture-jawlines-and-the-evidence/); [Dr Rachel Ho clinic blog](https://www.drrachelho.com/blog/mewing-jawline-slimming-review-singapore/)

### Inferences
- An app can include gentle tongue-to-palate rest posture (light contact, lips closed, teeth slightly apart, nasal breathing) as a posture cue, and tongue-press isometrics as strength work. It should label any claim about jaw reshaping as unproven and forbid "hard mewing" (sustained maximal pressing, clenching).
- The teeth-apart cue also matters for masseter safety (section 5): sustained clenching is the opposite of the TMD-friendly rest position.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed orthodontic study of mewing's effect on adult facial morphology was found. The negative conclusion rests on the absence of evidence plus anatomical reasoning from clinic sources.
- No study of tongue posture effects on submental muscle tone or appearance was found.

## 4. Masseter/masticatory hypertrophy through chewing or bite training: does it work, how fast, how much, is it reversible?

### Takeaway
Chewing and clenching training reliably increases maximum bite force within about 4 weeks to 3 months. Masseter thickness gains are documented mainly in older adults, including frail, edentulous and sarcopenic people. The best RCT in healthy adults (Jung et al. 2024, n=58, gum 3×/day for 6 months) found **no significant change in ultrasound masseter thickness or mandibular shape** despite higher bite force. For a healthy athletic young man, a visible squarer jaw from gum chewing is not supported by current evidence. Strength gains are likely and hypertrophy is uncertain and probably small. No detraining data exist for masseter size after training (botox data show the muscle shrinks with disuse; see section 6).

### Cited Findings
**Healthy young and adult populations**
- Jung et al. 2024, *J Oral Rehabil*, doi 10.1111/joor.13830, PMID 39215439 (RCT at Yonsei University, January–September 2020, 58 participants randomized). Gum chewing 3×/day for 6 months versus no training. Maximum occlusal force at 3 months was significantly above baseline and above controls (p<.001). Occlusal contact area rose as force rose (p=.020). **There was no statistically significant difference in masseter muscle thickness or mandibular shape compared with baseline** — [Yonsei repository](https://ir.ymlib.yonsei.ac.kr/handle/22282913/200840); [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/383604209_Effects_of_gum_chewing_training_on_occlusal_force_masseter_muscle_thickness_and_mandibular_shape_A_randomised_controlled_clinical_trial); [BVS / MEDLINE 39215439](https://busqueda.bvsalud.org/portal/resource/es/mdl-39215439)
- Kiliaridis S, Tzakis MG, Carlsson GE, *Am J Orthod Dentofacial Orthop* 1995;107:372-8, "Effects of fatigue and chewing training on maximal bite force and endurance". 4 weeks of training with hard chewing gum (isotonic) influenced masticatory muscle functional capacity and increased strength, but fatigue resistance did not rise — cited in [Shirai et al. 2018 (PMC5893462)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5893462/) and [Acta Odontol Scand download](https://medicaljournalssweden.se/actaodontologica/article/download/37666/42832/95392)
- Tzakis, Kiliaridis & Carlsson 1989 (*Acta Odontol Scand*), effect of chewing training on masticatory efficiency: 17 subjects chewed hard gum 1 h daily for 28 days, with 8 untrained controls — [Acta Odontol Scand PDF](https://medicaljournalssweden.se/actaodontologica/article/download/38892/44052/96908)
- Shirai et al. 2018: gum-chewing exercise improved masticatory performance, especially in people with dolichofacial (long-face) morphology — [PMC5893462](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5893462/)
- Ingervall & Bitsanis 1987 (*Eur J Orthod*, pilot study, long-face **children**): masticatory muscle training increased bite force and muscle activity, anterior facial height fell, posterior facial height rose, and the mandible rotated anteriorly in 9 of 12 cases. Growing children only, so not applicable to adult bone — [BORIS Bern](https://boris.unibe.ch/115382)
- Cross-sectional data: in 17 young adults masseter thickness was greater in men, as was bite force, but there was no stable linear thickness–force model. Another sample of 126 young adults (59 men, 25.1±4.8 years) was used to correlate thickness with maximal bite force — [SciELO Rev CEFAC](https://www.scielo.br/j/rcefac/a/TCgv4zNcfGBHPhzJnChmMLP/abstract/?lang=en); [HBO Kennisbank](https://hbo-kennisbank.nl/details/sharekit_fontys:oai:surfsharekit.nl:622facea-ca68-4dbc-9c53-3fb914240fc3)
- A 2024 CBCT study of 60 young adults found bite force correlated with sEMG, but masseter volume did not correlate with other parameters (limited CBCT soft-tissue imaging, small sample) — [Oral Radiology 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11282-024-00772-4)
- A registered 2026 trial is testing "Effects of Hard Gum Chewing on Facial Soft Tissue Morphology"; no results yet — [NCT07726511](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07726511)
- A study compares masseter activity across gum hardnesses with NIRS oximetry (title only retrieved) — [PubMed 39400845](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39400845/)
- A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis of masticatory training on masticatory function exists; results not retrieved — [J Prosthet Dent 2025](https://www.thejpd.org/article/S0022-3913(25)00653-5/abstract)

**Older adults (where thickness gains are reported)**
- Kim M-J et al., *J Oral Rehabil* 2020;47(9):1103-1109 (assessor-blind randomized trial, community-dwelling Koreans ≥65 years). The chewing-exercise group gained more occlusal force and masseter thickness than controls. PEDro score 6/10: assessors blinded, allocation not concealed — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/joor.13036); [PEDro record](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/62120)
- NMES + chewing RCT, *IJERPH* 2020;17(13):4902 (40 older adults, 35 analyzed). 20 min/day, 5 days/week, 6 weeks. Both chewing-alone and chewing+NMES groups significantly increased bite force and masseter thickness from baseline (p<0.05), with the NMES arm gaining more. Baseline masseter thickness was about 6.8–7.1 mm — [PMC7369815](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7369815/)
- Isometric clenching RCT (older adults with missing teeth): maximum clench 10 s, twice daily, 4 weeks. Maximum occlusal force and masseter thickness (contracted and resting) both improved — [PMC8012634](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8012634/)
- Kashiwazaki et al., *J Clin Med* 2023;12:6534 (RCT, 218 adults aged 65–85, 211 completed). Daily gum vs granular food for 2 months gave a significantly higher maximum bite force (p=0.01) with no difference in occlusal contact area — [PMC10607538](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10607538/)
- In older adults with sarcopenic dysphagia, gum chewing + NMES (8 weeks) beat gum alone for bite force and masseter thickness — [Park 2025, J Oral Rehabil](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/joor.14017). A gum-chewing pilot RCT in older adults also exists — [PMC11010614](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11010614/)
- A secondary source reports about a 9% thickness gain after a 6-week spring-resistance chewing device trial in adults 65+ (low reliability; verify the primary) — [Evexia Health](https://evexiahealth.org/masseter-muscle/)
- NMES alone vs placebo on masseter thickness in healthy ≥65 adults — [PMC7312302](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7312302/)

### Inferences
- **Speed**: bite-force (neural and strength) gains appear by 4 weeks (Kiliaridis 1995, clenching RCT) to 2–3 months (Kashiwazaki, Jung). Measurable thickness gains in older adults appear at 4–6 weeks of daily work. In healthy adults (Jung), even 6 months of gum gave no significant thickness change. That is consistent with a "detrained baseline" effect in the elderly (masseter about 7 mm) that is absent in young men, whose masseters are already used to daily chewing.
- **Magnitude**: no study found reports a mm masseter gain from training in healthy young men. The app should not promise visible jaw widening. If hypertrophy happens it is likely small compared with the roughly 1.9–2.2 mm facial contour change seen when botox *removes* a substantial share of masseter thickness (section 6).
- **Reversibility**: by analogy with all skeletal muscle and with botox disuse atrophy, any trained masseter gain is expected to regress after training stops. No direct masseter detraining study was found.
- Low-dose isometric clenching (10 s × 2/day) produced gains in older adults. For a TMD-risk-aware consumer app, short controlled isometrics with a bite pad may be gentler on the joint than hours of hard gum, though this has not been tested head-to-head.

### Gaps
- Mm or % masseter thickness changes for Kim 2020, the NMES trial and the clenching RCT were not retrieved, nor Kiliaridis 1995's % bite-force increase.
- No RCT was found of hard ("jawline") gums or jaw-exerciser devices in healthy young men measuring masseter size or facial width.
- No masseter detraining study was found.
- The "chewing training increases masseter thickness in young adults" claim (sometimes attributed to Kiliaridis) was not confirmed. Kiliaridis 1995 reported strength, not thickness.

## 5. Risks and contraindications of chewing, bite and jaw training (TMD, myalgia, bruxism, tooth wear, headache, asymmetry)

### Takeaway
Long daily gum chewing (hours) is associated with TMD symptoms (arthralgia, myofascial pain) and can trigger headaches in people prone to migraine or tension-type headache. The evidence is small and mostly cross-sectional, but the signal is consistent. Habitual one-sided chewing has conflicting evidence on facial asymmetry (a large cohort found no effect) but is linked to greater muscle activity and volume on the chewing side and to TMJ problems on that side. The Shaker head lift has its own neck-pain and dizziness profile (section 2). An app should cap chewing volume, enforce bilateral alternation and rest days, and stop on pain, clicking with pain, locking or headache.

### Cited Findings
- A critically appraised topic (UT Health San Antonio): longer gum-chewing duration is associated with worsening of existing TMD symptoms and new-onset symptoms. In the underlying cross-sectional study of people chewing 30, 60 or 120 min/day, gum chewers had significantly more TMD symptoms than non-chewing controls — [CATS UTHSCSA](https://cats.uthscsa.edu/published_cats_friendly.php?id=3006)
- In a single-centre study grouping chewers by daily hours, 63% of subjects chewing gum >3 hours at a time (Group D, n=8) reported TMD arthralgia and myofascial pain — [CATS UTHSCSA](https://cats.uthscsa.edu/found_cats_view.php?id=3006)
- Headache review (1 cross-sectional, 1 observational, 3 randomized studies, 3 case reports): gum chewing may trigger headache attacks in people with migraine and tension-type headache. No study reported more headache after gum chewing in non-migraineurs, and larger RCTs are needed — [IRIS Univ. Verona](https://iris.univr.it/handle/11562/927574)
- A paediatric report (Watemberg et al., Pediatr Neurology, as summarized by a blog): 19 of 30 children and teens with chronic headache were headache-free after a month without gum. Unverified secondary source — [Your Therapy Source blog](https://www.yourtherapysource.com/blog1/2013/12/20/gum-chewing-and-headaches/)
- A rapid review on chewing gum and TMJ disorders exists (findings not retrieved) — [Interdental J (Unmas)](https://e-journal.unmas.ac.id/index.php/interdental/article/view/6595)
- Chewing-side preference (Heikkinen et al., *Acta Odontol Scand* 2024, Northern Finland Birth Cohort 1986, n=748, 3D facial imaging): preference "does not have a statistically significant effect on facial asymmetry." Tooth loss on the non-chewing side predicted the preference, and chin asymmetry was associated with self-reported jaw pain. The study is cross-sectional — [PMC11423695](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11423695/)
- Conflicting: a literature review (14 studies, 2009–2021) reports that unilateral chewing goes with greater bite force, EMG activity and muscle volume on the chewing side, more mandibular deviation, and in some studies skeletal asymmetry — [Sinnun Maxillofacial J (UMI)](https://e-jurnal.fkg.umi.ac.id/index.php/sinnunmaxillofacial/article/download/63/37/288). Tiwari et al. 2017 (76 young adults): 98.6% had a preferred chewing side, which was "detrimental" to the TMJ on that side and related to lateral facial asymmetry — [J Orofac Sci 2017 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/0f8c6d3f4ff74f88b2b7304b53c03677)
- In TMD, masticatory muscle thickness by ultrasound differs between bruxers and non-bruxers — [Sci Rep 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-57696-6)
- Head-lift risks: neck and back pain, dizziness and fatigue, with high dropout even in healthy subjects — [PMC7366651](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7366651)

### Inferences
- **Contraindications and screening questions for the app** (clinical reasoning, not from a single trial): current TMD (jaw pain, painful clicking, locking, limited opening), recent jaw or dental surgery, orthodontic treatment in progress, many restorations or crowns or known tooth wear, bruxism, migraine or tension-type headache, cervical spine problems (for HLE/CTAR), dizziness. These users should skip chewing and bite progressions or get a dentist's clearance first.
- **Dose caps**: the TMD signal clusters at ≥30–120 min/day and especially >3 h per session. A conservative app ceiling of about 10–20 min/day of deliberate chewing (well below the 1 h/day used by Tzakis 1989) seems prudent. This is an inference from the dose groups, not a validated threshold.
- **Asymmetry mitigation**: alternate sides by time or by bolus, or chew bilaterally, and track symmetry in progress photos. The cohort data suggest habitual side preference does not reliably create visible facial asymmetry, but chewing-side muscle activity and volume differences are reported.
- **Stop rules**: stop the jaw program and see a dentist or physician for jaw or ear-area pain lasting more than 24–48 h, new or painful clicking, catching or locking, reduced mouth opening, morning jaw soreness (a possible sign of clenching or bruxism), new headaches, tooth sensitivity or cracks. For neck work, stop on dizziness, neck or back pain, or arm tingling.

### Gaps
- No prospective trial found measures TMD incidence under structured, time-capped chewing training in healthy young men.
- No data found on tooth wear or restoration fractures from hard "jawline" gums or silicone jaw exercisers. Only marketing pages were found, and they were excluded as unreliable.
- The Watemberg paediatric headache study was not verified against the journal.

## 6. Masseter (and temporalis) volume and perceived facial width or squareness

### Takeaway
Direct evidence that masseter volume determines how wide or square the lower face looks comes mostly from the reverse experiment. Botulinum toxin shrinks the masseter about 12–18% in resting thickness at 1 month and narrows the measured facial contour about 2 mm, which is the basis of "V-line" lower-face slimming in East Asian practice. So masseter bulk contributes to lower-face width, but the changes are millimetres. A wider face (fWHR) in men is linked to perceived dominance, with mixed effects on attractiveness. No study tested temporalis size against perceived face shape.

### Cited Findings
- Ultrasound trial of blind vs ultrasound-guided BoNT-A into the masseter: resting masseter thickness fell 12.38±7.59% (blind) vs 17.98±9.65% (US-guided) at 1 month, and facial contour fell 1.95±0.74 mm vs 2.22±0.84 mm. Baseline resting thickness was about 12–13 mm — [PMC7551286](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7551286/)
- Park MY et al. 2003 (*Dermatol Surg*), "Botulinum toxin type A treatment for contouring of the lower face": masseter thickness fell gradually over the first 3 months on ultrasound and CT — [PubMed 12752514](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12752514/)
- Yonsei thesis: BoNT injection changes muscle thickness but not the thickness from skin surface to masseter (the overlying soft tissue) — [Yonsei thesis PDF](https://ir.ymlib.yonsei.ac.kr/bitstream/22282913/148867/1/T013823.pdf)
- Ultrasound-guided single-point BoNT for lower-face contouring (50 patients): significant thickness reduction at 1 month — [PMC11396597](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11396597/)
- A BoNT masseter-hypertrophy trial protocol measured lower facial width from 3D photographic models, and notes that masseter volume has been measured by ultrasound, CT and MRI — [NCT02010775 protocol](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/75/NCT02010775/Prot_000.pdf)
- In hemifacial microsomia, masseter volume on the affected side correlated with ramus height (r=0.395, p=0.038) — [PMC11381231](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11381231/). A paediatric CT study cites strong correlations between masseter volume and mandibular morphology (Fujimoto 2022) — [ScienceDirect 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0940960226000737)
- Wider male faces (higher facial width-to-height ratio) are linked to perceived dominance and aggression. In one study women preferred wider-faced men for short-term relationships — [PsyBlog summary](https://www.spring.org.uk/?p=54336). In an objective-measurement study, more attractive female faces had less cheek fullness and more defined jaw contours; the male effect was similar in size but not significant — [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/objective-measurements-shed-light-on-the-geometry-of-facial-attractiveness/)

### Inferences
- If masseter atrophy of about 12–18% thickness gives about 2 mm of contour change, then a plausible training-induced hypertrophy of single-digit % in a young man (unproven; Jung 2024 found none) would change bigonial soft-tissue width by around 1 mm or less. That is probably below what a casual viewer notices. Lower body fat (section 1) probably changes jaw definition more than masseter hypertrophy does.
- The temporalis sits in the temporal fossa. Hypertrophy could slightly fill the temples and widen the upper-lateral face, but no data were found, so it should not be marketed as a shape lever.

### Gaps
- No study found tests masseter *hypertrophy* (as opposed to atrophy) against perceived squareness or attractiveness.
- No direct MRI or CT correlation of masseter volume with bigonial width in adult men was retrieved.

## 7. Cheekbones (zygomatic region): can they change without surgery?

### Takeaway
The zygomatic bone of an adult cannot be reshaped by exercise. No source found shows exercise-induced bone change in the adult midface, and age-related midface change is bone *resorption* plus fat loss and descent. How visible cheekbones are depends on soft tissue: overall facial fat or "facial weight", buccal fat, and the distribution of midface fat compartments. One small uncontrolled study (Alam 2018, middle-aged women) found facial exercises increased rated upper and lower cheek *fullness*, which for a lean man may add soft-tissue volume over the zygoma rather than "sharpen" it. Buccal fat removal, fillers and implants are medical options, listed for reference only.

### Cited Findings
- Alam M et al., *JAMA Dermatol* 2018;154(3):365-367 (uncontrolled pilot study, women aged 40–65, about 16 completers per secondary sources). Two 90-min training sessions, then 30 min of facial exercises daily for 8 weeks and every other day from week 9 to 20. Blinded dermatologists using the Merz–Carruthers scales rated upper and lower cheek fullness significantly better. Estimated age fell from 50.8 to 49.6 years (week 8) and 48.1 years (week 20). The participants were self-selected — [Northwestern News](https://news.northwestern.edu/stories/2018/january/facial-exercises-help-middle-aged-women-appear-more-youthful); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2018/01/180103160142.htm); [Practical Dermatology](https://practicaldermatology.com/news/daily-facial-exercises-help-women-to-look-three-years-younger-in-20-weeks/2457938)
- "Inside-out" facial aging: midface bone remodeling unbalances the upper, middle and lower facial thirds — [Aesthet Surg J, sjaa339](https://www.ovid.com/journals/aesj/pdf/10.1093/asj/sjaa339~the-facial-aging-process-from-the-inside-out); [PMC6250453](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6250453)
- Buccal fat pad excision for cheek or midface sculpting: only 2 published case series with more than 5 patients, and no published long-term follow-up on aging or late deformity. Removal in young people is controversial because fat lost with aging cannot easily be replaced — [Lily Talakoub / The Hospitalist](https://blogs.the-hospitalist.org/authors/lily-talakoub-md-0); [PMC6250453](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6250453)
- Facial weight is perceptible at about 1.3 kg/m² BMI change in male faces — [Re et al. 2013 (Aberystwyth)](https://research.aber.ac.uk/en/publications/wie-viel-gewicht-liegt-im-gesicht/)
- Adult facial bones have not been shown to be reshaped by tongue posture — [Acibadem International (clinic blog, low quality)](https://acibademinternational.com/blog/mewing-what-orthodontists-say-about-tongue-posture-jawlines-and-the-evidence/)

### Inferences
- For a 90 kg athletic man, the at-home levers for more visible cheekbones are lower body fat (facial weight is perceptible at small BMI changes), posture and photo angle (head slightly down or chin forward, lighting), and possibly hydration and sodium habits (not researched here). Exercise of the zygomaticus or buccinator is not shown to sharpen the zygomatic contour. Alam 2018 suggests it adds cheek fullness in middle-aged women, an outcome that may or may not match a "chiseled" goal.
- The app should state plainly that bone does not change with exercise and should not include "cheekbone exercises" with structural claims.

### Gaps
- No controlled trial of zygomaticus or buccinator training in young men was found, nor any ultrasound study of facial-muscle thickness after facial exercise.
- Alam 2018's exact sample size was not confirmed from the primary paper (secondary sources say 16).

## 8. Practical progression model for the app (weeks 1–12+), muscles for the 3D model, and stop rules

### Takeaway
A defensible 12-week program combines (1) seated CTAR and a gentle supine head lift for the suprahyoid muscles, (2) tongue-to-palate isometrics with gradual intensity progression, (3) capped, bilateral, low-to-moderate chewing or brief isometric bite work for the masseter, (4) neck-posture work, and (5) honest education that fat loss, not exercise, changes submental fat and cheekbone visibility. Volumes should be anchored to the studied protocols (Shaker: 3×60 s + 30 reps, 3×/day, 6 weeks; Wada JOAR: 10-s hold ×5 ×2/day, 4 weeks; Robbins/Oh IOPI: 8 weeks, 3 days/week; clenching RCT: 10-s maximal clench ×2/day, 4 weeks; chewing RCTs: 20 min/day, 5 days/week, 6 weeks). Start well below them, because those doses come from rehabilitation settings and some (HLE) have high dropout and neck pain.

### Cited Findings
- Studied doses: Shaker HLE 3×60-s holds with 60-s rest + 30 lifts, 3×/day, 6 weeks — [PMC6802897](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6802897); [MCW](https://www.mcw.edu/departments/medicine/divisions/gastroenterology-and-hepatology/patient-care/shaker-exercise). Fatigue sets in within 20-s holds — [Dysphagia 2008](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007%2Fs00455-008-9155-2)
- CTAR/JOAR/effortful swallow 3×/day for 6 weeks raised strength and suprahyoid volume in healthy seniors, with 86% median adherence — [Kraaijenga 2015 (UvA DARE)](https://dare.uva.nl/id/15093d06-2287-4238-a1b0-378fa402bd91)
- CTAR or Theraband chin tuck over 8 weeks raised maximum suprahyoid activation in healthy 18–40-year-olds, and Shaker did not — [Dysphagia 2020;35:717-724](https://avesis.hacettepe.edu.tr/yayin/cb6f0f09-2eaf-47bc-9baa-8a93733dbbb2/the-effects-of-different-exercise-trainings-on-suprahyoid-muscle-activation-tongue-pressure-force-and-dysphagia-limit-in-healthy-subjects)
- JOAR / jaw-opening: maximal opening 10-s hold, 5 reps, 2 sets/day, 4 weeks — [PubMed 22579648](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22579648/)
- Tongue IOPI: 8 weeks, 3 days/week, 30-min sessions in young adults, with gains partly retained 28 weeks later — [Oh 2015](https://www.springermedizin.de/effects-of-tongue-strength-training-and-detraining-on-tongue-pre/8525710). Robbins 2005 used progressive bulb compression for 8 weeks — [UMN](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/the-effects-of-lingual-exercise-on-swallowing-in-older-adults/)
- Masseter: maximal clench 10 s ×2/day for 4 weeks — [PMC8012634](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8012634/). Chewing 20 min/day, 5 days/week, 6 weeks — [PMC7369815](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7369815/). Gum 3×/day for 6 months raised force but not thickness in adults — [Jung 2024 (Yonsei)](https://ir.ymlib.yonsei.ac.kr/handle/22282913/200840). TMD symptoms cluster with 30–120 min/day and especially >3 h sessions — [CATS UTHSCSA](https://cats.uthscsa.edu/published_cats_friendly.php?id=3006)
- Muscles activated by these swallow-exercise tasks on MRI T2: suprahyoid, infrahyoid, sternocleidomastoid, lateral pterygoid — [Amsterdam UAS](https://www.amsterdamuas.com/subsites/en/urban-vitality/publications/pure-import/mri-assessment-of-swallow-muscle-activation-with-the-swallow-exercise-aid-and-with-conventional-exercises-in-healthy-volunteers-an-explorative-biomechanical-study.html). Shaker EMG fatigue: suprahyoid, infrahyoid, SCM — [Dysphagia 2008](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007%2Fs00455-008-9155-2)

### Inferences
**Proposed progression.** This synthesizes the studied doses, scaled down at the start for safety. It has not been validated as a whole. Assumes a healthy man with no TMD, neck or headache red flags.

| Phase | Weeks | Suprahyoid (CTAR seated: ball, towel roll or fist) | Head lift (supine, optional) | Tongue-to-palate press | Masseter (bilateral) | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 Learn | 1–2 | Isometric 3×10 s holds + 10 slow reps, RPE 5/10 | 3×10–15 s holds + 10 lifts | 5×5 s at about 50–60% effort, anterior tongue tip on the alveolar ridge | Normal sugar-free gum, 5 min/session, alternate sides every 30–60 s, 1 session/day | 1–2×/day, 5 days/week |
| L2 Build | 3–4 | 3×20 s holds + 15–20 reps | 3×20–30 s + 15 lifts | 10×5 s at 60–70% (Robbins-style progression) | 10 min/day (2×5 min) OR 5×5-s submaximal clench on a soft bite pad, teeth not grinding | 2×/day, 5 days/week |
| L3 Strength | 5–8 | 3×30–45 s + 20–30 reps; add band or harder ball | 3×45–60 s + 20–30 lifts (Shaker target only if no neck pain) | 10×10 s at 80% + 10 fast presses; add posterior-tongue presses | Up to 15–20 min/day firmer gum split into 2 sessions, OR 10-s near-maximal clench ×2/day (as in the PMC8012634 RCT), ≥2 rest days/week | 2–3×/day suprahyoid/tongue; jaw 5 days/week |
| L4 Maintain | 9–12+ | 3×60 s + 30 reps (study ceiling), 3–4 days/week | Optional | 3 days/week (Oh 2015 detraining shows the need to maintain) | Hold at ≤20 min/day or drop to 3 days/week; no daily "hard gum" marathons | 3–4 days/week |

- Add daily neck posture work (chin-tuck posture drills, screen-height cues) and progress photos every 2–4 weeks with standardized CMA and CVA measurement (section 1).
- **Stop or deload rules**: jaw pain or soreness lasting more than 24–48 h, new or painful TMJ clicking, catching or locking, reduced mouth opening, new headache, tooth pain, sensitivity or a cracked restoration, ear pain. For neck work: dizziness, neck or back pain, radiating arm symptoms. Resume one level lower after symptoms resolve, and see a dentist or physician if symptoms persist.
- **Expectation messaging**: strength gains in 4–8 weeks are likely. Visible masseter hypertrophy is unproven in healthy adults (Jung 2024). Exercise does not remove submental fat or reshape bone. Body-fat reduction and posture are the main appearance levers.

**Muscles for the 3D model** (standard anatomy; MRI and EMG papers above confirm activation of the starred groups):
- Suprahyoid*: anterior and posterior digastric, mylohyoid, geniohyoid, stylohyoid (CTAR, JOAR, head lift, effortful swallow)
- Infrahyoid*: sternohyoid, omohyoid, sternothyroid, thyrohyoid (co-activate during head lift and swallow exercises)
- Sternocleidomastoid* (dominant and fatiguing in Shaker; less in CTAR)
- Tongue: intrinsic muscles plus genioglossus, hyoglossus, styloglossus, palatoglossus (IOPI and palate presses)
- Masseter (superficial and deep), temporalis, medial pterygoid (jaw closing in chewing and clench); lateral pterygoid* (jaw opening and protrusion; T2-activated in Swallow Exercise Aid work)
- Platysma (superficial submental and neck sheet; its relation to pre- and post-platysmal fat explains why muscle work doesn't remove fat above it)
- Buccinator, zygomaticus major and minor (cheek exercise content; Alam 2018)

### Gaps
- No study has validated a combined consumer program like this, or any progression model, for appearance outcomes in healthy young men.
- No objective at-home measure of suprahyoid or tongue strength without devices (IOPI, dynamometer) was found. RPE, hold time and reps are the practical proxies.
- No evidence was found on the optimal gum hardness progression, or on a safe daily chewing ceiling for training as opposed to habitual chewing.
