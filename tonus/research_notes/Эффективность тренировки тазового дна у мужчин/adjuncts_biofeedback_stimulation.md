# Adjuncts and alternatives to plain pelvic floor / anal sphincter exercises: biofeedback, electrical and magnetic stimulation, rectal balloon training, consumer devices and apps

> Method note for the report writer: the page-fetch tool failed with DNS errors on every host tried (PubMed, PMC, Europe PMC, cochrane.org), so the numbers below come from search-engine retrievals of abstracts, registry entries and secondary summaries, not from full texts I read. Exact figures that come from abstracts are marked as such. Where a figure comes from a secondary summary or the sources disagree, I say so. Check effect sizes against the full papers before quoting them as definitive.
>
> Population key: **FI** = faecal incontinence patients; **OASI/postpartum** = women after obstetric anal sphincter injury; **PPI** = men with post-prostatectomy urinary incontinence; **ED** = men with erectile dysfunction; **healthy** = asymptomatic volunteers. Very few studies involve healthy men. Applying any of this to the "Тонус" user (a healthy, athletic man) is **extrapolation**.

## 1. Cochrane CD002111 and the key FI trials: exercise alone vs biofeedback vs electrical stimulation

### Takeaway
In people with faecal incontinence, the best-controlled trials show that most improvement comes from the non-specific package: education, advice, attention, and practising contractions. Biofeedback added to exercises beat exercises alone in one US RCT (Heymen 2009) but not in the largest UK RCT (Norton 2003). The Cochrane review (Norton & Cody 2012) calls the evidence too limited for firm conclusions. Electrical stimulation added to biofeedback has inconsistent, low-quality support.

### Cited Findings
**Cochrane CD002111 (Norton & Cody, 2012, Issue 7; DOI 10.1002/14651858.CD002111.pub3)** — systematic review of RCTs
- 21 eligible studies, 1,525 participants. Search last run 24 January 2012. Earlier versions: 2000 (original) and 2006 (pub2) — [Cochrane Library record](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD002111.pub2/information); [PubMed 22786479](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22786479/)
- Conclusion: the small number of trials and their methodological weaknesses "do not permit a definitive assessment." One trial found that biofeedback plus exercises beat exercises alone (RR for failing to achieve full continence 0.70, 95% CI 0.52–0.94). Exercises appear less effective than an implanted sacral nerve stimulator. Larger, well-designed trials are needed — [PubMed 22786479](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22786479/); [Cochrane abstract PDF](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD002111.pub3/pdf/CDSR/CD002111/CD002111_abstract.pdf)
- Version status: cochrane.org shows the review under "2022, Issue 3", but the Cochrane Library and PubMed records list the 2012 pub3 version as current. I found no substantively updated review (pub4) — [cochrane.org CD002111](https://www.cochrane.org/CD002111)

**Norton et al. 2003, Gastroenterology 125:1320–1329** — RCT, 4 arms, n=171 (landmark; UK, St Mark's)
- Arms: (1) standard care with advice; (2) advice plus sphincter exercises; (3) hospital computer-assisted biofeedback; (4) hospital biofeedback plus a home biofeedback device — [Gastroenterology full text](https://www.gastrojournal.org/article/S0016-5085(03)01368-4/fulltext)
- Improvement was similar across arms (e.g., 53% hospital biofeedback vs 54% advice only). Across the cohort, weekly incontinence episodes fell from a median of 2 to 0 (P<0.001) and the continence score (worst = 20) fell from a median of 11 to 8. 32 of 171 did not complete, with similar dropout across arms. Benefit was maintained at 1 year. Authors: "Neither pelvic floor exercises nor biofeedback was superior to standard care supplemented by advice and education" — [Gastroenterology](https://www.gastrojournal.org/article/S0016-5085(03)01368-4/fulltext); [Norton review, Gastroenterology 2004](https://www.gastrojournal.org/article/S0016-5085(03)01400-8/fulltext)

**Heymen et al. 2009, Dis Colon Rectum 52:1730–1737** — RCT with a run-in phase to control non-specific effects
- At run-in, 21% of patients improved enough with medication, education and behavioural strategies alone and were excluded. 108 were then analysed: pelvic floor exercises alone (n=63) vs manometric biofeedback plus exercises (n=45) — [PubMed 19966605](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19966605/); [DCR full text](https://journals.lww.com/dcrjournal/Fulltext/2009/10000/Randomized_Controlled_Trial_Shows_Biofeedback_to.8.aspx)
- At 3 months, 76% of biofeedback patients reported adequate relief vs 41% with exercises (χ²=12.5, P<0.001), and FISI fell more in the biofeedback arm (P=0.01). At 12 months the FISI advantage persisted, but the adequate-relief difference was borderline (P=0.056). Authors called this "definitive support" for biofeedback — [PubMed 19966605](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19966605/); [FIT Study protocol citing these data](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/21/NCT03811821/Prot_SAP_001.pdf)
- Conflicts: one secondary account gives 168 randomised (85 exercise vs 83 biofeedback), which does not match the abstract's 108 analysed. One review attributes the 76% vs 41% split to "supervised PFMT + sensitivity biofeedback vs non-supervised PFMT" — [PMC4174224 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4174224/). I treat the PubMed abstract as authoritative.
- A 2009 meta-analysis of 6 RCTs found an OR of 1.2 (0.7–2.1) for biofeedback vs other treatments — [Enck et al., Neurogastroenterol Motil 2009](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1365-2982.2009.01345.x)

**Ussing et al. 2019, Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol 17(11):2253–2261** — RCT, Denmark, supervised PFMT plus biofeedback vs attention-control (massage)
- Mean between-group differences in Vaizey score change were −1.83 to −2.24 points. The PFMT group had about fivefold higher odds of reporting improvement than controls. Authors recommend PFMT combined with conservative treatment as first-line — [PubMed 30580089](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30580089/); [CGH](https://www.cghjournal.org/article/S1542-3565(18)31391-0/fulltext); [Healio summary](https://www.healio.com/news/gastroenterology/20181228/pelvic-floor-muscle-training-with-biofeedback-helps-improve-symptoms-of-fecal-incontinence)
- This is the strongest sham/attention-controlled evidence that supervised training (with biofeedback) beats attention alone. It does not separate the biofeedback component from the exercise component.

**Bols et al. 2012, Neurourol Urodyn 31(1):132–138** — single-blind RCT, n=80, Maastricht: PFMT plus rectal balloon training (RBT) vs PFMT alone
- Primary Vaizey score: mean difference −1.19 (95% CI −3.79 to 1.42; P=0.37), not significant. RBT improved urgency control, global perceived effect and lifestyle adaptation — [Wiley abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/nau.21218); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51753279_Rectal_balloon_training_as_add-on_therapy_to_pelvic_floor_muscle_training_in_adults_with_fecal_incontinence_A_randomized_controlled_trial); [PhysioFIT design paper](https://bmcpublichealth.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1471-2458-7-355)

**Damon et al. 2014, Dig Liver Dis 46(3):237–242** — multicentre French RCT (Oralia group): standard conservative treatment vs that plus perineal retraining with biofeedback (adults with Wexner score >4)
- PEDro 5/10: no concealed allocation, no blinding, no ITT — [PEDro record](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/38608); [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT00292318](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00292318). Outcome numbers not retrieved (see Gaps).

**Solomon et al. 2003, Dis Colon Rectum 46(6):703–710** — 3-arm RCT in mild–moderate FI: biofeedback with anal manometry vs biofeedback with transanal ultrasound vs pelvic floor retraining with digital (finger) guidance alone. Outcomes: continence, QoL, sphincter strength, compliance — [PubMed 14598248 / Qigong Institute abstract](https://www.qigonginstitute.org/abstract-print/3728); [Bournemouth profile](https://staffprofiles.bournemouth.ac.uk/display/journal-article/45222). Results and n not retrieved.

**Electrical stimulation (ES) added to biofeedback/exercise**
- **Mahony et al. 2004, Am J Obstet Gynecol 191:885–890** — RCT in 60 women with early postpartum FI (54 completed; 96% had a sonographic external anal sphincter defect): intra-anal EMG biofeedback vs the same plus anal-sphincter ES, weekly for 12 weeks. Both arms improved continence score and squeeze pressure, with **no difference** between arms — [PubMed 15467559](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15467559/)
- **Fynes et al. 1999** — 40 women with obstetric FI: vaginal pressure biofeedback plus home exercises vs anal EMG biofeedback plus home exercises plus intra-anal ES. Cochrane: ES with anal biofeedback "provides more short-term benefits than vaginal biofeedback and exercises", but another study found "no added benefit from electrical stimulation over biofeedback and exercises alone". Allocation concealment was not reported — [Cochrane CD001310, PMC8406929](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8406929/); [PubMed 17636665](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17636665/)
- **Vonthein et al. 2013, Int J Colorectal Dis 28(11):1567–1577** — systematic review with meta-ANOVA of a 2×2 BF/ES design (endpoint: remission, measured as RR). "No trial showed superiority of control, or of BF alone or of ES alone when compared with BF + ES." Amplitude-modulated medium-frequency ES plus BF beat both low-frequency ES and BF alone. A secondary summary gives BF vs control RR 2.12 (95% CI 1.42–3.16) — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00384-013-1739-0); [PubMed 23900652](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23900652/?dopt=Abstract); [BCBS SC policy summarising it](https://provider.bcbssc.com/web/public/brands/medicalpolicy/external-policies/biofeedback-as-a-treatment-of-fecal-incontinence-or-constipation/)

**Guidelines**
- **ASCRS 2023** (Bordeianou et al., Dis Colon Rectum 2023;66(5):647–661): biofeedback "should be considered" for FI, as a conditional recommendation on low-quality evidence. It is a noninvasive option for patients who have not responded adequately to other conservative measures — [Guideline Central summary](https://www.guidelinecentral.com/guideline/9290/); [guideline PDF mirror](https://www.gamedii.com.br/docs/consensos/ascrs/2023-Fecal-Incontinence-CPG.pdf); [PA Health & Wellness policy quoting grade](https://www.pahealthwellness.com/content/dam/centene/Pennsylvania/policies/clinical-policies/PA.CP.MP.168%20Biofeedback__REV_DECEMBER_2024.pdf)
- **NICE NG210 (2021, women)**: consider biofeedback, ES or vaginal cones alongside PFMT for women who **cannot make an effective pelvic floor contraction** or who make little progress with supervised PFMT. The committee found the evidence on these add-ons inconsistent — [NG210 rationale](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng210/chapter/rationale-and-impact); [NG210 PDF](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng210/resources/pelvic-floor-dysfunction-prevention-and-nonsurgical-management-pdf-66143768482501)
- **NICE CG49 (FI, 2007)**: lists PFMT, bowel retraining, dietary management, biofeedback, ES and rectal irrigation as specialised options — [CG49 recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg49/chapter/Recommendations)
- **ANMS-ESNM position paper** (Rao et al., Neurogastroenterol Motil 2015, doi 10.1111/nmo.12520): biofeedback uses visual and verbal feedback and is safe. Its goals include sensory training — [PMC4409469](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4409469)

**Analogous evidence in women with urinary incontinence (Cochrane CD009252, Herderschee 2011)** — 24 trials, 1,583 women
- Women given biofeedback with PFMT were less likely to report no improvement (RR 0.75, 95% CI 0.66–0.86), but cure did not differ (RR 0.92, 0.81–1.05). The effect "could well be related to another variable, such as the amount of health professional contact" — [UroToday summary of shortened version](https://www.urotoday.com/recent-abstracts/pelvic-health-reconstruction/stress-incontinence/60214-feedback-or-biofeedback-to-augment-pelvic-floor-muscle-training-for-urinary-incontinence-in-women-shortened-version-of-a-cochrane-systematic-review-abstract.html); [PubMed 21735442](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21735442/)

### Inferences
- The Cochrane RR 0.70 "one trial" result most likely comes from Heymen 2009 (the only biofeedback-plus-exercise vs exercise RCT positive at that time), but the abstract snippet does not name it.
- The pattern across Norton 2003, Heymen 2009 (where 21% responded to the run-in alone) and Herderschee 2011 is consistent: much of the benefit comes from education, attention, contact and repeated practice. Feedback adds most when contraction quality is poor or symptoms are refractory.
- ES has no convincing additive effect in people who can voluntarily contract (Mahony 2004 null). Its positive signals come from mixed comparisons (Fynes) or specific waveforms in patients (amplitude-modulated medium-frequency stimulation).

### Gaps
- Results of Damon 2014 and Solomon 2003 (effect sizes, n) were not retrievable. From what was retrieved, I cannot confirm that Solomon found no difference between feedback modalities.
- Ussing 2019 sample size and exact odds ratio were not retrieved.
- No head-to-head trial in healthy people compares exercise alone vs exercise plus biofeedback.

## 2. Anal manometry: how much do resting and squeeze pressures change with exercise or biofeedback, and are there healthy-volunteer data?

### Takeaway
Squeeze pressure usually rises with supervised training (with or without biofeedback) in patients, but the change correlates poorly with symptoms and rarely differs between arms. There is essentially **no published RCT of anal-sphincter strength training with manometry in healthy men**. The closest data are a men's ED trial (PFMT plus manometric biofeedback raised anal pressure by about 44 cmH₂O vs control) and small physiology or EMG studies in healthy young adults.

### Cited Findings
- **Norton 2003 (FI, RCT n=171)**: among patients who had repeat manometry, resting and squeeze-increment pressures rose significantly, with **no between-group differences** after treatment. Baseline manometry did not predict outcome — [Gastroenterology](https://www.gastrojournal.org/article/S0016-5085(03)01368-4/fulltext)
- **Mahony 2004 (postpartum FI, RCT n=60)**: squeeze pressure improved in both the EMG-biofeedback arm and the biofeedback-plus-ES arm, with no difference between them — [PubMed 15467559](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15467559/)
- **Meta-analysis after colorectal surgery** (Support Care Cancer 2023/24, RCTs only): biofeedback beat usual care on both anal resting pressure and maximum squeeze pressure. The MSP gain was reported as about 25 mmHg (95% CI 4.08–48.50 mmHg). This is a between-group comparison, not pre/post — [Springer s00520-023-08294-1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00520-023-08294-1)
- **Network meta-analysis of FI treatments**: none of the 12 treatments compared was superior for resting pressure — [ScienceDirect, Int J Surg 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1743919119300901)
- **Home biofeedback pilot (FI, uncontrolled, 2026)**: median maximum squeeze pressure rose from 20 (0–80) to 60 (17–96) mmHg over 3 months (p<0.001) — [Tech Coloproctol 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10151-026-03384-8)
- **Radiotherapy-related FI pilot (n=7)**: median squeeze gain of 15 mmHg, not statistically significant — [PMC7707912](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7707912/)
- **Translumbosacral magnetic neuromodulation (FI, RCT n=33)**: anal squeeze pressure improved significantly only in the 1 Hz arm — [PubMed 32740081](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32740081/)
- **Men, ED (Dorey et al. 2004, Br J Gen Pract 54:819)** — RCT, n=55. PFMT plus manometric biofeedback plus lifestyle advice (n=28) vs lifestyle advice alone (n=27). At 3 months the intervention group showed a significant mean anal pressure increase of **44.16 cmH₂O vs control (P<0.001)**, plus better IIEF erectile domain and digital anal grades, with further gains at 6 months. Controls improved similarly after crossing over. Whole cohort: 40.0% regained normal erectile function, 34.5% improved, 25.5% did not improve. PEDro 6/10 — [PubMed 15527607](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15527607/); [BJGP](https://bjgp.org/content/54/508/819); [PEDro](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/10955)
- **Healthy young men, EMG (2026 pilot, uncontrolled, n=16, age 23±4, strength-trained)**: 6 weeks of PFMT, three short sessions per week. Mean EMG over five 10-s contractions rose from 7.8 µV to 13.32 µV (p=0.001; r=0.82). This is neuromuscular activity, not pressure or true strength — [PMC13106149](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13106149/)
- **Healthy young women, resistance device (Am J Physiol GI Liver Physiol, n=12 nulliparous, age ~21)**: 40 repetitive 3-s squeezes and 30-s long squeezes against an intra-anal compressible load (cRED device) caused significantly more fatigue in anal contractile integral and maximum squeeze pressure than unloaded squeezes. The authors infer "potential to strengthen" the sphincter, which is a physiological rationale, not a training outcome — [AJP-GI](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/ajpgi.00456.2020). A follow-up (Patel et al. 2021, Physiol Rep) compared fatigability of the external anal sphincter and puborectalis in healthy women — [PMC8685596](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8685596/)
- **Normative data, healthy adults (Anopress portable manometry, n=153 asymptomatic)**: squeeze endurance ranged 1.3–9.0 s in women and **2.0–10.0 s in men** — [J Neurogastroenterol Motil](https://www.jnmjournal.org/journal/view.html?doi=10.5056%2Fjnm17135)
- Standard HRAM endurance test in healthy volunteers: a single sustained 30-s squeeze, then 60-s recovery (n=115 healthy volunteers) — [PLOS One 2020](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0228761)

### Inferences
- 44.16 cmH₂O ≈ 32.5 mmHg (1 cmH₂O = 0.7356 mmHg). This is the order of squeeze gain a supervised, feedback-assisted program achieved in middle-aged men with ED (median age ~59). It cannot be attributed to biofeedback over exercise, because the control arm got no exercise.
- Healthy-male normative endurance tops out at about 10 s, so the app's 10-s maximal hold matches physiology. "50% long holds" (submaximal, longer) fit the HRAM 30-s endurance paradigm.
- Loaded training (cRED) is the only device-based approach with a strength-training rationale. It needs intra-anal hardware, so a no-hardware app cannot use it.

### Gaps
- No RCT or controlled pre/post study measured anal manometry before and after a sphincter training program in healthy men. The effect size of exercise alone on squeeze pressure in healthy users is unknown.
- No pooled within-patient mmHg change for biofeedback in FI was found.
- I could not retrieve the 2026 phase-3 translumbosacral neuromodulation trial's manometry results.

## 3. Electrical stimulation (intra-anal, transcutaneous, PTNS/TTNS) and magnetic stimulation (Emsella/HIFEM chairs, translumbosacral) — evidence and added benefit over PFMT

### Takeaway
Tibial nerve stimulation does not beat sham convincingly (CONFIDeNT: 38% vs 31% responders). Transcutaneous TENS for FI was null vs sham. Intra-anal ES adds nothing in people who can contract (Mahony 2004; in men, Goode 2011). HIFEM chairs have **no published RCTs in FI and none in healthy or incontinent men**. The newest positive signal is clinic-based translumbosacral magnetic neuromodulation for neuropathic FI, which is unrelated to consumer devices. None of these is shown to strengthen a healthy sphincter beyond voluntary training.

### Cited Findings
- **CONFIDeNT (Knowles et al., Lancet 2015;386:1640, DOI 10.1016/S0140-6736(15)60314-2)** — double-blind sham-controlled RCT, n=227 (PTNS 115, sham 112). ≥50% reduction in weekly FI episodes: 38% PTNS vs 31% sham, not significant. St Mark's score, FIQL and generic QoL were similar between arms. Conclusion: PTNS is not recommended first-line for unselected adults with FI. A later analysis found that absence of evacuatory difficulty predicted response — [NEJM JW summary](https://clinician.nejm.org/nejm-jw.NA38827); [Sussex repository](https://sro.sussex.ac.uk/id/eprint/57165/); [ISRCTN88559475](https://www.isrctn.com/pdf/ISRCTN88559475); [ESCP commentary](https://escp.eu.com/news/focus-on/faecal-incontinence/1818-where-are-we-with-percutaneous-tibial-nerve-stimulation)
- **Maastricht PTNS RCT**: ≥50% reduction in FI episodes in 13/29 PTNS vs 6/30 sham, but the mean-based comparison was not significant — [Maastricht CRIS](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/randomized-clinical-trial-of-percutaneous-tibial-nerve-stimulatio/)
- **Transcutaneous TENS for FI** — double-blind sham RCT, n=144, 9 centres, 3 months: no significant difference in median weekly FI or urgency episodes — [PubMed 23032981](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23032981). An editorial judges the evidence for both percutaneous and transcutaneous tibial stimulation weak compared with sacral nerve stimulation — [WJG editorial](https://www.wjgnet.com/1007-9327/full/v19/i48/9139.htm)
- **Intra-anal ES**: see Q1 (Mahony 2004 null; Fynes 1999 positive but confounded; Vonthein 2013). Cochrane CD001310: "it is not clear that the symptom improvement is due to electrical stimulation" — [Cochrane CD001310](https://www.cochrane.org/zh-hans/evidence/CD001310_electrical-stimulation-faecal-incontinence-adults); [PMC8406929](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8406929/)
- **Men, PPI — Goode et al. 2011, JAMA (MOTIVE)** — RCT, n=208 men 1–17 years after radical prostatectomy: behavioural therapy (PFMT plus bladder control) n=70; behavioural therapy plus biofeedback plus pelvic floor ES n=70; delayed-treatment control n=68. At 8 weeks, incontinence episodes fell by a mean of 55% with behavioural therapy vs 24% in controls. Adding biofeedback plus ES gave **no additional improvement**. Gains held to 12 months. No blinding — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT00212264](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00212264); [UroToday](https://urotoday.com/recent-abstracts/urologic-oncology/prostate-cancer/42877-editors-commentary-behavioral-therapy-with-or-without-biofeedback-and-pelvic-floor-electrical-stimulation-for-persistent-postprostatectomy-incontinence-a-randomized-controlled-trial.html); [MDedge](https://mdedge.com/content/behavioral-therapy-halves-persistent-postprostatectomy-incontinence); [PEDro 28159](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/28159)
- **Cochrane PPI reviews**: Anderson 2015 (CD001843.pub5) covered PFMT ± biofeedback, ES, extracorporeal magnetic innervation (ExMI), clamps and lifestyle. Evidence was weak and conflicting, "the value of the different approaches is still uncertain" — [Cochrane Library CD001843.pub5](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD001843.pub5/information); [cochrane.org](https://www.cochrane.org/CD001843). Johnson 2023 (CD014799) superseded it, comparing PFMT + biofeedback and electrical/magnetic stimulation vs no or sham treatment (GRADE; search to April 2022) — [Cochrane CD014799](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD014799.pub2/information); [PubMed 37070660](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37070660/)
- **HIFEM / Emsella chair**: no published RCT results in FI and no male efficacy data. Registered work includes a single-blind sham-controlled pilot of Emsella plus diet counselling in FI and a trial in Chinese men after robotic prostatectomy (HIFEM-assisted PFMT, 6 sessions) — [NCT04138602](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04138602); [NCT04873037](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04873037); [NCT06589869](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06589869). An ileal-pouch study's consent document states that HIFEM for FI "has not yet been studied" — [NCT07034443 ICF](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/43/NCT07034443/ICF_001.pdf). In women with urinary incontinence, a systematic review of chair-based magnetic stimulation reports improvement in continence and sexual function, with mild, uncommon adverse events and hints that combining it with PFMT may help more — [PMC12693354](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12693354/). A postpartum pilot of HIFEM plus PFMT was feasible but not powered for efficacy — [ScienceDirect 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1049386725001604)
- **Translumbosacral neuromodulation therapy (TNT; clinic magnetic stimulation over lumbosacral roots)**:
  - Frequency RCT (Rao et al., Am J Gastroenterol 2021, n=33): responder rate about 91% at 1 Hz vs 36% at 5 Hz and 55% at 15 Hz. Squeeze pressure, anal neuropathy and rectal capacity improved only at 1 Hz. No device-related serious adverse events — [PubMed 32740081](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32740081/)
  - Phase-3 sham-controlled RCT (Gastroenterology 2026, n=109, 2 US centres, 2019–2024): weekly sessions for 6 weeks of 2,400 or 3,600 pulses. Responders 66–81% active vs 32% sham. Caveat: the sham arm had lower baseline episode frequency, and durability is still under study — [AGA news May 2026](https://news.gastro.org/issues/2026/may-2026/magnetic-neuromodulation-reduced-fecal-incontinence/); [AGA news Aug 2026](https://news.gastro.org/issues/2026/august-2026/noninvasive-neuromodulation-reduces-fecal-incontinence-in-phase-3-trial/)

### Inferences
- Stimulation acts mainly as neuromodulation or passive contraction for people with neuropathy or who cannot contract. In people who can voluntarily contract, the two best trials of adding ES on top of exercise (Mahony 2004; Goode 2011, in men) show no added benefit.
- HIFEM chairs are marketed to men and for "toning", but no RCT evidence supports them for FI or for healthy men. The app should not recommend them as superior to exercise.
- None of these modalities can be delivered by a phone without hardware. TTNS and TENS units exist for consumers, but the TENS-for-FI sham trial was null.

### Gaps
- The Johnson 2023 Cochrane certainty ratings and pooled estimates for ES and magnetic stimulation in men were not retrieved.
- NICE IPG395 (PTNS for FI) wording was not retrieved.
- Schwandner 2011 (the amplitude-modulated medium-frequency "3T" trial) primary numbers were not retrieved, beyond Vonthein's summary.
- I found no data on HIFEM's effect on anal squeeze pressure.

## 4. Rectal balloon sensory retraining, urge-resistance and coordination (expulsion) training — who benefits

### Takeaway
Balloon-based sensory and urge-resistance training targets **rectal sensation and urgency**, not sphincter strength. Added to PFMT it did not improve overall FI severity (Bols 2012), but it improved urgency control. Balloon expulsion and coordination training is for dyssynergic defecation (constipation). None of these is relevant to a healthy man without urgency or evacuation problems, and none can be done without a rectal balloon.

### Cited Findings
- **Bols 2012 RCT (n=80, FI)**: PFMT plus RBT vs PFMT alone gave a Vaizey MD of −1.19 (−3.79 to 1.42; P=0.37). RBT improved urgency control, global perceived effect and lifestyle — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/nau.21218); [ICS poster](https://www.ics.org/Abstracts/Publish/106/000211_poster.pdf). A secondary analysis of the same trial looked at predictors of a favourable outcome — [Neurourol Urodyn 2012](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/nau.21236)
- **Chiarioni et al. 2002, Am J Gastroenterol 97:109–117**, "Sensory retraining is key to biofeedback therapy for formed stool fecal incontinence." The title states the finding (citation confirmed). Abstract numbers were not retrieved — [cited in Thieme review](https://thieme-connect.com/products/ejournals/html/10.1055/s-2003-45434)
- Norton's 2004 review describes rectal balloon training as retraining the rectal sensory threshold. It also states that biofeedback is advocated first-line for mild–moderate FI after failure of simple dietary advice or medication — [Gastroenterology 2004](https://www.gastrojournal.org/article/S0016-5085(03)01400-8/fulltext)
- Older component analysis: exercise and sensory-discrimination training are each effective for some FI cases. Sensory thresholds fell after biofeedback (with improvement sustained at 1 year, n=105), and pre-treatment sensory thresholds predicted response — [ANMS-ESNM position paper PMC4409469 and cited studies](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4409469); [Springer BF01536422](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/BF01536422)
- ANMS-ESNM: sensory training aims to enhance rectal perception in patients with **impaired rectal sensation**. Coordination and expulsion training is part of biofeedback for dyssynergic defecation — [PMC4409469](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4409469); [Rao, biofeedback for dyssynergic defecation, PMC4087765](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4087765/); [long-term dyssynergia RCT PMC3910270](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3910270/)
- Home-based biofeedback was non-inferior to office biofeedback for dyssynergic defecation (Rao et al., Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol 2018). A home vs office FI trial (NCT03216291; target n=33, completed 2018) has no retrieved results — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03216291](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03216291); [Physician's Weekly abstract summary](https://www.physiciansweekly.com/?p=326865)

### Inferences
- Who benefits: people with FI who have urgency or a sensory deficit (hyposensitivity or hypersensitivity) and people with dyssynergic defecation. A healthy athletic man has no sensory deficit to retrain.
- The relaxation and "reverse-Kegel" component already in Тонус is the app-deliverable part of coordination training. It is reasonable for preventing overactive or non-relaxing pelvic floor patterns. That is an inference; no trial in healthy men was found.

### Gaps
- No exact effect sizes from Chiarioni 2002.
- No trials of sensory or urge training in healthy people.

## 5. Home biofeedback devices, pressure-sensor trainers and smartphone apps — RCTs, male devices, adherence

### Takeaway
In women, RCTs show that **feedback-giving home devices** (pressure- or motion-sensing) beat home PFMT taught by written or verbal instructions (Wang 2024, n=452; leva, n=363). The plain app Tät gave durable, clinically relevant gains vs control. In men, a small 2026 RCT found an app-based PFMT program beat physio-guided PFMT after prostatectomy (PELVINTENSE). There are **no RCTs of male home biofeedback trainers**. Male consumer devices (e.g., the kGoal Boost sit-on cushion) have only marketing or validation data.

### Cited Findings
**Women, device-based home biofeedback**
- **Wang et al. 2024, JAMA Netw Open (doi 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2024.42925)** — multicentre assessor-blinded RCT, 5 Chinese centres, postpartum SUI, n=452 analysed (223 vs 229). All women got 3 months of supervised PFMT, randomised to add a home pressure-mediated biofeedback device or do home PFMT. The device arm had better outcomes at 3 months (primary: ICIQ-UI SF; secondary: PFM strength, adherence, self-efficacy) — [PMC11539013](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11539013/); [PubMed 39499517](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39499517/); [Contemporary OB/GYN](https://www.contemporaryobgyn.net/view/biofeedback-indicated-more-effective-for-sui-treatment-than-pfmt-alone)
- **leva motion-based digital therapeutic (Weinstein et al., Obstet Gynecol)** — virtual RCT, n=363, SUI or stress-predominant MUI, 8 weeks of leva vs standard home PFMT from written and verbal instructions. Greater UDI-6 improvement at weeks 4 and 8 and a greater fall in SUI episodes. A 2023 conference abstract reports the advantage persisted at 18 and 24 months, but that source is promotional. An earlier, smaller trial (29 vs 32) was stopped early with no primary-outcome difference — [MGH Advances](https://advances.massgeneral.org/urology/journal.aspx?id=2223); [MGH Advances, earlier trial](https://advances.massgeneral.org/obgyn/journal.aspx?id=1938); [Business Wire, company press release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230622175694/en/Axena-Health-celebrates-%E2%80%9CBest-Overall%E2%80%9D-abstract-showing-the-Leva-System%E2%80%99s-long-term-efficacy-for-female-urinary-incontinence)
- **Home digital biofeedback system for FI (Urogynecology 2026)** — single-blind randomised equivalence trial, n=38 women (36 completed), twice-daily exercises for 8 vs 16 weeks, optional remote coaching. Mean adherence 75.2%. Vaizey change −4.7±5.1 (8 weeks) vs −4.8±4.5 (16 weeks), P=0.918, both above the MCID. Brink strength and endurance improved. No serious adverse events. No exercise-only control arm — [PMC13089846](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13089846/); [LWW](https://journals.lww.com/fpmrs/fulltext/2026/04000/home_based_biofeedback_for_fecal_incontinence__a.20.aspx); [PubMed 41854471](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41854471/)

**Apps without hardware**
- **Tät (Sweden; Asklund/Sjöström RCT, n=123 women with SUI)** — 3 months of app-supported self-management was effective vs control. In the 2-year follow-up of the app arm only (Hoffman et al. 2017, Acta Obstet Gynecol Scand), 46/61 (75.4%) responded. ICIQ-UI SF fell by a mean 3.1 (95% CI 2.0–4.2) and ICIQ-LUTSqol by 4.0 (2.1–5.9). Protection-product use fell. Women who felt they contracted correctly rose from **30.4% to 67.4%**. This is an uncontrolled follow-up — [PubMed 28718223](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28718223/); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/318497305_Self-management_of_stress_urinary_incontinence_via_a_mobile_app_2-year_follow-up_of_a_randomized_controlled_trial); [Tät publications list](https://xn--tt-via.nu/language/en/publications/)
- Tät II (urgency or mixed UI): significant effects on all outcomes at 1 year — [Wadensten 2022, Neurourol Urodyn](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/nau.24898). Free real-world app use showed cost-effective PFMT delivery — [Int Urogynecol J 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00192-021-04755-5). In qualitative interviews, users described reminders and structure as key — [JMIR 2019](https://www.jmir.org/2019/3/e11296/)
- **Men — PELVINTENSE (Cancers 2026, doi 10.3390/cancers18091333)** — multicentre RCT, n=62 continent men having radical prostatectomy. Modular app-based PFMT vs standard physiotherapist-guided PFMT, from 3 weeks pre-op to 90 days post-op. Continence (ICIQ-SF Q1=0) at 30 days: 45.2% vs 3.6%. At 90 days: **74.2% vs 21.4%** (p<0.001). ARR 52.8%, NNT 2, adjusted OR 13.80 (95% CI 3.22–59.12). Small trial, very wide CI, unusually low control continence — [PMC13163010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13163010/); [PubMed 42122130](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42122130/); [UroToday abstract](https://www.urotoday.com/recent-abstracts/pelvic-health-reconstruction/stress-incontinence/169018-efficacy-of-a-modular-app-based-pelvic-floor-muscle-training-program-for-postoperative-continence-recovery-after-radical-prostatectomy-a-multi-center-randomized-controlled-trial-pelvintense-study.html)
- **Men — Swedish prospective cohort (not randomised)**: app-supported PFMT started before radical prostatectomy. Men's confidence that they contracted correctly rose from about 40% at baseline to about 74% at 1 month — [PMC9766858](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9766858/)
- **Men — other**: a J Urol 2022 three-site RCT compared a mobile perioperative behavioural program (including PFMT) with education control; results were not retrieved — [UroToday](https://www.urotoday.com/recent-abstracts/pelvic-health-reconstruction/post-prostatectomy-incontinence/136445-perioperative-mobile-telehealth-program-for-post-prostatectomy-incontinence-a-randomized-clinical-trial.html). A WeChat plus PFMT study after radical prostatectomy exists; results not retrieved — [PMC7109580](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7109580/)
- **Reviews**:
  - 2026 meta-analysis of app RCTs in women with UI (PROSPERO CRD420261472595): apps "should complement rather than replace supervised PFMT", especially for patients who need individualised rehabilitation or objective functional assessment. It notes adherence is low — [PMC13531428](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13531428/); [Frontiers Public Health 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1859960/full)
  - 2022 systematic review: app PFMT improved symptoms and adherence, but adherence slipped between 3 and 6 months — [BMC Womens Health 2022](https://bmcwomenshealth.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12905-022-01985-7)
  - 2022 review of RCTs (narrative; too heterogeneous to pool) — [BMC Nursing 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12912-022-00812-6)
  - Persuasive-design review (10 studies; meta-analysis not feasible) — [JMIR mHealth](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S2291522222001036)
- **Male consumer devices**:
  - kGoal Boost: sit-on cushion with pressure sensors that reads pelvic floor activity through clothing, with an app offering games and biofeedback. No RCT found — [FemTech Insider](https://femtechinsider.com/pelvic-health-company-kgoal-adds-a-kegel-trainer-for-men-to-its-product-line-up/)
  - "Kegel Nation" (UCSF): an app's timing of contractions and relaxations was validated in 10 subjects. This is measurement validation, not outcomes — [Urology Times](https://www.urologytimes.com/view/pelvic-floor-muscle-training-app-provides-biofeedback)
  - Men's apps such as "Kegel Pro" (phone-only) cite studies without showing outcomes — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6473720892)

### Inferences
- The device advantage in the women's RCTs (Wang 2024, leva) is over **instruction-only home PFMT**. Likely mechanisms are confirming correct contraction, real-time feedback and adherence or gamification. A no-hardware app can partly reproduce adherence and structure (as Tät and PELVINTENSE did) but cannot measure contraction.
- The improvement in perceived correct contraction (Tät 30→67%; Swedish men's app 40→74%) suggests a well-designed app improves self-efficacy. Self-report is not verified correctness.
- Perifit and Elvie are vaginal devices and cannot be used by men. An anal-probe consumer biofeedback device for men with RCT evidence was not found.

### Gaps
- Wang 2024's primary effect size and CI were not retrieved.
- No RCT data for Perifit or Elvie specifically were retrieved.
- No adherence figures from male app trials were retrieved.
- No RCT of any app or device in healthy men was found.

## 6. Self-feedback techniques without devices (palpation, mirror, cues, visualisation/3D animation, motor imagery) — does teaching quality matter?

### Takeaway
Yes. Contraction quality after brief instruction is unreliable: only 49% of women performed an "ideal" Kegel and 25% used a potentially harmful, Valsalva-type technique (Bump 1991). In men, cue wording strongly changes whether the pelvic floor elevates. "Squeeze your anus / shorten the penis / elevate the scrotum" work in more than 91%; "draw in" works in only 25%. Evidence for motor imagery or animation is preliminary (mostly registered trials). These cues and self-checks are cheap, and they are the most evidence-aligned thing a no-hardware app can add.

### Cited Findings
- **Bump et al. 1991, Am J Obstet Gynecol 165(2):322–329** — 47 women tested with urethral pressure profiles after brief standardised verbal instruction. 23 (49%) made an "ideal" effort (increase in urethral closure without appreciable Valsalva). 12 (25%) used a technique that could promote incontinence. Age, parity, weight and prior surgery did not predict success. Authors: simple verbal or written instruction "does not represent adequate preparation" — [UMN Experts](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/assessment-of-kegel-pelvic-muscle-exercise-performance-after-brie/); [Herman & Wallace summary](https://hermanwallace.com/blog/pelvic-muscle-contraction-after-verbal-instruction). Caveats: small sample and an arbitrary definition of an effective Kegel. Some clinics quote a 60/40 split instead — [feminapt](https://feminapt.com/blog/what-are-kegels/)
- **Healthy young men (IJERPH 2022;19:12031; n=35 male physiotherapy students; transabdominal ultrasound of bladder-base displacement)**: "squeeze your anus", "shorten the penis" and "elevate the scrotum" produced cranial bladder-base displacement in >91%. "Draw in" did so in only 25% and gave the lowest endurance. Authors advise avoiding "draw in" and the generic "squeeze your PFM" — [PMC9566287](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9566287/); [PubMed 36231333](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36231333/)
- **Stafford et al. 2016, Neurourol Urodyn (n=15 healthy men; transperineal ultrasound plus EMG)**: "shorten the penis" gave the greatest dorsal displacement of the mid-urethra and the most striated urethral sphincter activity, compared with other cues. Activation patterns differ by instruction — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/nau.22745); [Herman & Wallace](https://hermanwallace.com/blog/verbal-instructions-and-pelvic-floor-contractions-in-men). Transperineal ultrasound validity for estimating PFM activity in men — [PLOS One 2015](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0144342)
- **Ben Ami & Dar 2018, Neurourol Urodyn (n=56 physiotherapy students, mostly women)**: 90% contracted correctly with the "anus" instruction — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/nau.23810)
- **Motor imagery**:
  - Small Cairo study in incomplete spinal cord injury: greater PFM strength and ICIQ-SF reduction vs control — [MJCU 2020](https://dx.doi.org/10.21608/mjcu.2020.104900)
  - RCTs only registered (no results): NCT07853755 (pelvic-floor kinesthetic imagery vs fist imagery, 60 women with SUI); NCT06323941 (imagery plus isometrics in healthy women) — [NCT07853755](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07853755); [NCT06323941](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06323941)
  - Conference RCT, imagery of PFM contraction vs sit-to-stand imagery (n=30), outcomes not retrieved — [World Physio congress](https://world.physio/congress-proceeding/motor-imagery-learning-pelvic-floor-muscle-contraction-women-stress-urinary)
- **Video/visual teaching**: a planned 4-arm RCT in healthy women compares brochure, verbal (imagery metaphors such as hammock, elevator, faucet), visual (models, videos) and palpation-aided teaching. No results yet — [NCT07099170 listing](https://tundraspace.com/directory/studies/nct07099170)
- **Digital (finger) guidance as a feedback method**: Solomon 2003 used digitally guided retraining as an active comparator to manometric and ultrasound biofeedback — [abstract](https://www.qigonginstitute.org/abstract-print/3728)
- **NICE NG210** links add-on biofeedback or ES specifically to failure to make an effective contraction, which implies that checking contraction quality is the decision point — [NG210](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng210/chapter/rationale-and-impact)

### Inferences
- App implications: open with validated male cues ("squeeze the anus as if holding wind", "shorten/retract the penis", "lift the scrotum/testicles"). Explicitly warn against breath-holding, bearing down, buttock or adductor squeezing and abdominal "draw in". Add a self-check step: watch in a mirror for penile retraction and scrotal lift; palpate the perineum behind the scrotum for an inward lift rather than outward bulge; palpate the external anus for a closing "wink"; keep the buttocks relaxed. These mirror what clinicians check digitally. Direct trials of self-palpation or mirror use in men were not found, so the app should present these as a reasonable, low-risk practice, not a proven one.
- 3D animation and motor imagery are plausible aids for learning, but the evidence is preliminary. The app's 3D visualisation is defensible as education, not as a proven efficacy booster.

### Gaps
- No RCT tests self-palpation or mirror feedback vs verbal instruction alone in men.
- No published RCT tests 3D animation or video for learning the PFM contraction.
- No published data give the proportion of healthy men who contract incorrectly after verbal instruction, as measured by transperineal ultrasound; the 2022 study used a transabdominal approach.

## 7. Is anything clearly better than exercise alone for a healthy man, or are adjuncts mainly for patients who cannot contract correctly?

### Takeaway
I found **no evidence that any adjunct (biofeedback, ES, PTNS/TTNS, HIFEM, rectal balloon) is clearly superior to well-taught, adherent exercise for a person who can already contract correctly**, and no adjunct trials in healthy men at all. Guidelines position adjuncts for patients who **cannot make an effective contraction**, fail supervised PFMT, or have specific deficits (sensory loss, neuropathy, dyssynergia). For an app, the evidence-supported levers are correct-contraction teaching, structured progressive dosing, and adherence support (reminders, tracking, feedback-like engagement).

### Cited Findings
- Norton 2003: biofeedback and exercises were no better than standard care with advice — [Gastroenterology](https://www.gastrojournal.org/article/S0016-5085(03)01368-4/fulltext)
- Heymen 2009: biofeedback beat exercises alone (76% vs 41% adequate relief at 3 months), but only after excluding the 21% who responded to education alone. The relief difference was not significant at 12 months (P=0.056) — [PubMed 19966605](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19966605/)
- Cochrane CD002111: evidence too limited for definitive conclusions — [PubMed 22786479](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22786479/)
- Herderschee 2011 (women UI): the apparent biofeedback benefit may be confounded by more therapist contact — [UroToday](https://www.urotoday.com/recent-abstracts/pelvic-health-reconstruction/stress-incontinence/60214-feedback-or-biofeedback-to-augment-pelvic-floor-muscle-training-for-urinary-incontinence-in-women-shortened-version-of-a-cochrane-systematic-review-abstract.html)
- Men: biofeedback plus ES added nothing to behavioural PFMT (Goode 2011, n=208) — [NCT00212264](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00212264); [MDedge](https://mdedge.com/content/behavioral-therapy-halves-persistent-postprostatectomy-incontinence)
- ES added to biofeedback added nothing (Mahony 2004) — [PubMed 15467559](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15467559/)
- Rectal balloon training added to PFMT did not improve overall severity, only urgency (Bols 2012) — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/nau.21218)
- PTNS ≈ sham (CONFIDeNT) — [NEJM JW](https://clinician.nejm.org/nejm-jw.NA38827). TENS = sham — [PubMed 23032981](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23032981)
- HIFEM: no FI or male RCTs — [NCT07034443 ICF](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/43/NCT07034443/ICF_001.pdf); [PMC12693354](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12693354/)
- NICE NG210: add-ons are for those who cannot contract effectively or make little progress — [NG210](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng210/chapter/rationale-and-impact). ASCRS 2023: biofeedback is a conditional recommendation on low-quality evidence — [Guideline Central](https://www.guidelinecentral.com/guideline/9290/)
- Where device-assisted home training did beat plain PFMT (Wang 2024, leva), the comparator was instruction-only home PFMT in symptomatic women — [PMC11539013](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11539013/); [MGH Advances](https://advances.massgeneral.org/urology/journal.aspx?id=2223)
- App-delivered structure can match or beat human-guided PFMT in men (PELVINTENSE, small RCT) — [PMC13163010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13163010/)
- Training dose: a review notes the best results come from training for at least 6–12 weeks — [PMC8743604](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8743604/)

### Inferences
- For a healthy man who can contract correctly, nothing is demonstrably better than exercise. The ranking of likely added value for Тонус is: (1) contraction-quality teaching and self-checks; (2) adherence features (reminders, streaks, logs; Tät and PELVINTENSE are the evidence base); (3) optional, clearly labelled hardware such as a consumer pressure cushion or a pelvic-health physiotherapist visit with manometric or ultrasound biofeedback, only for users who are unsure they contract correctly. Electrical stimulation, HIFEM chairs, PTNS/TTNS and balloon training should not be presented as upgrades.
- A "red-flag / see a professional" path is warranted for FI, urgency, evacuation difficulty, pain or post-surgical status. Those are the populations in which adjuncts have a role.
- Progressive overload in strength training normally needs resistance. With no hardware, an app can only progress by hold duration, repetitions, posture (sitting → standing → functional or dynamic) and contraction intensity. That matches Тонус's design. Whether this raises squeeze pressure in already-strong healthy men is untested.

### Gaps
- No trial, of any adjunct or of exercise alone, used healthy men as participants with anal manometry outcomes.
- No study compared a no-hardware app plus self-palpation vs app alone.
- The long-term (>1 year) maintenance dose for a healthy user is unknown.
